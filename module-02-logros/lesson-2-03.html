★ Los participios irregulares — una lista cerrada
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Eight words. Learn them once and the irregular participle problem is over.
Last module you built the present perfect and used only regular participles — -ado and -ido. That was deliberate: a rule is easier to own when it is not competing with a list of exceptions in the same week.

Today you get the exceptions. There are about eight that matter, and they are a closed list — no new ones will appear later in the course. Learn these and you have the entire system.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to produce the eight core irregular participles from their infinitives.
2
I will be able to predict a compound verb's participle from its base — descubrir → descubierto.
3
I will be able to use these participles in the present perfect without splitting the pair.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Why these eight and not others

These are the oldest, most-used verbs in the language — hacer, decir, ver, poner. High-frequency words resist regularisation: they are said so often that the old forms survive instead of being smoothed out. English does exactly the same thing (go/went, see/saw) and for the same reason.

So the irregularity is a sign of usefulness. These eight are worth the ten minutes precisely because you will use them constantly.

Three more you will meet often enough to learn now: romper → roto, cubrir → cubierto, resolver → resuelto. That is eleven in total, and the list is finished — Spanish has no further common irregular participles waiting for you.

⭐ PATRONES · they are not random
Look again and you will see families rather than chaos:
• -cho: hecho, dicho
• -to after a consonant: visto, escrito, puesto, muerto, roto, abierto, cubierto
• -elto: vuelto, resuelto

Almost all of them end in -to or -cho. If a participle you are unsure about ends in -ado or -ido, it is regular; if it ends in -to or -cho, it is on this list.
🧠 PART 3 · LOS COMPUESTOS 9 min

Eight participles, but far more than eight verbs

Here is the payoff. Any verb built on one of these eight inherits its participle. So learning eight gives you dozens:

BaseCompoundParticipio
hacer → hechodeshacer, rehacer, satisfacerdeshecho, rehecho, satisfecho
poner → puestocomponer, proponer, suponercompuesto, propuesto, supuesto
volver → vueltodevolver, envolver, revolverdevuelto, envuelto, revuelto

So when you meet proponer for the first time, you do not need to look up its participle — you already know it is propuesto. This is the single highest-value thing in today's lesson: eight forms, memorised once, cover a large open-ended set of verbs. Note the one small spelling wrinkle: satisfacer gives satisfecho, with f rather than h.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · the commonest one in the language
«¿Qué ___ esta semana para prepararte?» (hacer, tú)
✅ TRY IT · predict the compound
If poner gives puesto, what does proponer give?
✅ TRY IT · the pair stays together
«Todavía ___ los resultados del examen.» (ver, nosotros — negative)
✅ TRY IT · no agreement in the perfect
«Las profesoras han ___ las cartas de recomendación.» (escribir)

Ahora tú: say four things about this school year using four different irregular participles. Say them aloud before revealing.

Este año he hecho mucho más trabajo del que esperaba. He escrito tres ensayos en español. Mis profesores me han dicho que he mejorado bastante. Y he vuelto a intentar el examen oral, aunque la primera vez no salió bien.

Check yours: participles fixed in -o with no agreement · no and pronouns in front of haber · and four different irregulars, not hecho four times.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Correo al comité de becas

Buenos días. Escribo para confirmar que he puesto toda la documentación en el sistema. He escrito la carta de motivación, he descubierto que faltaba una firma y ya la he conseguido. También he devuelto el formulario B que ustedes me mandaron.

Sé que han abierto las inscripciones esta semana y que muchos estudiantes han propuesto proyectos parecidos al mío. Todavía no he visto la lista de requisitos actualizada. ¿Me la pueden enviar?

Mis profesores me han dicho que el proceso es competitivo, pero no he perdido la esperanza. He vuelto a revisar cada documento tres veces.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Count the irregular participles: there are nine, including two compounds (descubierto, propuesto) that you could have predicted without looking them up. Find the two regular ones hiding among them (conseguido, perdido) and note that nothing marks them out — regular and irregular participles behave identically once formed. Only the forming differs.
🌎 CULTURA · COSTA RICA
La beca como camino

The email in Part 5 is a realistic document in Costa Rica, where public university is heavily subsidised and a state scholarship system (becas) is a normal route for students from rural areas. The Universidad de Costa Rica, founded in 1940, is public and highly regarded across Central America.

That matters for how the vocabulary lands. In much of Latin America «he conseguido una beca» is not a modest achievement mentioned in passing — it can be the sentence that changes a family's trajectory. When you write it in this module, write it as something that carries weight.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PERSEVERANCIA

The writer of that email checked every document three times. Not dramatic, not admirable-looking, and exactly what gets a scholarship application accepted. La perseverancia mostly consists of unremarkable repetition that nobody witnesses. So does memorising eight participles. Tres veces revisado no es desconfianza: es cuidado.
