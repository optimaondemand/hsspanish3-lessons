➡️ PART 5 · LO QUE VIENE 8 min

The course changes direction now

Modules 0 to 2 were all about the past — narrating it, ordering it, reporting what has happened. That work is finished. You now have four past forms and can put any two events in order.

From Module 3 the course turns toward influencing other people: telling them what to do, advising them, and eventually expressing what you want, doubt and hope. That is the second half of the semester's title — Opiniones y consejos.

Module 3 starts with formal commands — tome, visite, no lleve — the forms a doctor used on you back in 1.02, and the ones a travel guide uses on a reader. Theme: la ciudad y las direcciones. Culture: Colombia — Bogotá and Cartagena. Virtue: la cortesía.

💡 ALGO QUE VALE LA PENA SABER AHORA
The command forms you learn in Module 3 are the same forms you will use for the subjunctive in Module 7. Identical spellings, different job.

So Module 3 is not a detour before the hard part — it is the hard part, arriving early and disguised as something practical. Learn tome, visite and no lleve properly now and Module 7 will ask you only to learn when to use forms you already produce automatically.

Students who treat Module 3 as easy vocabulary about street directions find Module 7 very hard. You have been told.

🌎 CULTURA · COSTA RICA
Lo que te llevas de este módulo

Three things about Costa Rica worth keeping. The 1948 abolition, done in the barracks that is now the national museum — and the honest footnote that it followed a civil war and does not mean the country has no armed police. The investment that replaced it: free public education and a health system reaching small rural towns, which is why a student from a farming village can plausibly end up on a sea-turtle project. And con mucho gusto where other countries say de nada — a small, distinctively tico courtesy.

And one register warning to carry forward: «pura vida» is genuinely everywhere, and genuinely informal. It is not what you say in an interview.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PERSEVERANCIA

This module gave you the grammar of perseverance without saying so: the past perfect is how you report what you had already tried before something finally worked. Mariela's three applications, the interviewee's forgotten notes, a country holding a decision for seventy-five years — all of them need había to be told properly. Next module the virtue becomes la cortesía, which is perseverance pointed outward: doing the considerate thing repeatedly, including when nobody notices. Sin pluscuamperfecto, todo logro parece suerte.
