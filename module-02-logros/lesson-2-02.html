★ La trayectoria — contar un camino, no un momento
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
An achievement is a point. A trayectoria is a line.
Yesterday you named achievements. Today you learn to describe the path that led to one — the openings taken, the setbacks, the point where giving up was an option and you did not.

Ten more words, and a four-part shape for telling that kind of story. No new grammar; tomorrow the machinery arrives.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Why the setback is the interesting part

A story where everything went well is not a story. Notice what yesterday's scholarship letter chose to include: la primera vez fracasé. The writer volunteered a failure — because the failure is what makes the persistence visible.

Today's vocabulary is built around that: opportunities, setbacks, and the decision not to quit.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Two phrases with structure worth noting. Valer la pena behaves like gustar — the thing is the subject: vale la pena (singular), valen la pena (plural), ha valido la pena in the perfect. And darse por vencido is reflexive and agrees: a girl says me he dado por vencida. Two people say nos hemos dado por vencidos. That agreement is unusual — it happens because vencido here is an adjective after por, not part of the perfect.
🧠 PART 3 · LA FORMA DE UNA TRAYECTORIA 9 min

Four parts, and the tense each one wants

Not new grammar — a map of tenses you already own, pointed at a new kind of story.

PartWhat it doesTenseUseful opener
Punto de partidaWhere you started, what you were likeimperfectoAl principio no sabía…
OportunidadThe opening that appearedpretéritoUn día me ofrecieron…
ObstáculoWhat went wrong, and what you didpretérito + perfectoCasi me di por vencido, pero he…
ResultadoWhere you are now, and your judgementperfecto + presentHasta ahora he… / Ha valido la pena.

The pattern to notice: a trayectoria starts in the closed past and arrives in the open present. That is why it ends in the perfect — the path is not finished. Yesterday's letter did exactly this, which is why it read as ongoing rather than as a report.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 8 min
✅ TRY IT · valer la pena agrees with the thing
«Todas esas horas de práctica ___ la pena.»
✅ TRY IT · the agreement in darse por vencido
A girl says she nearly gave up. Which is correct?
✅ TRY IT · name the part
«Al principio no entendía casi nada y me sentía perdido en clase.» Which part of a trayectoria?

Ahora tú: tell a four-sentence trayectoria — one sentence per part from Part 3. Say it aloud before revealing.

Al principio no sabía nadar y me daba vergüenza entrar en la piscina. En sexto grado mi escuela ofreció clases gratis y me apunté. El primer mes fue horrible y casi me di por vencido, pero seguí yendo dos veces por semana. Ahora he mejorado tanto que he conseguido un puesto en el equipo, y ha valido la pena.

Trace the tenses: sabía, daba (imperfect starting point) → ofreció, me apunté, fue, me di, seguí (preterite path) → he mejorado, he conseguido, ha valido (perfect, arriving at now).
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La beca de Mariela

Mariela vivía en un pueblo pequeño de Guanacaste y quería estudiar biología marina. En su escuela no había laboratorio y nadie de su familia había ido a la universidad.

En el último año, una profesora le habló de una beca del gobierno. Mariela llenó la solicitud tres veces porque la rechazaron dos. La segunda vez lloró toda la tarde y pensó dedicarse a otra cosa.

Pero no se dio por vencida. Ha estudiado cuatro años en Puntarenas, ha trabajado dos veranos en un proyecto de tortugas y este mes ha recibido un premio de su facultad. Dice que el esfuerzo ha valido la pena, y que su madre está más orgullosa que ella.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Map the four parts onto the three paragraphs. Then notice the tense architecture: paragraph 1 is entirely imperfect (the starting point), paragraph 2 entirely preterite (the closed path), paragraph 3 entirely present perfect (arriving at now). Three paragraphs, three frames, each doing one job. That is what tomorrow's grammar will let you do with even more precision.
🌎 CULTURA · COSTA RICA
Lo que se compró con el ejército

Mariela's story is invented, but its background is not. Costa Rica's decision to abolish the army in 1948 was tied to a decision about what to fund instead, and the country has since maintained free public education and a national health system that reach into small rural towns — the kind of place a pueblo pequeño de Guanacaste would be.

Costa Rica's literacy rate is among the highest in Latin America, and it protects around a quarter of its territory as national parks and reserves — which is why a student from a farming town might realistically end up working on a sea-turtle project. The achievement vocabulary in this module is not decoration on the culture; the culture is a two-generation story about exactly this word: oportunidad.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PERSEVERANCIA

Mariela filled in the same application three times. That is the whole shape of la perseverancia: not enduring one hard thing once, but returning to the same task after it has already told you no. Note the reading does not skip the crying — perseverance that pretends the setback did not hurt is not perseverance, it is performance. Se llora y se vuelve a intentar; eso es perseverar.
