⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

The tense of a résumé

Think about how anyone describes what they have accomplished: I have played piano for six years. I have never given up on it. The window is open — the life is still running — so it is the present perfect, exactly what you built last module.

That is why Module 2 follows Module 1. The grammar is already yours; today you get the words that go in it.

✏️ ESCRIBIR CON TILDES · you will be graded on this
This module's headline word carries a tilde: el éxito. In this course accents are graded, including in typed short answers — exito will be marked wrong.

Note also that el éxito is a false friend: it means success, not “exit.” The exit is la salida. A module about achievement is a good place to stop confusing the two.

Fix your typing now rather than in the middle of a quiz: Cómo escribir las tildes y la ñ — Windows, Mac, phone and Chromebook.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Two false friends worth retiring now. El éxito means success, not exit — an exit is la salida. And el suceso, which looks like “success,” actually means an event. So «fue un éxito» = it was a success, while «fue un suceso» = it was an incident. These two trip up nearly every Spanish 3 student at least once.
🧠 PART 3 · CUATRO VERBOS PARECIDOS 8 min

All four translate as “achieve” — and they are not interchangeable

VerbWhat it really meansTypical objectExample
lograrTo manage to do something difficult+ infinitive, or a resultHe logrado terminar el proyecto.
alcanzarTo reach a level or targeta goal, a number, a standardHemos alcanzado la meta del año.
conseguirTo obtain — get hold ofa thing: a place, a job, a ticketHa conseguido una beca.
superarTo overcome or exceedan obstacle, a fear, a recordHe superado el miedo a hablar.

A quick test: if there is an obstacle, use superar. If there is a target, use alcanzar. If you obtained a thing, use conseguir. If you managed to do something hard, use lograr. Note also intentar is the honest one — it says you tried, without claiming you succeeded: lo he intentado tres veces.

Ahora tú: name three things you have achieved, using three different verbs from Part 3. Say them aloud before revealing.

He logrado aprender a tocar la guitarra sin profesor. He conseguido un puesto en el equipo de natación. Y este año he superado mi miedo a hablar delante de la clase.

Check yours: lograr + infinitive for something difficult you managed · conseguir for a thing obtained · superar for an obstacle beaten. And every one in the present perfect, because your life is an open window.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La carta de solicitud

Estimada profesora:

Le escribo para solicitar la beca de verano. Durante estos tres años he estudiado español todos los días y he alcanzado un nivel que no esperaba al empezar. He participado en el club de conversación y he ayudado a estudiantes de primer año. No todo ha sido fácil: he intentado el examen oral dos veces y la primera vez fracasé. Pero he seguido practicando y he superado ese reto.

Mi meta es estudiar un semestre en Costa Rica. Creo que el esfuerzo ha valido la pena.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Nine present perfects and exactly one preterite. Find the preterite (fracasé) and ask why: the first attempt is a single closed event inside an otherwise open account. That one deliberate switch is the same move you practised in 1.06 — and it is what makes this letter read like a person rather than a form.
🌎 CULTURA · COSTA RICA
Un país que decidió no tener ejército

This module's country is Costa Rica, and its achievement is unusual enough to be worth stating plainly: in 1948, after a civil war, Costa Rica abolished its army — and it has not had one since. The money that would have funded a military went substantially into schools and health care.

Costa Ricans call themselves ticos, and the national phrase you will hear everywhere is «pura vida» — used as hello, goodbye, thanks, and “all good.” But do not mistake the relaxed phrase for a relaxed history: the decisions this module looks at were difficult and contested. On Day 8 you will read a real Spanish-language account of the abolition itself.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PERSEVERANCIA

This module's virtue is la perseverancia — perseverance. Notice it is not the same as effort: el esfuerzo is what you spend today, la perseverancia is spending it again tomorrow after today did not work. The letter in Part 5 contains the whole idea in one line — he intentado el examen dos veces y la primera vez fracasé. A student who had only succeeded would have less to say. No se mide en un día, sino en la repetición.
