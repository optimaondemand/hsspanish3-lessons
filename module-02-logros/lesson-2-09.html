Lesson 2.09
★ Tarea oral — lo que he logrado y lo que había intentado antes
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two tenses, one story: what you have done, and what had already happened first.
Today you record the Tarea oral (20 pts) — 20% of your semester grade. The content is chosen: yesterday's reading asked you to pick something you have achieved and what had come before it.

The specific challenge is holding two perfect tenses apart out loud. He logrado and había intentado differ by one word, and that word carries the whole time relationship.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to speak for 60–90 seconds using both the present perfect and the past perfect.
2
I will be able to pronounce había with the stress on the í, so a listener hears which tense I chose.
3
I will be able to speak from a six-word outline rather than a script.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

One word apart

«He intentado tres veces.» — up to now
«Había intentado tres veces.» — before something else happened

Same participle, same word order, and the entire time relationship rests on he versus había. In writing the accent tells your reader. In speech only the stress does — ha-BÍ-a, three syllables, the middle one loud.

Swallow that and your listener hears a present perfect. The ordering vanishes.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Say «nunca antes había hecho eso» aloud. How many syllables did había get?
🎤 PART 2 · LA TAREA 9 min

El tema: ¿Qué has logrado, y qué habías intentado o dejado antes de lograrlo?

Something you have achieved — and what had already happened before it. A previous attempt, a thing you had given up, a skill you had never had. It does not have to be impressive; it has to be true and ordered.

⭐ ENSAYA TRES VECES · before recording
Say the whole thing aloud three times from your six words. It should come out differently each time — that is the proof you are speaking rather than reciting. On run three, listen specifically for ha-BÍ-a.
🔊 PART 4 · PRONUNCIACIÓN · LA DIFERENCIA 11 min

Three things to drill

  • he, has, ha, hemos, hanpresent — h silent, vowel audible
  • había, habías, habíamospast — ha-BÍ-a, three syllables
  • hecho, dicho, visto…the eight irregulars

1. Había is three syllables with the stress in the middle. Collapsing it to “HA-bia” is the commonest error, and it makes your past perfect sound like something else. Exaggerate the í more than feels natural.

2. The h is silent in every form. He is “e”, había is “a-BÍ-a”. Aspirating it sounds foreign; dropping the word entirely loses the tense.

3. Irregular participles are short — do not stretch them. Hecho is two crisp syllables, not “he-CHO-o”.

✅ TRY IT · which tense will your listener hear?
You say “HA-bia intentado” with the stress on the first syllable. What is the risk?
✅ TRY IT · what the rubric weighs
Two takes: one has a heavy accent but a clear había doing real ordering; the other is smooth but has no past perfect anywhere. Which scores higher?
⏺ PART 5 · GRABA Y ENTREGA 6 min

Record in Canvas on the assignment. Listen once before submitting and check three things:

  1. Is there a había in there at all, and does it sit before another past event?
  2. Did había come out as three syllables?
  3. Did you reach 60 seconds? Short takes are usually missing beat 6.

If all three pass, submit. If one fails, you know exactly what to fix.

🌎 CULTURA · COSTA RICA
Hablar de logros sin presumir

A register note that will make your recording land better. Across much of Latin America, and notably in Costa Rica, describing your own achievements is normally softened — with a mention of who helped, with an acknowledgement of luck, or with understatement. «Me fue bien» does a lot of work.

Straight self-promotion, the kind an American college essay rewards, can read as presumido — boastful. The safest and most natural move is the one this module's grammar already pushes you toward: say what had come before. Mentioning the earlier failure or the help you had received is not modesty for its own sake; it is how the achievement gets framed as a path rather than a claim.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PERSEVERANCIA

The past perfect is, in a sense, the grammar of perseverance. It is the tense that lets you say what you had already tried before the thing finally worked — and without it, an achievement sounds like it simply happened. Sin pluscuamperfecto, todo logro parece suerte.
