⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Why good advice gets ignored

Think about the last piece of advice you were given and did not take. It was probably not wrong. Most advice that gets ignored is perfectly sound — and it fails anyway, for one of three reasons: it answered a question nobody asked, or it arrived before the person had finished explaining, or it left them no room to decide anything.

None of those is a grammar problem, which is why this module is not a grammar module. But Spanish gives you unusually precise tools for all three, and the most useful of them is the structure you learned last fortnight: «te aconsejo que…» exists precisely so that you can have an opinion out loud without taking the decision away.

✏️ ESCRIBIR CON TILDES · you will be graded on this
Exactly one of today's ten words carries a written accent: la orientación. Nine do not. That is the interesting part, because the commonest accent error at this level is no longer forgetting the tilde — it is adding one that does not belong.

Look at the three that tempt people most:

la orientación — stressed on the last syllable, ends in -n → tilde
la consecuencia — stressed on the next-to-last, ends in a vowel → no tilde
la duda — same shape → no tilde

So the rule is not “long words take accents.” It is the one you have had since Spanish 1: a word stressed on the final syllable and ending in a vowel, -n or -s needs the mark; a word stressed on the penultimate and ending in a vowel does not. Every -ción noun takes it (la orientación, la decisión, la precaución) and no -encia or -anza noun does (la consecuencia, la confianza, la experiencia).

You will type these in a graded item on Día 7, and orientacion will be marked wrong because it is not a Spanish word. If your typing is not yet quick: Cómo escribir las tildes y la ñ — Windows, Mac, phone and Chromebook.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
The false friend, and it is a real trap. El compromiso does not mean “compromise.” It means a commitment — something you have undertaken and are now bound to. Tengo un compromiso el viernes means you have an engagement, not that you have met somebody halfway. English “compromise” is closer to un acuerdo, or un término medio. Get this one wrong in a written task and you say the opposite of what you meant.

Three words English collapses. El punto de vista is your angle — one person's, and openly partial. La perspectiva is the wider view, the one that comes from standing further back or from having seen the thing before. La orientación is neither: it is the help itself — guidance, the pointing of a direction. A school counsellor gives orientación; your best friend gives you su punto de vista; three years of distance give you perspectiva. Advice usually needs all three and people usually offer only the second.

And la confianza does double duty. One word for “trust” and “confidence” — which is arguably more honest than English, since they are the same thing pointed in different directions. Note the fixed phrase una persona de confianza: someone you can rely on. Advice from anyone else is just an opinion.
🧠 PART 3 · LO QUE TRAES DEL MÓDULO 7 9 min

One subject or two — the decision that has not gone away

Nothing here is new. Read it as a check on yourself rather than as instruction, because from Día 3 onward this decision gets made five or six times inside a single paragraph.

  • Quiero que estudies.two subjects → que + subjuntivo
TriggerOne subject → infinitivoTwo subjects → que + subjuntivo
quererQuiero ayudarte.Quiero que me escuches.
recomendarTe recomiendo esperar un día.Te recomiendo que esperes un día.
pedirTe pido un poco de tiempo.Te pido que me des un poco de tiempo.

The right-hand column is not politer than the left. It is more specific: it names who does the acting. Es importante escuchar is a general truth about the world. Es importante que escuches is about you, this week, and it cannot be read as anything else.

✅ TRY IT · ¿lleva tilde?
Which of these is spelled correctly?

Ahora tú: think of a decision somebody you know is facing right now — not you. In Spanish, say one alternativa they have, one consecuencia of it, and one duda you would want cleared up before offering any opinion. Stay in the indicative: today you are describing the situation, not pushing at it.

—Mi hermana no sabe si aceptar el trabajo del verano. La alternativa es quedarse en casa y estudiar para el examen de septiembre. La consecuencia de aceptar es que pierde julio entero. Mi duda es si el trabajo le sirve de verdad para lo que quiere estudiar; sin eso, no tengo nada útil que decirle.

Check yours: every verb indicative — no sabe, es, pierde, tengo — because you are reporting a situation. The one push in the model is deliberately absent. Notice also no sabe si aceptar: si takes the indicative or an infinitive and never a subjunctive, which is worth banking now, because it looks like a place where one might go.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

«¿Me recomiendas Chile?»

—Mi prima me preguntó ayer si le recomiendo Chile para enero. Le dije que la pregunta no tiene respuesta.
—¿Cómo que no tiene respuesta?
—Porque Chile mide más de cuatro mil kilómetros de norte a sur y menos de doscientos de ancho. En enero, en Arica hace un calor seco y no llueve nunca; en Punta Arenas el viento te tumba. Es el mismo país y son dos viajes distintos.
—Entonces le falta información.
—Le falta perspectiva. Yo puedo darle mi punto de vista, pero no le sirve de mucho: a mí me interesa el desierto y a ella el hielo. Lo que necesita es orientación — qué alternativas tiene, qué consecuencia trae cada una y cuánto cuesta cada vuelo interno.
—¿Y si no se decide?
—Le pido una cosa nada más: que me diga primero qué quiere ver. Sin eso, cualquier consejo mío es adorno.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Count the subjunctives in that dialogue. There is one: «que me diga», after le pido. Everything else reports — mide, hace, llueve, necesita, trae, cuesta — because the speaker is describing a country and a problem, not pushing anybody anywhere.

That ratio is worth noticing today and will matter enormously on Día 3. Students who have just learned the subjunctive tend to write paragraphs that are all subjunctive, and the result reads like a list of instructions. Real advice is mostly indicative with a few well-placed pushes.

Notice too how the last line splits the trigger from its clause with a colon: «Le pido una cosa nada más: que me diga…». That is ordinary written Spanish, and it is a useful move — it lets you announce that a request is coming before you make it.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Un país que no se puede aconsejar en general

This module's country is Chile, and its geography is the reason the dialogue above cannot be answered. Chile runs roughly 4,300 km from north to south and averages only about 180 km wide — in places under a hundred. It is a country shaped like a coastline, pinned between the Andes and the Pacific.

What that produces is not one climate but a stack of them. The northern end is the Atacama, the driest non-polar desert on earth, where some weather stations have gone years without measurable rain. The southern end is Patagonia — glaciers, ice fields, and the granite towers of Torres del Paine, where the wind is a daily fact rather than an event. Between them sits the central valley where most Chileans live and nearly all the wine is grown. Chile also administers Rapa Nui (Easter Island), some 3,700 km out into the Pacific.

Travelling from Arica in the far north to Punta Arenas in the far south covers a distance comparable to Lisbon–Moscow. So “have you been to Chile?” is a much less informative question than it sounds — and this fortnight, that is the point. Advice that does not ask which situation it is for is decoration.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA DILIGENCIA (MÓDULO 0)

Módulo 8 does not introduce a new virtue. It revisits the eight the semester has already named — la diligencia, la templanza, la perseverancia, la cortesía, el servicio, la responsabilidad, la atención, la prudencia — one per lesson, in the order you met them, and each time inside the single act of advising somebody. That is what a final module is for.

So today it is la diligencia, where the semester began. Diligence is the least glamorous virtue on the list and the only one the other seven need: it is simply doing the work in front of you, properly, on the day it is in front of you. Its test is not the first week of a course but the seventeenth — when the vocabulary list is the eighth of its kind, the novelty has gone, and there is a gate at the end of the fortnight. Say the ten words aloud anyway. La diligencia no se mide al empezar, sino al terminar.
