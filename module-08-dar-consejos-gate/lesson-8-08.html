Lesson 8.08
★ Lectura interpretativa — «La oración de la maestra», y dos trabajos para una misma palabra
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A Chilean village schoolteacher wrote this about her own job. Its first sentence contains the whole semester.
The last reading day of Semester 1, and the strongest text in it. Gabriela Mistral published Desolación in 1922; twenty-three years later she became the first Latin American to win the Nobel Prize in Literature.

«La oración de la maestra» is a sustained petition — one long act of asking — which means it runs on Módulo 8's grammar from the first line to the last. And in its opening sentence the word que appears twice, inches apart, doing two completely different jobs. That contrast is what you are here for.

The graded Lectura interpretativa (15 pts) opens today.

⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 7 min

Quién escribe, y por qué eso importa

Lucila Godoy Alcayaga was born in 1889 in Vicuña, in the Elqui valley — a narrow strip of green between bare mountains in the north of Chile, on the edge of the desert you read about in 8.06. She began working as a teacher while she was still a teenager, in rural schools, without a university degree, and she went on teaching for decades. She published under the name Gabriela Mistral.

In 1945 she became the first Latin American writer of any kind to win the Nobel Prize in Literature.

Hold on to the first fact while you read the second. This is not a poet imagining what a teacher's inner life might be like. It is a working country schoolteacher writing about her own job — the classroom she actually stood in, the pupils who actually forgot her — and asking, in the form her culture gave her for asking, to be made better at it.

Why this text sits in Módulo 8. A prayer is grammatically a petition: somebody asks somebody else to do something. That is precisely the structure this module owns. Every request in the poem is built out of the machinery you have spent two weeks on — which is why the reading day of the module about advising and requesting is a poem, and not a news article.

Busca estas tres cosas. No dictionary on the first pass:

  1. ¿Qué pide para sí misma, y qué pide no sentir?
  2. En la primera frase, que aparece dos veces. ¿Son iguales los dos verbos que la siguen?
  3. ¿Cuántos mandatos dirigidos a Dios puedes encontrar?
📚 PART 2 · EL TEXTO 10 min
📖 LECTURA AUTÉNTICA · DOMINIO PÚBLICO · 1922
¡Señor! Tú que enseñaste, perdona que yo enseñe; que lleve el nombre de maestra, que tú llevaste por la Tierra.

Dame el amor único de mi escuela; que ni la quemadura de la belleza sea capaz de robarle mi ternura de todos los instantes.

Maestro, hazme perdurable el fervor y pasajero el desencanto. Arranca de mí este impuro deseo de justicia que aún me turba, la mezquina insinuación de protesta que sube de mí cuando me hieren. No me duela la incomprensión ni me entristezca el olvido de las que enseñé.
[…]
Dame sencillez y dame profundidad; líbrame de ser complicada o banal, en mi lección cotidiana.
Gabriela Mistral, «La oración de la maestra», Desolación, 1922. Dominio público.

Sobre el texto. The first three paragraphs run continuously in the 1922 edition. The final paragraph comes from later in the same prayer, so material has been omitted — that is what the centred […] marks. Nothing inside the four paragraphs has been altered, cut or modernised; the spelling, the punctuation and the capital T in por la Tierra are Mistral's.

📘 GLOSARIO · español de 1922 · read this before your second pass
la quemadura = a burn, a burning · la ternura = tenderness · perdurable = lasting, enduring · pasajero = passing, short-lived (as an adjective; as a noun it is a passenger) · el desencanto = disenchantment, the loss of illusions · el fervor = fervour, ardour · arrancar = to tear out, to pull up by the roots · turbar = to disturb, to trouble, to unsettle · mezquino = mean, petty, small-minded · la insinuación = a hint, an insinuation · la protesta = protest, complaint · herir = to wound, to hurt · la incomprensión = being misunderstood · el olvido = forgetting, oblivion · la sencillez = simplicity, plainness · la profundidad = depth · banal = banal, trivial, empty · cotidiano = daily, everyday.

Two that decide whole sentences. La quemadura de la belleza is beauty as something that scorches — Mistral is asking not to be so taken by beautiful things that she has nothing tender left for the children in front of her. And las que enseñé is feminine plural: the girls she taught. She is asking not to be saddened when they forget her, which they will.

Three forms are not yours yet — sea capaz, no me duela, ni me entristezca. They are subjunctives, but they are not the kind this module taught you: two of them have no visible trigger word in front of them at all. Read them as wishes — “may it not be able to…”, “may it not hurt me…” — and move on. This is recognition only. Nothing on this page or in the graded item asks you to produce them, and the rest of that system — the subjunctive after emotion and after doubt — is Semester 2's work.

🧠 PART 3 · COMPRENSIÓN 9 min

Practice first — ungraded. The graded item asks the same four kinds of question, and you are expected to reread the text while answering both.

✅ IDEA GENERAL · gist
¿Cuál es la petición central del poema?
✅ DETALLE · detail
Según la tercera estrofa, ¿qué debe durar y qué debe pasar?
✅ GRAMÁTICA EN EL TEXTO · the Honors item
«Perdona que yo enseñe» — subjuntivo. «El nombre de maestra, que tú llevaste» — indicativo. Misma palabra que, a cinco palabras de distancia. ¿Por qué?
✅ INFERENCIA · inference
Pide sencillez y profundidad, y pide que la libren de ser «complicada o banal». ¿Por qué esas dos palabras juntas?
🔍 PART 4 · CUATRO MÓDULOS EN UN POEMA 8 min

Everything you built since October, in nine lines of 1922

En el textoQué esMódulo
perdona que yo enseñeVerb of petition + que + subjuntivoM7–M8
que tú llevasteQue relativo + indicativo (pretérito): a fact about the pastM6
Dame · hazme · líbrameAffirmative tú commands with the pronoun attached, tilde and allM4
Arranca de míAffirmative tú command, no pronoun attachedM4

cuando me hierenCuando + indicativo, because it is habitual—

On that last row: cuando me hieren is indicative and it is correct — she is not talking about one occasion but about what happens whenever it happens. Cuando can also take the subjunctive, in a use this semester does not teach; that belongs to Semester 2. Here the indicative is right, and now you know why.

💡 DOS TEXTOS, LA MISMA LECCIÓN · look back at 7.08
Reopen Módulo 7's reading day for a moment. Ricardo Palma's pedlar says «le pido y ruego que me preste esa puchuela» — request, so subjunctive — and in the very same speech, «que si él lo quiere» — indicative, because it reports a condition rather than asking for anything.

Mistral, a completely different writer, in a completely different century, in a completely different kind of text, does exactly the same thing in her opening sentence: que yo enseñe against que tú llevaste.

That is not a coincidence and it is not a rule invented by a textbook. Two authentic texts, twenty-six years and one border apart — Lima in 1896, Chile in 1922 — make the same point because it is how the language works: que triggers nothing. The verb in front of it decides everything. A student who can say that sentence out loud has finished Semester 1's grammar, whatever the Grammar Quiz says on Día 10.

🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Dos Premios Nobel de Literatura

Chile has around twenty million people and two Nobel laureates in literature: Gabriela Mistral in 1945 and Pablo Neruda in 1971. Very few countries of any size have two. Both were poets, both spent much of their lives representing Chile abroad in consular and diplomatic posts, and both are read across the Spanish-speaking world rather than only at home.

Mistral's route there was the unlikelier one. No university degree, a rural teaching career begun in her teens, and a first book published in 1922 in New York rather than Santiago — it was the Instituto de las Españas at Columbia University that brought out Desolación. She later worked on rural-education reform in Mexico at the invitation of its government, which is a thread back to Módulo 1's culture, and she died in New York in 1957.

She is on the 5,000-peso note. A country that puts a village schoolteacher on its currency is telling you something about what it thinks it is, and «La oración de la maestra» is the text where that idea is at its plainest: not a poem about teaching as a metaphor, but a working teacher asking to be made adequate to Monday morning.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PRUDENCIA (MÓDULO 7)

Módulo 7's virtue was la prudencia — not caution, but judging well what a particular situation asks for. This poem is an extended exercise in it, and the exercise is: what is actually worth asking for?

Notice what Mistral does not request. Nothing about recognition, nothing about better pupils, nothing about being treated fairly — and this is the startling line, she asks to have her own «impuro deseo de justicia» torn out by the roots. She has looked at her situation and concluded that the thing most likely to spoil her teaching is not her circumstances but a movement in herself that feels, from the inside, like a virtue. Knowing which of your own impulses to distrust is about as far as prudence goes.

And every request is for something that gets spent on somebody else: tenderness that does not run out, fervour that lasts, plainness and depth in Monday's lesson. She is not asking for tools. She is asking to be made into the kind of person the job needs, which is a far more accurate diagnosis than most of us manage about our own work. La prudencia empieza por saber qué vale la pena pedir.
