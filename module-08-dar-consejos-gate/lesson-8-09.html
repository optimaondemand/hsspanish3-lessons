★ Tarea oral — noventa segundos de consejo sobre un problema real
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
In writing, the difference between reporting and advising is one letter. Out loud, it is one vowel — and nothing else.
Today you record the Tarea oral (20 pts) — 20% of your semester grade, and the last recording of Semester 1.

The task is the one this whole module has been building toward: somebody brings you a real problem, and you advise them in connected Spanish for sixty to ninety seconds.

The specific difficulty is audible. Hablas and hables differ by a single unstressed vowel at the end of a word, and that vowel is the only signal a listener gets that you are advising rather than describing. Today's pronunciation work is about landing it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to speak for 60–90 seconds advising a specific person about a specific decision.
2
I will be able to pronounce the subjunctive ending clearly enough that a listener hears advice, not narration.
3
I will be able to carry three influence structures and two registers through one unscripted minute.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Six words, not a script

Same method as every module: a six-word outline, never a written-out text. With advice the difference is unusually audible. A read paragraph of advice sounds like a policy; the same content from six words sounds like a person who has thought about your problem — and the second one is the whole point of the task.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Say these two out loud, one after the other: «Te recomiendo que hablas con él» · «Te recomiendo que hables con él». What happened?
🎤 PART 2 · LA TAREA 9 min

El tema: Alguien te plantea un problema real. Dale tu consejo en sesenta o noventa segundos.

Use a real situation if you have one — your own or somebody's you actually know, with names changed if you prefer. Real dilemmas produce far better recordings, because you already know what the difficulty is. If you would rather not, take one of these three.

🧠 PART 3 · TU GUION DE SEIS PALABRAS 9 min

One word per beat, written in the margin of a page. These are the same seven jobs from 8.06, compressed to six for speech — the dato and the reason merge when you are talking.

BeatYour wordExampleStructure
1. The situation, named nataciónindicativo
2. The reason it is hard horarioindicativo + porque
3. El consejo central hablarque + subjuntivo
4. The practical detail jefeimpersonal + que, or infinitivo
5. What not to do silencionegativo
6. Hand it back tuyaindicativo o mandato

Beats 3, 5 and 6 are each named in the rubric. Write those three words first; if you run short of time on the recording, it is beats 2 and 4 that can go.

⭐ ENSAYA TRES VECES
Say the whole thing aloud three times from your six words, and change something each time. On run three, listen for three specific things: (1) did the vowel in your subjunctive endings survive the speed? (2) did you use more than one register, or did every piece of advice come out as te recomiendo que? (3) did the last sentence hand the decision back, or did it repeat the advice one more time?

Three runs is not a lot. The recordings that score badly are almost always first takes, and the giveaway is not the grammar — it is that they end twice.
🔊 PART 4 · PRONUNCIACIÓN DEL CONSEJO 11 min

Click each, then say it back at the same speed. Do not slow the model down — copy its rhythm.

  • Te aconsejo que hables con ella.the run-up is fast; the last word is not
  • Te pido que me escuches.two unstressed pronouns, then the verb
  • Sugiero que lo pienses bien.no pause before que
  • Recomiendo que lo intentes.intentes, not intentas
  • Es necesario que llegues temprano.the impersonal register, same ending

1. The trigger is a run-up, not the point. Te aconsejo que is five syllables of scaffolding said quickly and almost flat. The information is in the verb after it. English speakers do the opposite — they lean on recomiendo and then drop the ending, which puts the emphasis on the least informative part of the sentence.

2. Never pause before que. A pause there splits one sentence into two fragments and the listener loses the connection between them. Que belongs to the run-up: «sugieroque», almost one word, and then the verb lands clear.

3. Hold the final vowel deliberately. Hables, escuches, pienses, intentes, llegues — that unstressed -e- before the -s is doing all the grammatical work in the sentence, and it is the first sound to disappear when you speed up. Give it more than feels natural. Overdoing it is not a fault a listener will notice; losing it is.

✅ TRY IT · what the rubric weighs
Two takes. A: strong American accent, three different structures, a reason on two of them, ends by handing the decision back. B: smooth and quick, five te recomiendo que in a row, no reasons, ends by repeating the advice. Which scores higher?
✅ TRY IT · why one vowel matters
Why does the difference between hablas and hables matter more when you speak than when you write?
⏺ PART 5 · GRABA Y ENTREGA 6 min

Record in Canvas on the assignment. Listen to your take once before submitting and check four things:

  1. Did the first ten seconds name the person and the decision, with no advice in them?
  2. Can you hear the final vowel on every subjunctive?
  3. Did you use more than one register, or was it te recomiendo que throughout?
  4. Did the last sentence hand the decision back?

And one thing to avoid saying at all. Keep your trigger verbs in the present — te recomiendo que, te pido que, es importante que. If you reach for the past («le recomendé que…») Spanish requires a form this course has not taught you for the verb after que, and there is no way around it. To mention advice already given, use a noun or an infinitive: me pidió ayuda, le recomendé dos días de espera. You are advising somebody now, so the present is where you belong anyway.

If all four pass, submit. If one fails, it is faster to re-record than to talk yourself into it — you have unlimited takes and only the best one is graded.

🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Los 33 — y una nota de una sola línea

On 5 August 2010 the San José mine near Copiapó, in the Atacama, collapsed and sealed thirty-three men roughly 700 metres underground. For seventeen days nobody above ground knew whether any of them were alive.

Then a narrow exploratory drill broke into the shelter, and when it was pulled back up there was a note taped to it. Seven words: «Estamos bien en el refugio los 33».

Think about the constraint. One chance, a scrap of paper, and no way to send a second line — so every word had to earn its place. They are alive (estamos bien), they are together in a known place (en el refugio), and there are thirty-three of them, which is the number rescuers needed most. No complaint, no request, no explanation. The rescue took another fifty-two days; the last man came up through a rescue capsule on 13 October 2010, sixty-nine days after the collapse, and all thirty-three survived.

Chileans still quote that line. It is the national example of saying the necessary thing in the space available — which is, in a much smaller way, what you are being asked to do in ninety seconds today.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS OCHO, EN NOVENTA SEGUNDOS

This module has no virtue of its own, because advising somebody well needs all eight of them at once — and ninety seconds is short enough that you can watch each one arrive.

La atención (M6) is beat 1: you cannot restate a situation you did not listen to. La templanza (M1) is choosing one central piece of advice when you have four. La responsabilidad (M5) is beat 2, the reason — the sentence that commits you to what you are about to say. La prudencia (M7) is beat 3, deciding whether this moment wants hazlo or te pido que lo hagas. La cortesía (M3) is the register that carries it, and el servicio (M4) is advising for their good rather than for the pleasure of being right — which is beat 5, the thing you tell them not to do even though it will not be welcome. La diligencia (M0) is rehearsing three times for a listener who will hear it once.

And la perseverancia (M2) is take four. Nobody will hear takes one, two and three; nobody will know the submitted version was the one where you finally stopped saying te recomiendo que five times. En noventa segundos caben las ocho, y se nota cuando falta una.
