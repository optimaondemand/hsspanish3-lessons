★ El consejo por escrito — un párrafo entero, construido y luego criticado
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Five correct subjunctives in a row can still be bad advice. Today is about the paragraph, not the verb.
You can now build the sentences. Días 3, 4 and 5 gave you the trigger, the register choice, and real dilemmas to use them on.

What is left is the thing the graded work actually measures: a whole paragraph of advice that a real person could use. You will read one — a letter about a trip to Chile, dense with this module's grammar — take it apart to see how it is built, then take apart a bad one, which is the faster teacher of the two.

At the end you draft the paragraph you will submit tomorrow. Tomorrow you revise it. You do not start again.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Think of the last time somebody advised you

Not a teacher, not a form — a person, about something that mattered. Bring it to mind, and notice what they actually did with the two minutes they had.

Almost certainly they did not open with the advice. They said something first that proved they had understood the situation. Then they gave you one thing, with a reason attached. Then they left the decision with you, because it was yours.

That shape is not a courtesy added to advice. In Spanish it is largely grammatical, and this module has given you every piece of it: the que + subjunctive for the push, the infinitive and the impersonal for the softer register, the plain command when directness is what a moment calls for.

📖 PART 2 · A LEER · UNA CARTA CON CONSEJOS 11 min

El contexto. Someone who has lived in Santiago for three years writes back to a friend who has just announced a two-week itinerary. Read it once for the advice and once for the grammar. Two things to watch for: where the central piece of advice sits, and how many different structures carry the influence.

✏️ TEXTO DEL CURSO · escrito para esta lección
Recibí tu mensaje anoche y me pongo a escribir de inmediato, porque tu plan tiene un problema de mapa. Chile mide más de cuatro mil trescientos kilómetros de norte a sur y su ancho medio no llega a ciento ochenta: es un país largo y estrecho, y todo aquí se mide en horas de vuelo. Tú quieres ver el desierto de Atacama y también las Torres del Paine en la misma semana, y hay más de tres mil kilómetros en línea recta entre los dos. Te aconsejo que elijas uno. Si eliges el norte, es importante que reserves el vuelo a Calama con tiempo y que subas despacio, porque San Pedro de Atacama está a dos mil cuatrocientos metros y los geiseres del Tatio, mucho más arriba. Si eliges el sur, te pido que no confíes en el pronóstico: en la Patagonia hace sol, llueve y vuelve a hacer sol en la misma tarde, y el viento no descansa nunca. Una advertencia para los dos casos: conviene llevar bloqueador incluso en invierno, porque el aire seco del norte engaña y en el sur el viento esconde el sol. No te recomiendo que hagas los dos viajes; te recomiendo que vuelvas.
Texto del curso, escrito para esta lección. Datos geográficos verificados: longitud de Chile, altitud de San Pedro de Atacama y del campo geotérmico del Tatio.

Ayuda de vocabulario — after your second reading: el ancho medio = the average width · estrecho = narrow · en línea recta = as the crow flies · elegir = to choose · con tiempo = well in advance · el pronóstico = the forecast · el bloqueador = sunscreen · engañar = to deceive, to be misleading.

Count the influence structures before you turn to Part 3. There are five pieces of advice in that letter and they arrive in four different shapes. That ratio is the whole lesson.

🧠 PART 3 · CÓMO ESTÁ CONSTRUIDO 9 min

Seven jobs, in this order

#The jobIn the letterStructure
1Reconocer — prove you read itRecibí tu mensaje… tu plan tiene un problema de mapaindicativo
2El dato — the fact the advice rests onChile mide más de cuatro mil trescientos kilómetros…indicativo
3El consejo central — one thing, shortTe aconsejo que elijas uno.que + subjuntivo
4El detalle prácticoEs importante que reserves… y que subas despacioimpersonal + que
5La advertenciaTe pido que no confíes en el pronósticoque + subjuntivo, negativo
6El registro alterno — no second person at allConviene llevar bloqueadorimpersonal + infinitivo
7El cierre — hand the decision backNo te recomiendo que hagas los dos viajes; te recomiendo que vuelvas.contraste

Notice job 3. The central advice is four words long, it arrives third rather than first, and everything before it exists to make it land. A reader who was told «elige uno» in sentence one would argue. A reader who has just read the distance agrees before the advice is given.

Notice job 6 too, because it is the one students skip. Conviene llevar bloqueador names nobody. There is no tú, no que, no subjunctive — and it is still advice. Día 4's register table is what makes that available to you, and a paragraph that never uses it sounds like it has one gear.

🔍 PART 4 · LA CRÍTICA 8 min

A paragraph with nothing wrong in it

Here is advice to a student who is thinking of dropping a hard class. Read it and find the error.

⚠️ UN PÁRRAFO DÉBIL
Te recomiendo que estudies más. Te recomiendo que hables con tu profesora. Te recomiendo que no dejes la clase. Te recomiendo que pienses en tu futuro. Te recomiendo que tomes una decisión pronto.

There is no error. Five triggers, five subjunctives, five correct forms — estudies, hables, dejes, pienses, tomes — and the last two even carry a stem change and a diphthong correctly. On a grammar quiz this scores full marks.

As advice it is useless, and it is worth being precise about why: it acknowledges nothing, it gives no reason for anything, it offers five pieces of advice that compete with one another, and it uses one structure five times so that nothing in it sounds more important than anything else. The rubric for tomorrow's task scores four separate criteria that this paragraph fails, and grammar accuracy is not one of them.

✅ TRY IT · diagnose it
Which is the most useful single description of what is wrong?
✅ TRY IT · the best repair

✍️ PART 5 · TU BORRADOR 6 min

El tema de Lengua en acción: Una persona concreta que conoces tiene que tomar una decisión difícil. Escríbele un párrafo de consejos.

Draft it now, in six to eight connected sentences. Tomorrow you revise this draft and submit it — 20 points. A real person and a real decision produce a far better paragraph than an invented one, so use Día 5's dilemma if you have one, or your own.

RequirementDetail
Length6–8 connected sentences — a paragraph, not a list
Sentence 1Name the person and the decision. No advice yet.
Structures≥3 influence structures in at least two different registers (que + subjuntivo · infinitivo · impersonal · mandato)
ReasonsAt least two pieces of advice carry a porque or ya que
The negativeOne thing you deliberately do not advise
El cierreThe last sentence leaves the decision with them
AccentsGraded. elijas, reserves, confíes — read your -íes endings twice

Write it before you open the checklist.

Read your draft against these seven, in order, and mark the ones you cannot find:

1. Does sentence 1 name the person and the decision, with no advice in it?
2. Is there a dato — something true about the situation that your advice rests on?
3. Can you point to one sentence as the central advice? If two compete, cut one.
4. Count your structures. Three or more, in two or more registers?
5. Do at least two pieces of advice have a reason attached?
6. Is there something you say not to do?
7. Does the last sentence hand the decision back — or does it repeat the advice?

Most drafts fail 2, 5 and 7 together, which is one habit rather than three: giving advice without saying why, and then repeating it because it did not sound convincing the first time.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
El país más largo del mundo — y el cielo más limpio

This module's country is Chile, and its shape is not a curiosity — it explains almost everything else. Just over 4,300 kilometres from the Peruvian border to Cape Horn, with an average width under 180, Chile runs through the driest desert on Earth, a Mediterranean centre, temperate rainforest, and Patagonian ice. In the far north there are weather stations that have gone decades without recording measurable rain.

That dryness turned out to be worth money. Dry air, high altitude and almost no cloud make northern Chile the best place on the planet to point a telescope, and the world's observatories moved in: ALMA, sixty-six antennas on the Chajnantor plateau at about 5,000 metres, and the Very Large Telescope at Paranal, among many others. Chile negotiated a permanent share in the deal — astronomers based in Chile are guaranteed 10% of observing time on telescopes built on Chilean soil, which is why a small country now trains a disproportionate number of the world's astronomers.

And Torres del Paine, three granite towers at the other end of the country, is roughly as far from the Atacama as Madrid is from Moscow. That is why the letter in Part 2 says what it says.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA RESPONSABILIDAD (MÓDULO 5)

Módulo 5's virtue was la responsabilidad — stewardship, learned in the Galápagos, where it meant owning the consequences of what you do to something that cannot answer back. Advising somebody is the same virtue pointed at a person.

Look at what the letter in Part 2 actually does. It tells a friend to cancel half a trip they have been planning for months. If the writer is wrong — if the two weeks would in fact have worked — that friend loses something real, and the writer is the reason. Which is why the letter gives its facts before its advice: advice you are willing to be held to looks different from advice you are only offering.

That is also the deepest reason the weak paragraph in Part 4 fails. Five recommendations with no reasons attached commit the writer to nothing; if any of them turns out badly, there is nothing to point at. Naming your reason is how you take responsibility for the advice in advance. Quien aconseja se queda con una parte de lo que pase después.
