Lesson 8.02
★ Los verbos de decidir — lo que hace quien recibe el consejo
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Advice is half of an exchange. The other half has its own ten verbs, and nobody ever teaches them.
Yesterday you learned the nouns of advising: el apoyo, la orientación, la consecuencia. Every one of them belongs to the person doing the advising.

Today you get the verbs that belong to the person on the receiving end — somebody proposes, you weigh, you reflect, and eventually you commit or you do not dare. Those are the verbs a real conversation about a decision actually runs on, and Módulo 7 gave you none of them, because Módulo 7 was about the pushing.

Still no new grammar. Every one of today's verbs is used here with an infinitive or a noun — Spanish you have had since the first year. What they insist on instead is the right preposition, and that is Part 3.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use ten verbs of proposing, weighing and committing.
2
I will be able to attach the preposition each one demands — comprometerse a, atreverse a, reflexionar sobre.
3
I will be able to distinguish plantear, proponer and animar, which do three different jobs English blurs together.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Three stages, and only one of them is hard

Every decision somebody takes advice about moves through the same three stages, and Spanish has separate verbs for each:

1. Alguien propone  →  2. Tú consideras  →  3. Te comprometes

Most people are fine at stage 1 and stage 2. Stage 2 is even pleasant — weighing options costs nothing and feels like progress. Stage 3 is where decisions actually go to die, and Spanish is unusually honest about it: the verb for committing, comprometerse, is built from compromiso, the obligation you take on. You do not decide in Spanish so much as bind yourself.

📚 PART 2 · PROPONER, PESAR, COMPROMETERSE 12 min

Click the 🔊 and repeat aloud. Four of these ten are reflexive, and the audio gives you the form you will actually write.

  • animarto encourage, to urge on
  • proponerto propose, to put forward
  • plantearto raise, to set out (a question)
  • considerarto consider, to weigh
  • reflexionarto reflect, to think over
  • valorarto value; to weigh up
  • arriesgarseto take a risk
  • atreverse ato dare to
  • comprometerseto commit oneself
  • agradecerto thank, to be grateful for
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Three verbs, three different jobs. English uses “suggest” for all of them and loses the distinction. Proponer puts a course of action on the table: propongo esperar una semana. Plantear puts a question or a problem there for consideration: me planteó una duda que no había pensado. And animar does neither — it supplies the nerve: me animó a presentarme. Notice that these are three genuinely different favours. The best advice you have ever been given was probably a plantear, not a proponer: somebody asked you the question you had been avoiding.

Two irregulars you already own. Proponer is poner with a prefix, so it behaves exactly like it: propongo in the yo form, and the past participle is propuesto — me ha propuesto un plan. That is Módulo 2's puesto with four letters in front. And agradecer joins the -zco family from Módulo 3: agradezco, like conozco.

A note on valorar. It swings between two meanings and context sorts them: valoro tu apoyo is “I appreciate your support,” warm and personal; hay que valorar las dos alternativas is “we must assess both options,” cool and analytical. Both are extremely common. And for thanking, keep agradecer attached to a noun — te agradezco la orientación — which is the cleanest and most useful pattern.
✏️ PART 3 · LA PREPOSICIÓN MANDA 9 min

Learn the verb with its preposition attached, or you will lose it

Spanish verbs of this kind come with a preposition welded on, and the preposition is not guessable from English. «Me comprometo estudiar» is not a small slip; it is the sort of thing that reads as a first-year error in an otherwise good paragraph. Read the middle column aloud as part of the verb.

VerboSe construye con…Ejemplo
animara alguien + a + infinitivoMi vecina me animó a presentarme.
proponer+ sustantivo o infinitivoPropongo esperar una semana.
plantear+ sustantivo (a alguien)Me planteó una pregunta incómoda.
considerar+ sustantivo o infinitivoConsidera cambiar de plan.
reflexionarsobre + sustantivoReflexiona sobre las consecuencias.
valorar+ sustantivoValoro mucho tu apoyo.
arriesgarsea + infinitivoSe arriesga a perder el semestre.
atreversea + infinitivoNo se atreve a decírselo.
comprometersea + infinitivoMe comprometo a estudiar dos horas.
agradecer+ sustantivo (sin preposición)Te agradezco la orientación.

Three of the ten take a before an infinitive — arriesgarse, atreverse, comprometerse — and all three are the stage-3 verbs, the ones about actually doing the thing. One takes sobre. And agradecer takes none at all, which is the one people over-correct.

💡 NADA NUEVO HOY · Y UN AVISO PARA MAÑANA
Every example in that table uses a noun or an infinitive. No que, no subjunctive, nothing you did not already have last September.

But look at proponer again. It is a verb of influence — it pushes at the world — and you already know what Spanish does when a verb like that takes a whole clause with two subjects:

«Propongo esperar una semana.» — one subject: me. Infinitive.
«Propongo que esperemos una semana.» — two subjects. Que + subjuntivo.

That second sentence is not new grammar. It is Módulo 7 with a Módulo 8 verb, and you can build it right now. Tomorrow you start building five of them in a row, in one paragraph, which is a different skill entirely.

✅ TRY IT · atreverse o arriesgarse
After three weeks of silence, he finally says the difficult thing out loud. Which verb does Spanish reach for?

Ahora tú: take a decision you have actually made in the last year and narrate it in three sentences, using one verb from each stage — somebody propuso or planteó, you consideraste or reflexionaste, and you te comprometiste or te atreviste. Keep the prepositions attached. Say it aloud before revealing.

Mi profesora de música me propuso tocar en el concierto de diciembre. Reflexioné sobre el asunto casi un mes, porque nunca había tocado solo delante de tanta gente. Al final me atreví a decir que sí, y me comprometí a ensayar cuatro días por semana hasta el concierto.

Check yours: reflexionar sobre · atreverse a · comprometerse a · and propuso spelled with that -pus- stem it inherits from poner. Notice nunca había tocado — Módulo 2's past perfect, doing exactly its job: a moment further back than the story's own past.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Los ciento veinte escalones

Vivo en el Cerro Alegre, en Valparaíso. Para llegar a mi casa hay que subir ciento veinte escalones o tomar el ascensor, que tiene más de cien años y todavía funciona.

Hace un mes mi vecina me planteó algo. El municipio busca guías jóvenes para los recorridos de los cerros, y ella me propuso presentarme. Yo dije que no al tiro: mi inglés es regular y nunca he hablado delante de un grupo.

Pero ella no me animó con discursos. Me planteó una sola pregunta: «¿Cuántas veces has dicho que no por costumbre?»

Esa semana reflexioné sobre el asunto. Consideré las dos alternativas, valoré lo que ganaba, y acepté que me arriesgaba a quedar en ridículo delante de veinte turistas. Al final me atreví a mandar el correo. Hoy me he comprometido a dar dos recorridos por semana durante todo el verano.

Le agradecí el empujón. Y ella me dijo una cosa que no he olvidado: «Yo no te di un consejo. Te quité una excusa.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
All ten of today's verbs are in that passage. Find them, and check the prepositions as you go: reflexioné sobre, me arriesgaba a, me atreví a, me he comprometido a, and le agradecí el empujón with nothing in between.

Then count the subjunctives. There are none. A whole passage about a decision, advice and a commitment, and not one que + subjunctive — because every verb here takes an infinitive or a noun. Worth knowing before tomorrow: the subjunctive is a tool for a specific job, not a mark of sophistication.

Two spirals to notice. «¿Cuántas veces has dicho que no?» is Módulo 1's present perfect with Módulo 2's irregular participle dicho — and it is the right tense, because the question is about a life up to now rather than a finished occasion. And «una cosa que no he olvidado» is Módulo 6's que doing ordinary relative work, with the indicative, which is a useful reminder that que by itself triggers nothing at all.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Valparaíso: una ciudad con ascensores en la calle

Valparaíso is built on more than forty steep hills — cerros — dropping straight into the Pacific. From the 1880s the city solved that with ascensores: funicular railways running up the hillsides as ordinary public transport, not as attractions. The oldest still in service dates from 1883. Of roughly thirty built, only a handful run today, though several have been restored, and locals genuinely commute on them.

The port was once the most important on the Pacific coast of South America — the stop for everything rounding Cape Horn. When the Panama Canal opened in 1914, that traffic went away, and Valparaíso spent most of the twentieth century as a beautiful city with a shrinking reason to exist. Its historic quarter was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2003, partly on the strength of exactly that: an unusually intact nineteenth-century port town that no economic boom ever knocked down.

Two writers are attached to these hills. Pablo Neruda kept a house here, La Sebastiana, high on Cerro Bellavista, and won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1971. He was not Chile's first: Gabriela Mistral won it in 1945, the first Latin American writer of any kind to do so. Chile therefore has two Nobel laureates in literature, which for a country of under twenty million is remarkable — and you will read Mistral's own prose on Día 8.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA TEMPLANZA (MÓDULO 1)

Módulo 1 named la templanza — temperance — and today's verbs are where it lives. Temperance is not saying no to things. It is the accurate measuring of what you can actually carry, which is why it belongs to comprometerse above all: a commitment you cannot keep does more damage than a refusal, because somebody has already built on it.

The narrator in the passage got this right by accident. She spent a week weighing before she agreed, and then promised two recorridos a week rather than every day — a promise sized to fit her actual life. Advising others well requires the same restraint in the other direction: do not press somebody into a commitment you would not size for yourself. La templanza pesa antes de prometer.
