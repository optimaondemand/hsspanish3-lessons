Lesson 8.05
★ Aconsejar en un dilema — cuando las dos opciones son buenas
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Easy advice is about a good option and a bad one. Real advice is about two goods — and it is the only kind anybody actually needs.
Every practice item so far this fortnight had an answer waiting behind it. Talk to her. Sleep during the day. Go and see the head of programme. You were choosing how to say the advice, not what it was.

Today the situation has no right answer, and the person asking has already thought about it longer than you have. Both of his options are genuinely good. He is not being weak, or indecisive, or slow — he is standing between two things that both matter, and everything you know about giving advice now has to work without the safety of knowing the answer.

This is the last full practice day of the semester. Everything after it is assessed.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to write a paragraph of advice about a dilemma in which both options are good.
2
I will be able to advise about the process — te pido que…, es importante que… — when I cannot advise about the outcome.
3
I will be able to argue the side I would not choose, in Spanish, as strongly as the side I would.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

The reason he has not answered

When somebody has been sitting on a decision for weeks, the ordinary assumption is that they lack nerve. Occasionally that is true. Far more often the delay is information: they are stuck because both options are worth having, and no amount of thinking will turn one of them bad.

That changes the adviser's job completely. You cannot supply the answer, because there is not one to supply. What you can supply is everything else — the thing they have not looked at, the question nobody has asked them, the deadline they are pretending is not there, and the permission to choose either one.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
If both options are genuinely good, what is the adviser actually for?
📚 PART 2 · EL CASO DE MATÍAS 10 min

Matías tiene diecisiete años y vive en Puerto Natales, en la Patagonia chilena, a unos cien kilómetros de las Torres del Paine. Su familia tiene una empresa pequeña de excursiones: seis camionetas, cuatro guías y una oficina en la calle principal.

En marzo le llegaron dos ofertas la misma semana.

La primera es de su propia familia: un puesto pagado de dos años como guía y aprendiz de administración. Se queda en casa, gana dinero desde el primer mes y aprende el negocio que su papá levantó en veinte años. Su papá lleva un año enfermo. No lo dice, pero necesita a alguien.

La segunda es un programa técnico en Antofagasta, en el norte: dos años de formación en instrumentación y mantenimiento de telescopios, con práctica en un observatorio del desierto. Es una carrera con futuro real en Chile. Antofagasta queda a más de tres mil kilómetros de Puerto Natales.

Matías no ha contestado ninguna de las dos. El plazo del programa técnico vence el viernes.

OpciónVentajasDesventajasEl riesgo de verdad
Quedarse en Natales Dinero desde el primer mes; aprende el negocio; está con su papá No estudia lo que le interesa; queda atado a un pueblo pequeño Quedarse por obligación y no perdonárselo después
Irse a Antofagasta Formación real; una carrera con futuro; sale de su pueblo Tres mil kilómetros; su papá enfermo; la empresa necesita otro guía Volver en dos años y encontrar la empresa cerrada

Read the fourth column again. Those are not the same as the disadvantages: a disadvantage is a cost you can see and price, and a risk is a cost you only find out about later. Neither option is safe, and no list will make one of them safe. That is what a dilemma is — and it is why «haz una lista de pros y contras» is such thin advice.

🧠 PART 3 · TRES HERRAMIENTAS 10 min
  • Recomiendo que lo intentes.recomendar — the outcome
  • hable, hables, hable, hablemos, hablenlas formas, una última vez antes del Día 10
1️⃣ NOMBRA LOS DOS BIENES ANTES DE RECOMENDAR NADA
This is move 1 from Día 3, but with something specific to say. Do not open by sympathising in general. Open by naming why the choice is hard, which tells the person you have understood the actual shape of it:

«Sé que las dos opciones son buenas, y que por eso llevas dos meses sin contestar. Eso no es indecisión; es que las dos cosas valen.»

Two indicative sentences. They cost nothing and they buy you everything, because the commonest thing Matías has heard for two months is some version of “just decide.”
2️⃣ ACONSEJA SOBRE EL PROCESO, NO SOBRE EL RESULTADO
Here is the most useful thing in this lesson. When you cannot recommend an outcome, recommend a process — and Spanish makes this easy, because the subjunctive of influence does not care whether you are pushing at a decision or at the steps before it:

«Te pido que no contestes hoy.»
«Es importante que hables con tu papá antes de contestar.»
«Sugiero que escribas las dos listas en papel, no en la cabeza.»
«Te recomiendo que llames al programa y que pidas una semana más.»

Not one of those four tells Matías which option to take. Every one of them makes the decision better, and the last one may simply dissolve the deadline that is doing half the damage. Process advice is always available, it is almost never wrong, and it is what people who are good at this actually give.
3️⃣ DI DÓNDE SE ACABA TU CONSEJO
In a dilemma the limit is not politeness; it is accuracy. You genuinely do not know, and saying so is information rather than weakness:

«No sé cuál es mejor. Lo que sí sé es que el plazo del viernes no es el criterio.»
«Es tu decisión, y yo no tengo que vivir con ella.»
«Yo en tu lugar me quedaría — pero mi vida no es la tuya, y esto es solo mi punto de vista.» — the honest way to offer your own preference is to label it as yours.

Note lo que sí sé es que…: Módulo 6's lo que, and it takes the indicative, because you are reporting what you know rather than pushing.
⚠️ DOS MANERAS DE FALLAR · y un aviso de gramática
La certeza falsa. «Es obvio que tienes que quedarte.» If it were obvious he would have answered in March. False certainty ends the conversation and teaches the person not to bring you the next one.

El abandono. «Tú decides.» — and nothing else. This looks like respect and is usually avoidance. The limit belongs at the end of a paragraph that did some work first, not instead of it.

Y la gramática: notice that «es obvio que» above is followed by tienes, the indicative. Not every impersonal expression takes the subjunctive. Es necesario que, es importante que, es mejor que all push at the world, so they take it. Es obvio que, es verdad que, está claro que all assert that the world is a certain way, so they take the indicative, exactly like any other report. The frame looks identical; what decides is whether the expression is claiming or pushing. Expect that distinction on Día 10.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · proceso o resultado
Which of these advises Matías about the process rather than the outcome?
✅ TRY IT · ¿infinitivo o que?
You are writing to Matías himself. «Es importante ___ con tu papá antes de contestar.»
✅ TRY IT · las tres son gramaticales
All three of these are correct Spanish. Which opening leaves Matías somewhere to stand?

Ahora tú — el trabajo largo del día, y hazlo en dos partes.

Parte A. Write Matías a paragraph of advice: six to eight sentences, the four moves from Día 3, at most three subjunctives, and at least one piece of process advice. Decide which option you are steering him towards, and let the paragraph steer honestly.

Parte B — this is the Honors part. Now write the strongest paragraph you can for the other option. Not a weaker version for balance: the strongest one. Find the argument you left out of Parte A, and make it.

Do both before revealing. Parte B is the one that teaches you something, and it will also tell you whether you actually believe Parte A.

PARTE A — hacia Antofagasta
Sé que las dos opciones son buenas, y sé que por eso llevas dos meses sin contestar. Eso no es indecisión; es que las dos cosas valen. Pero hay una diferencia real entre ellas: el programa técnico tiene plazo y la empresa de tu familia no. Te aconsejo que llames el lunes y que pidas una semana más. Sobre todo, es importante que hables con tu papá antes de decidir nada, porque llevas dos meses decidiendo por él sin haberle preguntado. De todos modos, la decisión es tuya, y volver a Natales en dos años sigue siendo posible.

PARTE B — hacia Natales
Entiendo que el programa te tienta, y con razón: es una carrera con futuro y no aparece todos los años. Pero hay algo que las listas no ponen. Tu papá lleva un año enfermo y no te lo ha dicho, y eso también es información. Te aconsejo que te quedes los dos años, y que uses ese tiempo para estudiar instrumentación por tu cuenta. Además, es mejor que le digas a tu papá que te quedas porque quieres y no porque te sientes obligado; esa frase vale más que los dos años. Lo digo porque he visto a gente irse y volver bien, y a gente quedarse y no perdonárselo nunca. Pero es tu vida y yo no tengo que vivir con la decisión.

Check yours, in this order:
· Move 1 first? Both models open in the indicative and both name why it is hard rather than sympathising in general.
· Ratio. Three subjunctives each (llames, pidas, hables · quedes, uses, digas) among roughly a dozen verbs. Everything else reports.
· One trigger, two clauses? Te aconsejo que llames… y que pidas… — te aconsejo is stated once.
· Process advice present? Model A has two (llames, pidas). Model B's is subtler: que le digas por qué te quedas — a step, not an outcome.
· Limit at the end, not instead of the work? Both close with it, in one sentence, after doing something first.
· And the honest question: which of your two paragraphs was harder to write? That is the one you did not believe when you started — and writing it is how you found out.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La respuesta de Matías

Tía:

He leído tu correo tres veces. Tenías razón en una cosa: yo no estaba decidiendo; estaba esperando el viernes.

Me atreví a hablar con mi papá. Me costó, porque nunca habíamos hablado de esto, y me dijo algo que no me esperaba: que la empresa aguanta dos años sin mí, y que él también fue el primero de su familia en salir de Natales.

Le pedí una semana más al programa y me la dieron. He considerado las dos cosas con calma y todavía no sé qué voy a hacer. Pero ya no es lo mismo: antes tenía dos opciones y una excusa; ahora tengo dos opciones.

Te agradezco el correo, y te agradezco más otra cosa: me planteaste la pregunta que yo no me estaba haciendo, y no me dijiste qué hacer. Me comprometo a avisarte antes del jueves.

Matías

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Count the subjunctives in Matías's reply. There are none — and that is the shape of the whole exchange. The adviser pushes; the person who has been advised reports. He narrates what he did (me atreví, le pedí, me la dieron), what he now knows (la empresa aguanta), and what he will do (me comprometo a avisarte). Not one clause tries to move anybody.

Five of Módulo 8's verbs are working here: atreverse a, considerar, agradecer, plantear, comprometerse a. Check the prepositions as you read.

Two grammar spirals worth pausing on. «Nunca habíamos hablado de esto» is Módulo 2's past perfect doing exactly what it is for: a stretch of time reaching back behind the story's own past. And «me la dieron» is a double-object pronoun from Spanish 2 — la semana and a mí, both compressed into three words.

And the line the whole lesson is aimed at: «antes tenía dos opciones y una excusa; ahora tengo dos opciones.» His aunt did not decide anything for him. She removed the thing he was hiding behind, which is very often the most any adviser can do.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
Dos decisiones chilenas de 2010

Chile spent 2010 making decisions under pressure, twice, and both are worth knowing.

On 27 February 2010 an earthquake of magnitude 8.8 struck off the Maule region — among the strongest ever instrumentally recorded anywhere on earth — followed by a tsunami along the central coast. Around five hundred people died. That is a great many, and it is also far fewer than an 8.8 has produced elsewhere; engineers generally credit decades of strict seismic building regulation, developed after Chile's own earlier disasters. Chile nonetheless tightened its building code again afterwards, which is a more honest comment on the night than any comparison: the codes worked, and they were still not finished.

Five months later, on 5 August 2010, the San José copper-gold mine near Copiapó collapsed with 33 men roughly 700 m underground. For seventeen days nobody knew whether they were alive. On 22 August a probe came back up with a note attached: «Estamos bien en el refugio los 33». All thirty-three were brought to the surface, one at a time in a capsule named Fénix, on 13 October — sixty-nine days after the collapse.

The detail that belongs in today's lesson is how the rescue was planned. There were three drilling proposals, and rather than choose the best one, the engineers drilled all three at once — Plan A, Plan B and Plan C, in parallel, at enormous cost. Plan B reached the men. It was a refusal to choose between competing goods, and it worked because it could be afforded.

Matías cannot drill three shafts. Most people cannot. Which is the honest limit of the comparison, and worth remembering the next time somebody tells you not to decide yet.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO (MÓDULO 4)

Módulo 4 named el servicio, and advice is where it is easiest to counterfeit. The counterfeit is recognisable: it sounds like helping and it is actually about being right. It arrives quickly, it is certain, it does not ask questions, and it tends to be the advice the adviser wishes somebody had given them.

Real service in a dilemma is slower and much less satisfying. You ask the question nobody has asked. You name the deadline out loud. You find the one fact that has been left out — tu papá lleva un año enfermo y no te lo ha dicho — and you hand it over even though it makes the choice harder rather than easier. Then you stop, and you let somebody else decide, and you accept that you will not be thanked for the part that took the most work. Matías's aunt did all of that, and what he thanked her for was the question. Se aconseja para servir, no para tener razón.
