Lesson 8.04
★ Suavizar o insistir — cuatro maneras de dar el mismo consejo
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You can now say the same thing four ways. All four are correct. Choosing between them is no longer grammar.
Read these four sentences. They give identical advice.

«Habla con ella.»
«Te aconsejo hablar con ella.»
«Te aconsejo que hables con ella.»
«Es mejor que hables con ella.»

Not one of them is more correct than the others, and not one is a “polite version” of another. What differs is who you would say it to, and what each one quietly claims about the relationship between you.

You have spent a semester assembling these four. Módulo 4 gave you the first, Spanish 1 the second, Módulo 7 the third, and Módulo 7's impersonal frame the fourth. Today they stop being four grammar points and become one decision. This is the most useful lesson in the module, and it is the one the Día 10 gate leans on hardest.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to give one piece of advice four ways: command, verb + infinitive, que + subjunctive, impersonal.
2
I will be able to say who each form is for, and what each implies about how close we are.
3
I will be able to mix two or three of them deliberately inside a single paragraph.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

The one that sounds “nicest” is not always the kind one

  • Te aconsejo que hables con ella.the version that leaves room

Here is the trap this lesson exists to disarm. Students who learn all four naturally assume they form a politeness ladder: the bare command is rude, the subjunctive is polite, the impersonal is politest of all. Use it, and be safe.

It is not a ladder. Say «es mejor que hables con ella» to your own brother, who has just told you something difficult and asked you point-blank what to do, and you will sound as though you are managing him — chilly, evasive, suddenly formal for no reason he can see. He asked you. The four-word answer was the warm one.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Your closest friend has wronged someone and knows it. He asks you straight out: what should I do? Which comes out of your mouth?
🧠 PART 2 · LAS CUATRO FORMAS 12 min

Same advice, four different claims about us

  • Es necesario que llegues temprano.the impersonal frame — nobody is advising
La formaQué es¿A quién?Lo que implica
Habla con ella. mandato tú (Módulo 4) Un hermano, un amigo de años, alguien que te preguntó de frente We are close enough that softening would be strange — and I am sure enough to say it in three words.
Te aconsejo hablar con ella. verbo + infinitivo Un compañero de clase, un colega, alguien a quien no conoces bien This is my recommendation, offered and set down. Neutral, slightly cooler, a little professional.
Te aconsejo que hables con ella. verbo + que + subjuntivo (Módulo 7) Un amigo cuya decisión es de verdad suya I have an opinion and you have a choice. Both of us are in the sentence.
Es mejor que hables con ella. marco impersonal Alguien a quien un consejo directo le cae mal; o cuando la razón no es tuya sino de la situación This is not about me. The situation says so, not I.
🔍 QUIÉN APARECE EN LA FRASE · the analysis worth doing once, slowly
The difference between the four is not politeness. It is which people are actually present in the sentence. Count them.

1. Habla con ella. — The listener appears only in the verb ending. Yo is nowhere. Nothing at all stands between the speaker and the instruction; that is why it is fast, and why it can be either warm or brutal depending on who says it.

2. Te aconsejo hablar con ella. — Now yo appears (aconsejo) and the listener appears as te. But the action verb is an infinitive, so nobody is conjugated as the one who does it. The advice is named rather than aimed.

3. Te aconsejo que hables con ella. — The listener appears twice: once as te, the person advised, and once inside hables, the person who would act. Yo is there too. This is the most explicit of the four about both people — which is precisely why it feels the most respectful. It names you as the one who decides.

4. Es mejor que hables con ella. — Hables still names the listener as the actor, but yo has vanished completely. Nobody is advising. The situation is.

The pattern: the more grammar you put between yourself and the instruction, the more room the other person has to move — right up to form 4, where you take yourself out of the room altogether. That last step is a real tool and it is not always a kindness. Part 3.
🧠 PART 3 · ELEGIR 11 min
❓ TRES PREGUNTAS ANTES DE ELEGIR
1. ¿Qué distancia hay entre nosotros?
Close → form 1 or 3. Distant, or unequal, or professional → form 2 or 4. A bare command travels badly upwards: from a seventeen-year-old to a manager, «hable con el cliente» is grammatically perfect and socially inverted.

2. ¿De quién es la decisión?
If it is genuinely theirs — their family, their money, their future — forms 3 and 4 say so structurally. Form 1 quietly claims the decision is obvious, which it may not be.

3. ¿Quiero responder por este consejo?
The most honest of the three. Form 1 and form 3 both put your name on it: if it goes wrong, you said it. Form 4 does not — and that is either generous (the advice stands on its own merits, not on our friendship) or evasive (advice with nobody behind it). Only you know which one you are doing.
⚠️ NO ES UNA ESCALERA DE CORTESÍA
Say this to yourself once and you will not need to relearn it: it is the mismatch that offends, not the form.

A bare command to somebody you barely know sounds like an order. The impersonal frame to your brother sounds like a manager. Both are wrong in the same way and for the same reason — the grammar claims a relationship that is not the one you have.

You met this idea in Módulo 3, from the other end: no es el mandato lo que ofende, sino el mandato solo. Same principle. The form is never the problem. What surrounds it, and who it is aimed at, is everything.
🔢 MEZCLARLAS EN UN PÁRRAFO · what fluent advice actually looks like
Nobody writes a paragraph in one register. A good one moves, and the movement carries meaning. Watch:

«Entiendo que no quieres pelear con nadie. Es importante que se lo digas pronto [4 — impersonal, so it does not sound like my opinion against hers], y te aconsejo que lo hagas en persona [3 — now I own it, and it is still your call]. Lo digo porque un mensaje se malinterpreta. Y llámame después [1 — bare command, because that part is between us and needs no ceremony].»

Three registers in five sentences, and each is doing a different job: the impersonal opens the hard subject without a fight, the subjunctive commits the adviser while leaving the choice, and the single bare command at the end is a small piece of intimacy. That last move — closing a careful paragraph with one blunt sentence — is worth stealing outright.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
There are two more moves that soften advice further, and neither needs a subjunctive at all — both run on the plain present indicative you have had since Spanish 1.

La pregunta: «¿Por qué no hablas con ella hoy?» This is advice disguised as a question, and it is the gentlest thing on this page. It offers the idea without asserting it, which lets the other person adopt it as their own. Spanish uses it constantly.

La hipótesis: «¿Y si le escribes primero?» Even lighter — it floats an option rather than recommending one, and it is the natural way to put a suggestion on the table without taking up any space.

Both are worth having in your Día 9 recording, because a spoken paragraph that uses only que + subjunctive sounds like a written one read aloud. Real speech mixes questions in.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 7 min
✅ TRY IT · ¿quién desaparece?
Which of these removes the adviser from the sentence entirely?
✅ TRY IT · el registro correcto
Your younger brother has been avoiding a conversation for a week. He finally asks you outright what he should do. What do you say?
✅ TRY IT · nombrado dos veces
In which sentence is the listener named twice — once as the person advised, and once as the person who would act?

Ahora tú: take one piece of advice you would genuinely give somebody this week. Write it all four ways. Then write one sentence in English saying which you would actually use, and to whom. The fourth step is the lesson; the first three are the exercise.

1. Deja el trabajo del sábado.
2. Te aconsejo dejar el trabajo del sábado.
3. Te aconsejo que dejes el trabajo del sábado.
4. Es mejor que dejes el trabajo del sábado.

To my cousin, who has told me twice that she is exhausted and has asked me what I think, I would use 3. She has asked, so I should have an opinion and say it — but it is her money and her job, and 3 is the only one of the four that says both of those things at once. I would use 4 with her mother listening, and 1 only if she were crying.

Check yours: form 2 keeps dejar in the infinitive with no que · forms 3 and 4 both take dejes · and the reasoning names a relationship, not a level of politeness. If your explanation says “because it is more polite,” go back to Part 3's second box.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

El mismo consejo, tres veces

Camila lleva todo el semestre pensando en cambiarse de carrera. Tres personas le dicen lo mismo — que vaya a hablar con la jefa de carrera — y ninguna lo dice igual.

Su amiga Fran, por mensaje:
—Oye, ¿cachái que llevas todo el semestre diciendo lo mismo? Habla con la jefa de carrera. Hoy, al tiro. No te va a comer. Mándale un correo ahora mismo y después me cuentas. Ya po.

Camila, escribiéndole a su profesor de primer año:
Estimado profesor Rojas: le escribo porque estoy considerando un cambio de carrera y valoro mucho su punto de vista. Ya he hablado con mi familia y tengo bastante claro lo que me interesa, pero me falta orientación sobre los plazos. ¿Tiene un ratito esta semana para conversar?

El profesor Rojas, contestando:
Camila: gracias por escribir. Entiendo que la decisión le pesa; a esta altura del año le pesa a todo el mundo. Antes de nada, es importante que hable con la jefa de carrera, porque los plazos administrativos no dependen ni de mí ni de usted. Después conversamos con calma. La espero el jueves a las cuatro.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Same advice, three registers. Name the form each person chose and why it fits.

Fran uses form 1 twice — habla, mándale — bare tú commands with a pronoun attached, straight out of Módulo 4. They are friends; the shortness is the affection.

Camila does something subtler. She is not advising, she is asking — and she picks the neutral, distanced machinery on purpose: estoy considerando, valoro su punto de vista, me falta orientación. Nothing in her message tells anyone what to do.

Profesor Rojas chooses form 4, the impersonal frame, with usted: es importante que hable. He is her teacher and could perfectly well have said «le aconsejo que hable» — but the impersonal version puts the reason where it belongs. The deadline is not his opinion. Note how he says so out loud in the next clause: no dependen ni de mí ni de usted. That is the honest use of form 4, and the opposite of hiding behind it.

One more thing to notice: the professor still opens with move 1 from yesterday, «entiendo que la decisión le pesa», and he generalises it — a esta altura del año le pesa a todo el mundo — which takes the shame out of the problem in nine words.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
El castellano de Chile: rápido, y lleno de diminutivos

Chilean Spanish has a reputation across the Spanish-speaking world for being hard to follow, and Chileans themselves make the joke before anybody else does. The reasons are real: speech is fast, final -s and final consonants get softened or dropped, and the vocabulary is full of local items other countries do not use.

Three things you will meet immediately. ¿Cachái? — from cachar, to catch on — is the Chilean tag question, roughly “you get me?”, and it appears at the end of half the sentences in an informal conversation. It is often typed without the accent in messages, but that form «cachái» is a Chilean voseo verb ending, related to «¿cómo estái?» — a whole second-person system alongside tú. You are not asked to produce it in this course; recognise it and keep writing standard tú forms. Po, from pues, tags onto the end of anything: sí po, no po, ya po. And al tiro means right now, immediately.

But the feature that belongs in this lesson is the diminutive. Chileans use -ito and -ita constantly, and usually not to mean small. Un ratito is not necessarily a short while; una preguntita is not a small question; un cafecito may be a full cup. The suffix is a softener — it takes the edge off a request, exactly the way the four forms in today's lesson take the edge off a piece of advice. Camila asks for un ratito rather than un rato, and that single syllable does the same work as choosing form 2 over form 1. Chilean Spanish softens with morphology where this lesson softens with syntax. Both are register, and both are learned.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO (MÓDULO 4)

Módulo 4 named el servicio, and today it turns into a technical decision. The four forms carry the same content — habla con ella, te aconsejo hablar con ella, te aconsejo que hables con ella, es mejor que hables con ella — and what changes is how much of you is left in the sentence. In the first, yo is nowhere. By the fourth you have taken yourself out of the room altogether.

So the choice cannot be made on your own comfort, and that is the difficulty. The bare command is the fastest to say; the impersonal frame is the easiest to stand behind. Neither of those facts is about the person in front of you. Service is choosing the form this listener can actually act on, including when that is the form that makes the conversation harder for you — which is why Part 2 gives every form an audience and refuses to rank them. Se sirve con la forma que el otro puede oír, no con la que a ti te sale fácil.
