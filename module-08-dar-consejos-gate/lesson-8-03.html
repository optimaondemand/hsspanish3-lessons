Lesson 8.03
★ El consejo en párrafo — cuando una recomendación no cabe en una frase
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A sentence can hold a recommendation. It cannot hold advice.
Módulo 7 taught you to build one correct sentence: «te recomiendo que hables con ella». Today that sentence becomes a paragraph, and three things appear that a single sentence never needed — an order, connectors, and the discipline to stop pushing.

No new forms today. Not one. Everything on this page is the subjunctive of influence you already own, arranged. The reason it still takes a whole lesson is that most students, on their first attempt at a paragraph of advice, write four flawless subjunctive sentences in a row and produce something no human being would say.

That failure is worth looking at directly, so we start with it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to build a paragraph of advice in four moves, in order.
2
I will be able to run two que clauses off a single trigger without repeating the verb.
3
I will be able to mix indicative and subjunctive inside one paragraph, and say why the mixture is the point.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

A paragraph with nothing wrong with it

Read this out loud. Every verb form in it is correct.

«Te recomiendo que hables con ella. Te recomiendo que le expliques todo. Te recomiendo que no esperes más. Te recomiendo que seas honesto.»

Four subjunctives, four correct triggers, zero errors. A grammar checker would pass it without comment.

And nobody has ever spoken like this. It is four pushes with nothing between them — no sign that the speaker heard anything, no reason given, no order, and no room left. It would land as impatience, or as a list of instructions read off a card. Correct Spanish is not the same as usable Spanish, and the gap between them is what the rest of this module is about.

What is the single biggest problem with that paragraph?
🧠 PART 2 · LOS CUATRO MOVIMIENTOS DEL CONSEJO 11 min

Four moves, in this order

You have met a shape like this before. In Módulo 3 a set of directions had four moves — distance, turn, landmark, fallback — and the one people skipped was the landmark. Advice has its own four, and the one people skip is the first.

  • Te aconsejo que hables con ella.aconsejar — the central recommendation
  • Sugiero que lo pienses bien.sugerir — softer, less committed
  • Te pido que me escuches.pedir — a request, not a recommendation
MovimientoLo que haceModoEjemplo
1. La situaciónShows you understood, before you adviseindicativoSé que llevas tres semanas pensando en esto.
2. El consejo centralOne recommendation, clearly the main onesubjuntivoTe aconsejo que hables con ella esta semana.
3. El apoyoOne or two supporting moves, same triggersubjuntivo…y que le digas exactamente lo que sientes.
4. La razón y el límiteWhy you say it — and where your advice stopsindicativoLo digo porque el silencio ya te cuesta más. Pero la decisión es tuya.

Move 1 is the one people skip, exactly as the landmark was in Módulo 3 — and it is the difference between advice and an instruction. It costs one sentence in the indicative and it buys you the right to the three that follow.

📝 LOS CUATRO MOVIMIENTOS, EN UN SOLO PÁRRAFO
«Sé que llevas tres semanas pensando en esto, y sé que desde fuera parece más simple de lo que es. Te aconsejo que hables con ella esta semana, y que le digas exactamente lo que sientes, sin ensayarlo demasiado. Lo digo porque el silencio ya te está costando más que la conversación. Pero la decisión es tuya, y yo te apoyo igual.»

Six clauses. Two subjunctives, both in the middle, both pushing at the same thing. Everything else reports.

Compare it with the paragraph in Part 1. Same advice. Same grammar. The difference is entirely arrangement — and it is the difference between something a friend says and something a form letter says.
🧠 PART 3 · UN VERBO, DOS CLÁUSULAS 10 min

You do not repeat the trigger. Spanish expects you not to.

This is the single most useful thing on today's page. One verb of influence can govern two or three que clauses. You state the trigger once and hang everything else off it:

Te aconsejo que hables con ella y que lo hagas antes del viernes.
Es importante que duermas y que no tomes café después de las seis.

Notice what repeats and what does not. The que repeats. The trigger does not. And the second verb is still subjunctive, because it is still under the same push — the trigger's reach does not end at the first clause.

In speech and in less formal writing you may drop the second que altogether:

Te aconsejo que hables con ella y le digas la verdad.

The que is optional. The subjunctive is not. «y le dices la verdad» would break the sentence in half — it would stop being part of what you are advising and become a claim that she is being told the truth.

🔄 Y AL REVÉS: DOS VERBOS, UNA CLÁUSULA
The mirror image is just as common, and you read it a fortnight ago. In Palma's «El alacrán de fray Gómez», the pedlar says:

«a su paternidad le pido y ruego que me preste esa puchuela…»

Two verbs of request — pido and ruego — sharing one que clause and one subjunctive. That is not decoration; the doubling is what makes the request sound like begging rather than asking.

So the structure flexes in both directions: one trigger, several clauses, or several triggers, one clause. What you must not do is what Part 1 did — repeat the whole trigger for every clause.
🧭 CONECTORES QUE UN PÁRRAFO DE CONSEJO NECESITA
Para ordenar: primero · luego · además · antes de nada
Para dar peso: sobre todo · lo más importante es que… · lo primero es que…
Para razonar: por eso · lo digo porque… · en cambio
Para cerrar y dejar espacio: de todos modos · en todo caso · pero la decisión es tuya

Two of these are triggers in disguise and behave exactly like es importante que: lo más importante es que hables con ella, lo primero es que duermas. Same impersonal frame, and useful because it lets you rank your advice instead of listing it.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · ¿qué movimiento falta?
«Te aconsejo que hables con ella y que lo hagas esta semana. Lo digo porque el silencio ya te cuesta más que la conversación.» Which of the four moves is missing?
✅ TRY IT · la segunda cláusula
«Te aconsejo que hables con ella y que ___ paciencia.» (tener)
✅ TRY IT · las dos son correctas
Both of these are correct Spanish. Which one tells this particular person that the conversation is theirs to have?

Ahora tú — this is the real work of the day. A friend has been offered a place on a school trip that clashes with the week of his brother's wedding. Write him a paragraph of advice: six sentences, all four moves, at most three subjunctives. Use one trigger with two que clauses hanging off it. Write it before you reveal the model; a model read first becomes a template.

Entiendo que no quieres elegir, y tienes razón: las dos cosas importan y no se repiten. Pero llevas cuatro días sin contestarle a nadie, y eso ya es una respuesta. Te aconsejo que hables primero con tu hermano y que le preguntes qué significa para él tener a su hermano ahí. Sobre todo, es importante que decidas esta semana, porque el colegio necesita los nombres el lunes. Lo digo porque he visto lo que pasa cuando el plazo decide por uno. De todos modos, es tu decisión, y ninguna de las dos te convierte en mala persona.

Check yours, in this order:
· Move 1 present? Entiendo que no quieres elegir — indicative, and it comes first.
· One trigger, two clauses? Te aconsejo que hables… y que le preguntes… — te aconsejo appears once.
· Ratio. Three subjunctives (hables, preguntes, decidas) out of about ten verbs. If yours is above half, you have written Part 1's paragraph again.
· Move 4 both halves? A reason (lo digo porque…) and a limit (es tu decisión).
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Consejo de un técnico del desierto

—Entiendo que quieres quedarte las cuatro noches. Casi todos los estudiantes llegan con esa idea y es razonable. Pero llevo once años aquí arriba y te voy a decir lo que veo.
—Dígame.
—Te aconsejo que duermas de día desde hoy mismo, y que no intentes aguantar las dos primeras noches a base de café. La altura y el turno de noche juntos no perdonan. Además, te pido una cosa que parece tontería: que tomes agua cada hora, con sed o sin ella.
—¿Y la cámara?
—Sugiero que la dejes en la pieza la primera noche. Sube al mirador sin nada y mira. La Vía Láctea aquí no se parece a ninguna fotografía, y si llegas mirando por una pantalla, te pierdes justo eso. La cámara la vas a tener las otras tres noches.
—¿Tan distinto es?
—El Atacama es el desierto no polar más seco del planeta. Hay estaciones donde no se registra lluvia durante años. Por eso están aquí los telescopios: cielo despejado casi todas las noches y aire sin humedad. No es suerte, es geografía.
—Ya.
—Lo digo porque he visto a mucha gente subir una sola vez en su vida. Tú decides. Pero si yo subo una vez, subo mirando.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find the four moves. Move 1 is the first line — entiendo que quieres, es razonable, all indicative, and it concedes something before it advises. Move 4 is the last — a reason and then «tú decides», which is the limit stated in two words.

Then count. There are five subjunctives (duermas, intentes, tomes, dejes — and que la dejes under sugiero) in a passage of about twenty verbs. Everything else reports: llevo, no perdonan, no se parece, es, están.

Two structures to spot. «Te aconsejo que duermas… y que no intentes…» — one trigger, two clauses, exactly Part 3. And «te pido una cosa…: que tomes agua» — the trigger announced, then the clause after a colon, the same device you met in 8.01.

One usage note worth stealing: la pieza is what a Chilean calls a bedroom. In most of Latin America you would hear el cuarto or la habitación; in Chile, la pieza.
🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
El desierto que se convirtió en observatorio

The Atacama is the driest non-polar desert on earth. Some weather stations in it have gone years without recording measurable rainfall, and parts of the interior are so dry that organic material does not rot. It is also high, thin-aired and almost cloudless — and those four facts together have made northern Chile the most important place on the planet for looking at the sky.

Two sites are worth knowing by name. Paranal, at about 2,600 m, carries the European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope. And on the Chajnantor plateau, at roughly 5,000 m — high enough that staff work on oxygen and are not permitted to stay long — stands ALMA, an array of 66 antennas that began full science operations in 2013. Chile hosts a very large share of the world's professional telescope capacity, and that share keeps growing as new instruments are built.

The consequence Chileans notice is a strange one: the darkness is an economic resource. Chile regulates outdoor lighting in the northern regions to keep the sky usable, which makes it one of very few countries where the absence of something is written into environmental rules. There is also a long-standing agreement in Chilean astronomy that a share of observing time on these telescopes goes to Chilean researchers — the price of the sky, paid in access.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA (MÓDULO 3)

Módulo 3 named la cortesía, and the four movements are courtesy turned into a structure. Notice what movement 2 does on its own: te aconsejo que hables con ella esta semana, with nothing around it, arrives as a verdict, and the only replies available are yes and no.

The other two movements give the listener somewhere to stand. Movement 1 restates the situation, so they know you heard it before you spoke — sé que llevas tres semanas pensando en esto. Movement 4 gives the reason and then names its own limit: lo digo porque el silencio ya te cuesta más. Pero la decisión es tuya. A reason can be disagreed with, which means the other person can argue with your reason instead of with you, and the limit hands the choice back on purpose. Both exist for the benefit of the person listening, which is what courtesy is — imagining the sentence from the other side before you send it. It is not softness; movements 2 and 3 stay firm the whole way through.

The technician on Chajnantor does the same thing when he says llevo once años aquí arriba before he tells you anything about the altitude. He is offering you grounds, not credentials. La cortesía no adorna el consejo; deja por dónde responder.
