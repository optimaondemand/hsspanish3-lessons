✏️ LOS TRES QUE SE ESCRIBEN · seven minutes, now
Reopen 8.01 and 8.02, play every 🔊 with your eyes closed, and say the meaning aloud before you look. Then write these three from memory:

1. la orientación — the only word in this module's twenty that carries a tilde, and it carries it for the ordinary -ción reason you banked in Módulo 4. Orientacion is not a word.
2. el compromiso — no tilde, and worth writing out because the verb comprometerse is in the same vocabulary set. The noun is not compromizo and not comprometimiento.
3. atreverse a — the preposition is part of the word. Atreverse alone will be marked wrong, exactly as insistir without en would be. Spanish verbs that carry a fixed preposition carry it into the dictionary entry.

If typing tildes is still slow: Cómo escribir las tildes y la ñ.

Two of the fifteen items play a grammar clip rather than a word, and ask you to hear the difference between an indicative and a subjunctive ending. Hablas and hables differ by one vowel and nothing else, so listen for the vowel and not for the sense.

El tema: Una persona concreta que conoces tiene que tomar una decisión difícil. Escríbele un párrafo de consejos.

You wrote this yesterday in 8.06 Part 5. Do not start over. Open the draft, run yesterday's checklist, fix what it catches, and submit that. A revised paragraph outscores a fresh one written at speed almost every time — the rubric rewards shape, and shape is what revision produces.

✅ PART 5 · ANTES DE ENTREGAR 5 min

Read your paragraph out loud and do one targeted pass: stop at every que and ask what verb is in front of it. If it is a verb of wanting or influencing, the next verb is a subjunctive. If it is ser, saber, decir reporting a fact, or a noun, it is not. That single check catches most of what this task loses points on.

One thing to catch before you submit. If any sentence has come out as «le recomendé que…» or «le pedí que…» — a trigger verb in the past — rewrite it. Spanish handles a past-tense trigger with a form this course has not taught you, and there is no legal way around it: what follows que would have to change too. Keep your triggers in the present (le recomiendo que…) or the present perfect (ya le he dicho…). And if you need to mention advice somebody already gave, use a noun or an infinitive instead of a que clause: me pidió ayuda, me recomendó dos días de espera. You are giving advice today, not reporting it, so this should cost you nothing.

Then submit both items. The Vocabulary Check has a single attempt, so do Part 3's seven minutes of listening first, not after.

One note on what is coming, so it does not surprise you on Día 10: this module's Grammar Quiz is the semester gate. It needs 16 out of 20, it is cumulative, and the next module stays locked until you reach that score. You have two attempts and there is nothing on it you have not been taught — but Día 10 is not the day to meet that fact for the first time.

🌎 CULTURA · CHILE
¿Cachai?

On a listening day, the honest disclosure first: the clips in this course are recorded in a neutral Latin-American voice, and Chilean Spanish does not sound like that. Other Spanish speakers routinely name it the hardest variety in the Americas to follow, and they are not being unkind — three things happen at once.

It is fast, and the final -s softens or vanishes. Más o menos comes out closer to máh o menoh, and los amigos loses both. The diminutive does an enormous amount of work — un ratito, una preguntita, al tirito — and it is rarely about size. It softens: a preguntita is a question you are apologising for asking. And Chile keeps verb endings the rest of the region dropped: ¿cachai? — “got it?” — carries the -ái ending of an old second-person form that survives in Chilean speech and almost nowhere else in casual use.

Two more you will hear in the first hour: al tiro (right away, immediately) and po, a worn-down pues tacked onto the end of everything — sí po, no po, ya po. None of this is sloppy Spanish. It is a national accent with its own grammar, and it is why Chileans abroad are often asked to repeat themselves and Chileans at home understand each other perfectly.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA ATENCIÓN (MÓDULO 6)

Módulo 6 spent two weeks on la atención in front of a painting — looking long enough that the picture began to give something back. Today asks for it twice, in two completely different registers.

The literal one first: the Vocabulary Check is a listening test with a single attempt, and two of its items turn on hearing one unstressed vowel at the end of a word. That is attention with nothing clever about it — eyes closed, clip played twice, meaning said aloud before you look.

The second one is what revision is made of. You are not writing a paragraph today; you are re-reading one that already felt finished, against a checklist whose answers you think you know. Everything that separates the two paragraphs in 8.06 is visible only to somebody looking on purpose. And it is the same faculty either way: advising somebody well is mostly noticing — their situation before you speak about it, and your own sentences before they reach them. Corregir es volver a mirar, y casi nunca se mira bien la primera vez.
