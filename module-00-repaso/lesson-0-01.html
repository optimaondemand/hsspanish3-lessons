★ Bienvenidos a Español 3 Honores
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You can already tell a story in Spanish. This year you learn to say what you think about it.
Bienvenidos. In Spanish 2 you finished the past-tense system — you can narrate. Español 3 Honores takes the next step: opinar, aconsejar, dudar, esperar — opinion, advice, doubt, and hope. That work needs a new mood (the subjunctive), and we build toward it all semester.

This first module is a repaso, but not a re-teach. We reactivate your narration in the past so it is fast and automatic, because everything new this year gets built on top of it. Today: the words that tell your listener when something happened.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to place events in time using ten expressions of time and sequence.
2
I will be able to answer ¿qué hiciste? using fui, hice and estuve without stopping to think.
3
I will be able to explain what changes in Spanish 3 — and why the course moves from narrating to opining.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Say one true sentence about your summer

Before you read anything else, say this out loud and finish it honestly: —El verano pasado yo… Do not translate word by word. Whatever came out is your Spanish waking up. Hold on to how that felt, because by Day 10 it should come out twice as fast.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Be honest — how did that sentence come out?
📚 PART 2 · ¿CUÁNDO PASÓ? 12 min

The words that put a story in order

A story is not just verbs. What makes narration sound native is the scaffolding — the words that tell your listener when things happened and in what order. Click the 🔊, repeat aloud, and notice that most of these you already recognise. Recognising is not the goal this week; producing is.

  • el verano pasadolast summer
  • la semana pasadalast week
  • hace dos mesestwo months ago
  • anochelast night
  • primerofirst
  • luegothen, next
  • después deafter
  • mientraswhile
  • de repentesuddenly
  • al finalin the end, finally
✏️ ESCRIBIR CON TILDES · you will be graded on this
Start the year with this settled: in this course accents are graded, including in typed short answers. anecdota will be marked wrong, because it is not a Spanish word — it is la anécdota.

And the whole of this module rests on one accent. «Yo paso» is I pass, right now. «Él pasó» is he passed, over and done. Same five letters; the tilde is the entire difference between present and preterite, and you are about to spend two weeks narrating in the past.

So get fast at typing ó í ñ ¿ ¡ now rather than in the middle of a quiz: Cómo escribir las tildes y la ñ — Windows, Mac, phone and Chromebook, with the shortcuts worth memorising.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Two of these will matter enormously on Day 5. Mientras almost always pulls the imperfect (a scene in progress), and de repente almost always pulls the preterite (a single interrupting event). Say them as a pair now — mientras… de repente… — and you have pre-loaded the hardest contrast in the module.
🧠 PART 3 · REACTIVACIÓN RÁPIDA 8 min

Three verbs that answer almost any ¿qué hiciste?

You are not learning these today — you are switching them back on. Ir, hacer and estar in the preterite carry an enormous share of real conversation about the past. Read the yo and tú rows aloud until they sound like one word rather than a conjugation.

Subjectir (to go)hacer (to do/make)estar (to be)
yofuihiceestuve
túfuistehicisteestuviste
usted / él / ellafuehizoestuvo
nosotros/asfuimoshicimosestuvimos
ustedes / ellos / ellasfueronhicieronestuvieron

Two things worth re-noticing: none of these carries an accent in the yo form (fui, not fuí), and hacer spells its él form with z — hizo — to keep the sound. Both are classic Spanish 2 slips; clear them now and they stop costing you points.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 8 min
✅ TRY IT · choose the time expression
You want to say the event interrupted something already in progress: Yo leía tranquilamente y ___ sonó el teléfono.
✅ TRY IT · the yo form
«El verano pasado ___ a Puerto Rico con mi familia.»
✅ TRY IT · spelling that carries sound
«Mi hermano ___ la cena anoche.» (My brother made dinner last night.)

Ahora tú: describe your summer in three sentences, and make each one start with a different time expression from Part 2. Say it out loud before you reveal the model.

El verano pasado fui a la playa con mis primos. Luego hice un curso de natación en junio. Al final estuve en casa casi todo agosto.

Notice what the model does not do: it never says «y después y después y después». Three different markers — el verano pasado / luego / al final — is what separates Honors narration from a list.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

El primer día

Anoche no dormí bien. Primero revisé mi horario tres veces; luego busqué mis cuadernos y no los encontré. Mientras mi madre preparaba el desayuno, yo practicaba mi presentación en voz baja. De repente ella me preguntó: «¿Con quién hablas?». Al final me reí y le expliqué todo. Hoy estoy nervioso, pero listo.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Read it once more and count the five time markers. Then notice something you are not asked to explain yet: revisé, busqué, preguntó and me reí are single completed events, while preparaba and practicaba are scenes running in the background. Two different past tenses, doing two different jobs. Day 5 is entirely about that.
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
De los mapas a las voces

In Spanish 1 and 2 you toured the Spanish-speaking world as geography — twenty-one countries, capitals, foods, festivals. Español 3 changes the question. This year each module still features a country, but what you meet there is a theme and a voice: how a place talks about health, its cities, its environment, its art, its arguments. You will read what people from those countries actually wrote, not summaries of them.

That shift is the whole reason this course adds a reading strand. Beginning on Day 8 of every module, you read one real text — and the first one is by a Cuban writer who published a magazine for children in New York in 1889.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA DILIGENCIA

This module's virtue is la diligencia — diligence: doing the unglamorous work carefully because it is the work in front of you. A repaso module is exactly where diligence shows. Nothing here is new, so it is easy to skim; and skimming is precisely what makes the subjunctive hard in eight weeks' time. The students who find Module 7 manageable will be the ones who took Module 0 seriously. La diligencia no es entusiasmo — es constancia.
