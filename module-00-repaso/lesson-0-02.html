★ Las palabras que arman una historia
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A story is not a list of verbs. It has a shape.
Yesterday you put events in time. Today you learn the words that give a story shape — the ones that set a scene, spring a surprise, and land a point. No new grammar today: the tenses you already own are enough. What we add is the vocabulary of narration itself, and the three-part structure every anecdote in every language quietly follows.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use ten narration expressions, including resulta que and sin embargo.
2
I will be able to identify the three parts of an anecdote: situación, complicación, resolución.
3
I will be able to use acabar de + infinitive to say what had just happened.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

What makes a story worth telling?

Think of the last funny thing that happened to you. You did not narrate it in order from waking up. You started somewhere, something went wrong, and you landed a punchline. That instinct is already in you — in English. Today we give it Spanish words.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
When you tell a story to a friend, where do you usually start?
📚 PART 2 · EL VOCABULARIO DE LA NARRACIÓN 12 min

Ten expressions that do real work

Click the 🔊 and repeat each one aloud. Several of these are phrases, not single words — learn them whole, the way a speaker actually reaches for them.

  • ocurrirto happen, to occur
  • lo que pasówhat happened
  • darse cuenta deto realize
  • acabar deto have just (done something)
  • al principioat first, in the beginning
  • resulta queit turns out that
  • sin embargohowever, nevertheless
  • el recuerdothe memory
  • la anécdotathe anecdote, the story
  • menos mal quethank goodness that
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Darse cuenta de is reflexive and keeps its de: me di cuenta de que… — never «me di cuenta que» in careful writing, and never realicé, which means I carried out, not I realized. That false friend costs points on nearly every Spanish 3 writing task; retire it today.
🧠 PART 3 · LA FORMA DE UNA ANÉCDOTA 9 min

Three parts, and the tense each one prefers

This is not new grammar — it is a map of grammar you already have. Every anecdote has three moves, and each move quietly favours one past tense. Learn the map now and Day 5 will feel like recognition rather than instruction.

PartWhat it doesTense it prefersSignal words
SituaciónSets the scene: where, when, what was going onimperfectoal principio, mientras, siempre
ComplicaciónThe single event that breaks the scenepretéritode repente, entonces, y luego
ResoluciónHow it ended, and what you make of itpretérito + commental final, menos mal que, resulta que

And one structure worth having today: acabar de + infinitivo = to have just done. Acababa de salir cuando empezó a llover. (I had just left when it started to rain.) Notice acababa is imperfect — background — while empezó is preterite — the event. The map is already working.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 8 min
✅ TRY IT · which connector?
«Pensábamos que el examen era el viernes. ___ , era el jueves.»
✅ TRY IT · the false friend
How do you say “I realized that I had no money”?
✅ TRY IT · name the part
«Era una tarde de julio y no había nadie en la calle.» Which part of the anecdote is this?

Ahora tú: tell a two-sentence anecdote with a clear situación and complicación. Say it aloud, then compare.

Al principio todo iba bien: yo estudiaba en la biblioteca y hacía mucho silencio. De repente sonó la alarma de incendios y todos salimos corriendo.

Count the tenses: iba, estudiaba, hacía — three imperfects building a scene. Then sonó, salimos — two preterites breaking it. You did that without being taught it today.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La llave

Al principio no me preocupé. Acababa de cerrar la puerta y la mochila estaba en el suelo, así que busqué la llave con calma. Sin embargo, después de cinco minutos me di cuenta de que no estaba. De repente recordé el autobús: la dejé en el asiento. Resulta que una señora la encontró y la entregó al conductor. Menos mal que alguien fue más responsable que yo. Es un recuerdo incómodo, pero es una buena anécdota.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find the three parts. Where does the situación end and the complicación begin? Then find all six expressions from Part 2 — every one is in there, doing the job it was listed for.
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
La sobremesa

In much of the Spanish-speaking world there is a word for the part of a meal after the eating stops and nobody leaves the table: la sobremesa. It can run twenty minutes or two hours, and what happens in it is almost entirely storytelling — anecdotes, arguments, family history retold for the hundredth time.

English has no single word for this, which is part of why the skill you are building this module matters beyond a grade. Narrating well in Spanish is not a school exercise; in a culture with a named institution for after-dinner talk, it is how you take part.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

The first of the seven arrives with today's reading. «La llave» ends on a line most people would leave out — es un recuerdo incómodo, pero es una buena anécdota — and the narrator admits plainly that somebody else behaved more responsibly. That is honesty (la honestidad), and notice it is also what makes the story worth telling: an anecdote in which the teller comes out looking perfect has no complicación and therefore no shape. When you choose your own material this module, choose the true version rather than the flattering one. La anécdota honesta se cuenta mejor que la conveniente.
