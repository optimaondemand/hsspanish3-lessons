📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Correct sentences are not yet a paragraph. Today you build the paragraph.
Yesterday you learned to choose a tense one sentence at a time. That is not how narration actually works — in real writing you hold a whole scene in view and the tenses arrange themselves around it.

This is the Honors standard for the rest of the year: connected-paragraph length, in every written and spoken task. Today you build one deliberately, with the tools you have. Tomorrow, graded work opens.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

2
I will be able to vary my connectors instead of repeating y luego.
3
I will be able to read a longer authentic-style narrative and follow its tense logic.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

The most common weakness in Spanish 3 writing

It is not verb errors. It is this: Fui a la tienda. Luego compré pan. Luego volví a casa. Luego comí. Every verb correct, every sentence correct — and it reads like a shopping list, because every sentence has the same length, the same shape, and the same connector.

An Honors paragraph varies. Some sentences are long and set a scene; one is short and lands the event.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Look honestly at your own Spanish writing so far. Which is closest?
🧠 PART 2 · LA ARQUITECTURA DEL PÁRRAFO 10 min

A shape you can reuse all year

SentenceJobTenseUseful opener
1–2Set time, place, atmosphereimperfectoEra… / Hacía… / Al principio…
3What you were doingimperfectoMientras…
4The event — keep it shortpretéritoDe repente…
5–6What followedpretéritoEntonces… / Después…
7How it endedpretéritoAl final… / Menos mal que…
8Your comment on itpresent or imperfectAhora sé que… / Todavía recuerdo…

Sentence 4 is the hinge. Make it the shortest sentence in the paragraph — a short sentence after three long ones lands like a drumbeat, and it is the cheapest stylistic trick in any language.

⭐ LA REGLA DE LOS CONECTORES
Do not use the same connector twice in one paragraph. You have plenty: al principio · mientras · de repente · entonces · después de · sin embargo · resulta que · menos mal que · al final. Nine options, and an eight-sentence paragraph needs at most five.
🧠 PART 3 · MODELO ANOTADO 10 min

The shape, filled in

El examen que no era

1 Era lunes por la mañana y yo estaba más nervioso de lo normal. 2 Hacía frío en el pasillo y todos mis compañeros repasaban sus apuntes en silencio. 3 Mientras yo buscaba un bolígrafo en la mochila, pensaba en todas las fechas que no recordaba. 4 De repente la profesora abrió la puerta y se rió. 5 Entonces nos explicó que el examen era el miércoles, no el lunes. 6 Después de un segundo de silencio, todos empezamos a hablar a la vez. 7 Al final salimos al patio y estudiamos juntos una hora. 8 Todavía recuerdo ese lunes como el mejor día de repaso del semestre.

Count what the model does: sentences 1–3 are long and entirely imperfect — no plot yet. Sentence 4 is ten words and carries two preterites. Sentences 5–7 advance and close. Sentence 8 steps outside the story to comment. Five different connectors, none repeated.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · diagnose the weakness
«Fui al parque. Luego jugué al fútbol. Luego comí un helado. Luego volví a casa.» What is the main problem?
✅ TRY IT · which sentence is the hinge?
In a paragraph following Part 2's shape, which sentence should be the shortest?
✅ TRY IT · tense inside reported speech
«Nos explicó que el examen ___ el miércoles.» (the exam WAS on Wednesday)

Ahora tú — the real practice for tomorrow's graded task. Write a 6–8 sentence narrative paragraph about something that went wrong, following Part 2's shape. Check three things before you reveal: no connector repeated, sentence 4 is the shortest, and sentences 1–3 contain no preterite.

Before you call it finished:
• Sentences 1–3: imperfect only. Any preterite there means you started the plot too early.
• Sentence 4: the event, and the shortest line you wrote.
• At least four different connectors, none used twice.
• Every -er/-ir imperfect has its accent: vivía, comía, hacía.
• Check saber/conocer/querer/poder — did you mean the state or the moment?
• Final sentence steps outside the story and comments on it.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La carta de mi bisabuela

Mi bisabuela nació en un pueblo de Galicia, en el norte de España, y salió de allí a los diecinueve años. En casa guardábamos una carta que ella escribió desde el barco. En la carta contaba que el mar estaba tranquilo, que había gente de cuatro países en su camarote y que nadie hablaba el mismo español. Decía que tenía miedo, pero que no quería volver.

Cuando llegó a Buenos Aires, no conocía a nadie. Trabajó once años en una panadería y aprendió a leer de noche. Un día conoció a mi bisabuelo en la cola del pan. Mi madre todavía guarda esa carta en una caja de metal, y cada vez que la leemos nos damos cuenta de que la letra era de alguien que estaba aprendiendo a escribir.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Two things to notice. First, the tense logic: preterites carry her life's events (nació, salió, llegó, trabajó, aprendió, conoció) while imperfects hold what the letter described (estaba, había, hablaba, tenía). Second, the pair you learned yesterday appears again on purpose: no conocía a nadie (she knew no one) and then conoció a mi bisabuelo (she met him). Same verb, two tenses, two meanings.
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
«Nadie hablaba el mismo español»

That line from the reading is historically exact. Between roughly 1880 and 1930, millions of Europeans crossed to the Americas, and Argentina received more immigrants per head of population than almost anywhere on earth — above all Spaniards and Italians. Buenos Aires absorbed so many that the city's Spanish changed permanently: the intonation of porteño speech, and words like laburo (work, from Italian lavoro), come from that arrival.

So the Spanish you are learning is not one thing that spread outward unchanged. It was remade by the people who carried it — which is why this course visits a different country each module rather than teaching “Spanish” as if it had one home.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA DILIGENCIA

A woman who worked eleven years in a bakery and taught herself to read at night is the module's virtue made concrete. La diligencia is not intensity — it is showing up at the same task after the novelty is gone. Tomorrow graded work opens; that is precisely where this virtue gets tested rather than admired. Se aprende de noche, poco a poco, sin público.
