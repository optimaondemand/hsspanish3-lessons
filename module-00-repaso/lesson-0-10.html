⭐ CÓMO USAR DOS INTENTOS · the part students get wrong
Two attempts is not permission to guess your way through the first one. Take attempt 1 seriously — its value is telling you precisely which of the eight areas above is weak. Then go back and reread that lesson before attempt 2. Students who take both attempts back to back usually score nearly the same twice, having learned nothing in between; students who review in the middle gain several points.

➡️ PART 5 · LO QUE VIENE 8 min

Why Module 0 had to come first

Module 1 introduces the present perfect: he hablado, has comido, hemos vivido — “I have spoken.” It is built from haber plus a past participle, and it answers a question the preterite cannot: not what happened, but what has happened so far.

You needed this module first for a specific reason. Spanish makes you choose among three ways to talk about the past — hablé, hablaba, he hablado — and each one means something different. If the first two were still uncertain, adding a third would produce guesswork rather than precision.

And this is the shape of the whole year: perfect tenses, then commands, then por/para, then relative pronouns, and from Module 7 the subjunctive — a whole new mood for saying what you want, doubt, and hope. Each layer sits on the one before. Empezamos con lo que pasó; terminamos con lo que esperamos.

🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Un continente de matices

One last thing about the tense you just mastered — it is not used identically everywhere. In much of Spain, he comido (present perfect) covers what a Mexican or Argentine speaker would render as comí (preterite): ¿Has comido? in Madrid, ¿Comiste? in Mexico City, for the same lunch on the same afternoon.

Neither is wrong. This course teaches the Latin-American preference because that is the Spanish most students here will actually hear and use — but knowing the split matters, because it means that when you meet a Spaniard using the perfect where you expected the preterite, they are not making a mistake. They are speaking their own Spanish. Recognising that difference instead of correcting it is a real part of Intermediate proficiency.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

All seven came through these ten days in order: honesty in an uncomfortable anecdote, self-government in accents nobody checks, courtesy toward a listener who needs the scene before the events, perseverance in a great-grandmother who learned to read at night, responsibility when the graded work opened, service in a magazine written for the children of América, and courage in pressing record. None of them is a Spanish topic, and together they decided how well this module went for you. Modules 1 through 7 now take one each. Siete virtudes, y ninguna se practica sola.
