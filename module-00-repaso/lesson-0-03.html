Lesson 0.03
★ El pretérito — la máquina de los hechos
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
One job: a completed event. One tense for it.
Today is machinery. The pretérito reports events that happened and finished — the complicación and resolución of yesterday's map. You built this in Spanish 2; today we rebuild it to the point of automaticity, because from Module 1 onward it becomes the base you conjugate other things from. No new vocabulary today — all of your attention goes on form.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate regular -ar, -er and -ir verbs in the preterite, with correct accents.
2
I will be able to produce the strong (irregular) preterites — tuve, pude, puse, vine, dije, traje — and explain why they carry no accents.
3
I will be able to spell yo forms like busqué, llegué and empecé correctly.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Where the accent falls changes who is speaking

Say these two aloud and hear the difference: hablo (I speak, now) versus habló (he spoke, finished). Same five letters. The accent is not decoration — it is the entire tense, and in writing it is the only thing distinguishing present from past.

🧠 PART 2 · LAS FORMAS REGULARES 11 min

Two sets of endings, not three

Good news you may have forgotten: in the preterite, -er and -ir verbs share one set of endings. So there are only two patterns to hold.

Subject-ar · hablar-er · comer-ir · vivir
yohablécomíviví
túhablastecomisteviviste
usted / él / ellahablócomióvivió
nosotros/ashablamoscomimosvivimos
ustedes / ellos / ellashablaroncomieronvivieron

The four accented forms are the whole battle: hablé, habló, comí, comió. Every accent sits on the final syllable. Note also that hablamos and vivimos are identical in the present and the preterite — context alone tells you which, exactly as in English “we cut”.

⭐ ORTOGRAFÍA · the yo forms that change spelling
Three -ar families change spelling in yo only, to protect the sound: buscar → busqué (c→qu), llegar → llegué (g→gu), empezar → empecé (z→c). The verb has not become irregular — the sound stayed the same and the spelling moved to keep up.
🧠 PART 3 · LOS PRETÉRITOS FUERTES 10 min

A closed list, and a rule that makes it easy

These are called fuertes (strong) because the stress falls on the stem, not the ending. That single fact explains why they carry no written accents at all — the accent you would expect on yo and él simply is not there.

VerbNew stemyoél / ella
tenertuv-tuvetuvo
poderpud-pudepudo
ponerpus-pusepuso
sabersup-supesupo
venirvin-vinevino
quererquis-quisequiso
decirdij-dijedijo
traertraj-trajetrajo

All eight take the same endings: -e, -iste, -o, -imos, -ieron. Two footnotes worth having: stems ending in j (dij-, traj-) drop the i in the ellos form — dijeron, not «dijieron». And saber in the preterite means found out: Supe la verdad ayer = I found out the truth yesterday.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · the accent decides the subject
«Ayer mi hermana ___ con el profesor.» (spoke)
✅ TRY IT · spelling that protects sound
«Anoche yo ___ mis llaves por toda la casa.» (buscar)
✅ TRY IT · strong preterite, no accent
«Yo no ___ ir a la fiesta porque tenía que trabajar.» (poder)
✅ TRY IT · the j-stem rule
«Mis primos ___ que llegarían tarde.» (decir)

Ahora tú: say four things you did yesterday, and make at least two of them use strong preterites from Part 3.

Ayer tuve un examen de matemáticas. Puse el despertador a las seis y llegué temprano. No pude terminar la última pregunta, pero le dije al profesor que estudié mucho.

Four strong preterites (tuve, puse, pude, dije), one spelling-change yo (llegué), one regular (estudié). Not one written accent on the strong forms.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

El concierto

El sábado tuvimos suerte. Mi hermano consiguió dos entradas y me dijo que fuéramos temprano. Llegamos a las siete y nos pusimos cerca del escenario. La banda empezó tarde, pero cuando salieron, todo el mundo se levantó. Yo no pude escuchar bien al final porque grité demasiado. Al salir supimos que fue su último concierto de la gira.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Every bolded verb is a preterite. Sort them into two piles: strong stems with no accent (tuvimos, dijo, nos pusimos, pude, supimos) and regular forms (llegamos, empezó, salieron, se levantó, grité). Notice the whole paragraph is events — no scene-painting at all. That is what a pure preterite text feels like, and tomorrow you will see its opposite.
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Un idioma con academia

The reason busqué is spelled with qu and not c is not an accident of history — it is a decision, published. Spanish is governed collaboratively by the Real Academia Española together with twenty-two sister academies across the Americas, Africa and Asia, which agree on spelling as a group.

That is unusual. English has no such body; its spelling was settled by printers and dictionaries. When you write empecé correctly you are following a rule that Mexico, Argentina, Colombia and Spain all voted on — one more sign that this language belongs to many countries rather than one.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA DILIGENCIA

Today's diligence is unglamorous and decisive: writing the accent every single time, including in rough drafts and notes nobody will read. Accents you skip in practice are accents you will skip on the Grammar Quiz. Se escribe bien cuando nadie mira, o no se escribe bien.
