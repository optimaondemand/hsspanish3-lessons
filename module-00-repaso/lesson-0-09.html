Lesson 0.09
★ Tarea oral — narrar en voz alta
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Writing lets you stop and think. Speaking does not. That is why it is worth the most.
Today you record the Tarea oral (20 pts) — the single most heavily weighted component of this course at 20% of your semester grade. You already have the content: the memory you chose yesterday and the paragraph shape you built on Day 6.

What today adds is the part that only speaking teaches — holding a narrative together in real time, at connected-paragraph length, without a script to read from.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to narrate a personal memory aloud for 60–90 seconds using both past tenses.
2
I will be able to speak from a six-word outline instead of reading a script.
3
I will be able to pronounce preterite and imperfect endings so a listener can hear which tense I chose.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Do not write it out. Really.

The instinct is to write the whole thing and read it aloud. Resist it — a read script always sounds read, it flattens your intonation, and it teaches you nothing you can use in a conversation. Worse, it makes the task harder: you end up trying to remember wording instead of remembering a story, and one forgotten phrase derails the whole recording.

You will build a six-word outline in Part 3 and speak from that. It feels riskier and produces a far better result.

🎤 PART 2 · LA TAREA 9 min

The prompt and how it is scored

El tema: Cuenta un recuerdo importante — algo que querías hacer o ver antes que nada, y qué pasó.

Narrate an important memory: something you wanted to do or see before anything else, and what happened. This is Martí's traveller, transposed to your own life.

RequirementDetail
Length60–90 seconds of connected speech (Honors standard)
LanguageSpanish only — no English words filled in
Both tensesA scene in the imperfect and events in the preterite, each doing its own job
DeliverySpoken from an outline. Do not read a script.
AttemptsRecord as many times as you like; submit your best take
Scored on · 20 ptsWhat earns full marks
Aspect accuracyScene in imperfect, events in preterite — used deliberately, not by luck
Narrative shapeA recognisable situación, complicación, resolución
RangeVaried connectors; vocabulary from this module
FluencyContinuous speech. Pauses are fine; long silences and restarts are not
ComprehensibilityA patient listener follows you without needing a repeat

Note what is not on that list: a native accent. Comprehensibility is scored; sounding Mexican is not.

🧠 PART 3 · TU GUION DE SEIS PALABRAS 9 min

Six words, six beats

Write one word per line — a noun or verb, no sentences. Six words is enough to hold a 90-second narrative and few enough that you cannot read it aloud.

BeatYour wordExample (a trip to see the ocean)Tense you will use
1. Where / when agostoimperfecto
2. The scene cocheimperfecto
3. What you wanted marimperfecto (quería)
4. The event lluviapretérito
5. What followed esperamospretérito
6. Now recuerdopresente
⭐ ENSAYA TRES VECES · before you record
Say the whole narrative aloud three times from your six words. It will come out differently each time — that is the point, and it is proof you are speaking rather than reciting. If run three is not noticeably smoother than run one, do a fourth. Nobody's first take is their best.
🔊 PART 4 · PRONUNCIACIÓN DEL TIEMPO VERBAL 11 min

Your listener has to hear which tense you chose

In writing, the accent marks the tense. In speech, stress does — and the difference between hablo and habló is entirely which syllable you lean on. Say each pair aloud, exaggerating the stressed syllable more than feels natural:

  • HA·blo / ha·BLÓI speak / he spoke
  • lle·GUÉ / lle·GA·baI arrived / I was arriving
  • co·MÍ / co·MÍ·aI ate / I used to eat
  • SU·pe / sa·BÍ·aI found out / I knew

The third pair is the one to drill hardest: comí and comía differ by a single unstressed vowel at the end, and swallowing it makes your aspect choice invisible to the listener. Finish the word.

✅ TRY IT · where does the stress go?
You want your listener to hear “he spoke” rather than “I speak”. Which syllable do you lean on?
✅ TRY IT · what the rubric actually rewards
Two recordings: one has a strong accent but perfect aspect choice; the other sounds smoother but uses the preterite throughout. Which scores higher?
⏺ PART 5 · GRABA Y ENTREGA 6 min

Record in Canvas using the media recorder on the assignment. Before you submit, listen to your take once and check three things:

  1. Can you hear a scene before the first event? If it starts with an event, re-record — you skipped beats 1–3.
  2. Are your -ía endings audible, or did you swallow them?
  3. Did you reach 60 seconds? Most under-length takes are missing beat 6 — the comment from now.

If all three pass, submit. If one fails, you know exactly what to fix, and the re-record takes ninety seconds.

🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Contar bien es una habilidad social

In Day 2 you met la sobremesa — the long conversation after a meal. Here is why it matters to today's task: in much of the Spanish-speaking world, being a good storyteller is a recognised social skill, on a level with being a good listener. Someone who can hold a table with a well-shaped anecdote is valued for it, and children hear that skill modelled constantly.

So the 60–90 seconds you are recording is not an artificial school exercise dressed up as communication. It is a rehearsal of the specific thing you would actually be asked to do at a table in Bogotá or Guadalajara: ¿Y qué pasó? — and then it is your turn to hold the room.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA DILIGENCIA

Rehearsing three times when one would probably pass is the whole of today's virtue. Nobody sees the rehearsals; they only hear the take. La diligencia is precisely the work that leaves no visible trace except in the quality of the result. Nadie oye los ensayos, pero se oyen en la grabación.
