Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The tense you choose is not grammar. It is the meaning.
This is the most important lesson in Module 0, and the one Spanish 2 students most often finish half-sure. You already have both tenses. Today you learn that Spanish is not asking you which form is correct — it is asking what you mean. Both answers are often grammatical; they simply say different things.

By the end you will be able to put one preterite into a paragraph of imperfects and watch a story appear.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use the mientras… de repente frame to combine a scene with an interrupting event.
2
I will be able to explain how saber, conocer, querer and poder change meaning between the two tenses.
3
I will be able to narrate a short episode choosing each tense deliberately, and justify my choices.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Same words, two different stories

Read these two sentences and decide what changed:

«Cuando llegué, mi hermano comía.»
«Cuando llegué, mi hermano comió.»

Both are correct Spanish. But the first says he was already eating when you walked in; the second says he started eating once you arrived — perhaps because you arrived. The grammar did not change. The story did.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which of those two sentences would you have produced by instinct?
🧠 PART 2 · EL CONTRASTE 11 min

One question to ask every time

Before you choose, ask: “Am I moving the story forward, or holding it still?” Moving forward is preterite. Holding still — describing, setting up, filling in — is imperfect.

⭐ EL MARCO CLAVE · the frame to memorise
You met these two words on Day 1 for exactly this moment:

Mientras + imperfecto… de repente + pretérito.

Mientras caminaba a casa, de repente empezó a llover. The scene runs; the event cuts in. This single frame produces correct, natural narration more reliably than any rule you can memorise, and it works in every module for the rest of the year.
🧠 PART 3 · LOS VERBOS QUE CAMBIAN DE SIGNIFICADO 9 min

Four verbs where the tense changes the word

This is the Honors half of the lesson. With four common verbs, switching tense does not just shift the aspect — it changes what the verb means in English. A state held (imperfect) versus the moment it began or was resolved (preterite).

VerbImperfecto = the statePretérito = the moment
sabersabía — I knew (already)supe — I found out
conocerconocía — I knew (was acquainted with)conocí — I met (for the first time)
quererquería — I wantedquise — I tried / no quise — I refused
poderpodía — I was able (in general)pude — I managed to / no pude — I failed to

Read the difference out loud and it becomes obvious: Conocía a María = I knew María. Conocí a María en Bogotá = I met María in Bogotá. Same verb; the preterite marks the single moment the state began. No quise ir is the sharpest of the four — it does not mean “I didn't want to go,” it means I refused to go.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · the frame
«Mientras yo ___ la cena, alguien llamó a la puerta.»
✅ TRY IT · meaning change
You want to say “I met my best friend in third grade.”
✅ TRY IT · the sharpest one
«Le invité tres veces, pero no quiso venir.» What does this actually say?
✅ TRY IT · habit or event?
«Todos los veranos ___ a la casa de mi abuela.»

Ahora tú: narrate a small mishap in four sentences. Sentences 1–2 set the scene (imperfect), sentence 3 breaks it (preterite), sentence 4 resolves it. Then reveal the model.

Era sábado por la mañana y no había nadie en casa. Yo escuchaba música y limpiaba mi cuarto sin prisa. De repente se apagó la luz y todo quedó en silencio. Al final supe que mi padre cortÓ la electricidad para arreglar un enchufe.

Two imperfects setting the scene (era, había, escuchaba, limpiaba), two preterites breaking it (se apagó, quedó), and one meaning-change verb doing real work: supe = I found out, not «I knew».
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

El autobús de las siete

Eran las siete menos cinco y la parada estaba llena de gente. Hacía frío y yo no llevaba chaqueta. Mientras buscaba mi tarjeta en la mochila, llegó el autobús. Subí corriendo, pero cuando quise pagar, no encontré la tarjeta. El conductor me miraba con paciencia. Supe entonces que la dejé en casa. Menos mal que una señora que estaba detrás de mí pagó mi pasaje. No la conocía, pero ese día conocí a alguien generoso.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Italic verbs are imperfect (the scene); bold verbs are preterite (the events). Read only the bold ones in order — llegó, subí, quise, encontré, supe, dejé, pagó, conocí — and you still have the whole plot. Now read only the italics: you have the atmosphere and no story at all. That division is the entire lesson.

Then find the last two lines: no la conocía (I didn't know her) followed by conocí a alguien generoso (I met someone generous). The same verb, twice, in two tenses, meaning two different things — on purpose.
🌎 CULTURA · EL MUNDO HISPANO
Una distinción que el inglés perdió

English once had this distinction and lost it. Old English marked aspect more openly, and modern English still gropes for it with helpers — was walking, used to walk, would walk — three constructions where Spanish uses one word, caminaba.

This is why the contrast feels hard: you are not learning a new rule so much as re-learning a distinction your language stopped requiring you to make. Speakers of Russian, Greek or Arabic often find it straightforward. The difficulty is specifically an English-speaker's difficulty — which is worth knowing, because it means it is a habit of attention to build, not a talent you either have or lack.

★ LA VIRTUD · LAS SIETE VIRTUDES

Today's focus line — the tense you choose is the meaning — makes this a question of honesty before it is a question of grammar: sabía and supe tell two different truths about the same afternoon, and only one of them is what actually happened to you. Getting it right also asks for self-government, enough of it to put the question — ¿muevo la historia o la detengo? — every single time, even when a guess would probably pass. Guessing correctly teaches you nothing; asking builds the habit. The seven virtues rarely show up one at a time. La pregunta correcta, repetida, se convierte en instinto.
