Lesson 0.08
★ Lectura interpretativa — un viajero en Caracas
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Today you read something a Spanish speaker wrote for readers, not for learners.
Every module of this course has a reading day, and this is the first. The text is real: published in New York in 1889 by the Cuban writer José Martí, in a magazine he wrote for children across the Americas.

It was not simplified for you, and you will not understand every word. That is normal and it is not the task. The task is to understand what happens and why it matters — and to notice that Martí builds his scene with exactly the two tenses you spent this week rebuilding.

The graded Lectura interpretativa (15 pts) opens in Canvas today.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read an authentic 19th-century Spanish passage for gist without translating word by word.
2
I will be able to explain why Martí chose the imperfect for one clause and the preterite for another.
3
I will be able to infer what the writer values, from what the traveller does.
⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 6 min

Prepárate: contexto y tres cosas que buscar

El contexto. Simón Bolívar led the wars that freed much of South America from Spanish rule in the early 1800s. By 1889 there were statues of him in city squares across the continent. José Martí — a Cuban poet, journalist and revolutionary living in exile in New York — opened an essay about three liberators with a small story about a traveller arriving in Caracas.

Busca estas tres cosas as you read. Do not look up vocabulary yet:

  1. ¿Qué hace el viajero cuando llega? ¿Qué no hace?
  2. ¿Cuáles verbos cuentan los hechos y cuáles pintan la escena?
  3. ¿Con qué compara Martí la estatua al final?
⭐ CÓMO LEER UN TEXTO AUTÉNTICO · read this before you start
Read the whole passage twice before looking up a single word. On the first pass you are only asking: who, where, what happens? Unknown words will resolve themselves more often than you expect from the words around them. Looking up as you go breaks the sentence into pieces and makes comprehension harder, not easier — this is the single most important reading habit this strand is here to build.
📚 PART 2 · EL TEXTO 10 min
📖 LECTURA AUTÉNTICA · TEXTO DE DOMINIO PÚBLICO
Cuentan que un viajero llegó un día a Caracas al anochecer, y sin sacudirse el polvo del camino, no preguntó dónde se comía ni se dormía, sino cómo se iba a donde estaba la estatua de Bolívar. Y cuentan que el viajero, solo con los árboles altos y olorosos de la plaza, lloraba frente a la estatua, que parecía que se movía, como un padre cuando se le acerca un hijo.
José Martí, «Tres héroes», La Edad de Oro, núm. 1, Nueva York, julio de 1889. Texto de dominio público.

Ayuda de vocabulario — only after your second reading: sacudirse el polvo = to shake off the dust · el camino = the road, the journey · oloroso = fragrant · acercarse = to come close.

🧠 PART 3 · COMPRENSIÓN 10 min

Practice first — these are ungraded. The graded item in Canvas asks the same three kinds of question.

✅ IDEA GENERAL · gist
¿Qué hace el viajero al llegar a Caracas?
✅ DETALLE · detail
¿A qué hora del día llega el viajero, y cómo lo sabes?
✅ INFERENCIA · inference
Martí compara la estatua con «un padre cuando se le acerca un hijo». ¿Qué sugiere esa comparación?
🧠 PART 4 · LOS TIEMPOS DE MARTÍ 8 min

A 19th-century writer using this week's grammar

Look at the passage again. The bold verbs are preterite; the italic verbs are imperfect. This is not a coincidence and it is not decoration — it is Martí controlling exactly what you noticed on Day 5.

VerbTenseWhy Martí chose it
llegópretéritoThe single event that starts the story. One arrival, finished.
no preguntópretéritoOne decisive act — and the whole point of the anecdote is what he chose not to do.
se comía, se dormíaimperfectoNot events at all: general practice — where one eats, where one sleeps.
estabaimperfectoDescription. The statue's location is a standing state, not something that happened.
llorabaimperfectoThe camera left running. Not “he cried once” but a scene we are standing inside.
parecía, se movíaimperfectoOngoing impression — how it kept seeming to him, not a completed movement.

Now test it: if Martí had written lloró instead of lloraba, the traveller would have cried once and stopped, and the moment would close. Lloraba holds the scene open and lets us stand in the square with him. One tense choice carries the emotion of the whole passage. That is what you were building all week.

🎤 PART 5 · PUENTE A LA PRODUCCIÓN 6 min

Martí's traveller shows what he values by what he does first. Tomorrow's Tarea oral asks you to do the same thing with your own material — so start thinking now:

¿Hubo un lugar, un objeto o una persona que quisiste ver antes que nada?

Was there something you wanted to see or reach before anything else — a place you went straight to, a person you needed to find first? You do not have to answer now. Just choose the memory, so that tomorrow you are recording rather than deciding.

🌎 CULTURA · JOSÉ MARTÍ Y LA EDAD DE ORO
Una revista para los niños de América

Martí (1853–1895) was Cuban, exiled for most of his adult life, and is remembered across Latin America as both a writer and a revolutionary — he died in combat in the Cuban war of independence. In 1889, living in New York, he founded La Edad de Oro: a magazine written for children of the Americas, and one of the reasons his prose is read at every level of Spanish instruction.

Notice what that means about the text you just read. Martí wrote it for young readers — which is why a beginner can follow it — and he did not simplify his ideas to do it. He assumed children could handle liberty, exile and grief if the writing was clear enough. That is a decision about respect, and it is why this course opens its reading strand with him rather than with something easier.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA DILIGENCIA

Martí's traveller has just finished a long journey and does not stop to wash or eat. That is diligence pointed at something worth it — the discipline of doing the important thing first rather than the comfortable thing first. In this module it looks smaller: reading the passage twice before reaching for the dictionary, because that is the harder and better order. Lo primero primero, aunque cueste.
