Lesson 5.03
★ Por — la causa, el camino y el precio
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Six uses, one idea. Por never points at a destination — it describes what surrounds the action.
Textbooks usually present por as a list of eight uses to memorise, and students usually memorise it and then guess anyway. This course does it differently: you get one organising idea, and the uses fall out of it.

Here it is. Por covers the ground behind and around an action — the cause that produced it, the route it travelled, the time it filled, the price it cost, the means it used, the person it was done in place of. Every one of those is context, not target.

Today is por alone. Tomorrow is para alone. On Day 5 you put them side by side, and that is the hardest lesson of the module.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to state the single idea that holds all the uses of por together.
2
I will be able to name which job por is doing in a sentence I have not seen before.
3
I will be able to use por + infinitive to give the cause of something, and use six fixed expressions built on por.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

You have been reading por for two days

Both readings this week were full of it, and nobody explained any of it:

Los animales llegaron por el aire  ·  through, along which route
El número ha crecido por la fama  ·  because of
La residencia está limitada por ley  ·  by means of, by way of
Por eso  ·  a fixed expression: for that reason

Four different English translations. But look at what none of them does: none of them names a destination or a goal. That single negative fact is more useful than any list, and it is what Part 2 turns into a rule.

🧠 PART 2 · LOS SEIS TRABAJOS DE «POR» 12 min

The surroundings of an action

💡 LA IDEA CENTRAL
Picture the action in the middle of the sentence. Por describes everything around it and behind it: what caused it, what route it took, how long it lasted, what it cost, what carried it, and on whose behalf it happened.

Nothing on that list is an endpoint. That is the whole idea, and it is what you will use on Day 5.
El trabajoEn inglés se siente como…Ejemplo
1. La causa · el motivobecause of, out ofMuchas especies están amenazadas por la actividad humana.
2. El intercambio · el precioin exchange forPagamos cien dólares por la entrada al parque.
3. La duraciónfor (a length of time)Los científicos vivieron en la isla por tres meses.
4. El recorridothrough, along, aroundCaminamos por el bosque nublado hasta el mirador.
5. El medioby, by means ofEnviaron las muestras por correo y avisaron por teléfono.
6. En lugar de alguienon behalf of, instead ofFirmé la petición por mi hermana, que estaba de viaje.
  • por la mañana, por teléfono, por tres horas, por el bosquefour jobs, one word — listen for how unstressed it is

A note on job 3. Latin America uses por with a length of time freely — estudié por dos horas — and you will hear it constantly in Ecuador. In Spain the plain phrase without any preposition, estudié dos horas, is more usual, and durante dos horas is neutral everywhere. All three are correct; none is a mistake. Learn por for recognition and use whichever you prefer in production.

🧠 PART 3 · POR + INFINITIVO · Y LAS EXPRESIONES FIJAS 9 min

When the cause is an action

Job 1 has an important extension: the cause can itself be a verb. Por + infinitive means because of doing or for having done — never in order to.

FraseSignifica
Multaron al barco por tirar residuos al mar.because it dumped waste — the fine is a consequence
Le dieron un premio por proteger el bosque durante veinte años.because he protected it — the prize is a reward
Perdimos el barco por llegar tarde.because we arrived late — the cause of the loss

Read those three again and notice the shape: in every case the por phrase explains something that already happened. It looks backwards. Tomorrow you meet a construction that looks identical and points forwards instead, and Day 5 exists to keep the two apart.

Six expressions to know whole

These are fixed. Do not analyse them — memorise them as single units, the way you memorised está a tres cuadras in Módulo 3.

ExpresiónUso
por esofor that reason — your move-3 connector
por ejemplofor example
por lo menosat least
por supuestoof course
por finfinally, at last
gracias por + infinitivo / sustantivothank you for — gracias por venir, gracias por tu ayuda

Gracias por venir is worth pausing on, because English “thank you for coming” tempts every learner toward para. It is por, and it fits job 1 perfectly: you are naming the reason for the thanks, something the other person already did. Thanks always look backwards.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Por su aislamiento

Las Galápagos existen por un punto caliente en el fondo del océano. La placa de Nazca se mueve hacia el este y pasa por encima de ese punto, así que las islas del oeste son las más jóvenes y todavía tienen volcanes activos: Fernandina ha entrado en erupción varias veces en los últimos años.

Y por su aislamiento — mil kilómetros de océano abierto — el archipiélago recibió muy pocos colonizadores biológicos. Los reptiles resistieron el viaje por mar mejor que los mamíferos, porque aguantan semanas sin agua dulce. Por eso hay tortugas gigantes e iguanas, y casi ningún mamífero terrestre nativo.

Los científicos de la Estación Charles Darwin trabajan allí por temporadas de varios meses. Muchos llegan por un contrato corto y se quedan años. Por lo menos una cosa está clara: el archipiélago cambia a los científicos que lo estudian.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Nine uses of por. Go through them and assign each a number from Part 2's table. Two are fixed expressions, so they get no number.

Then answer the question the title asks. Isolation is a cause — job 1 — and it is the single most important word in the whole Galápagos story. Almost everything remarkable about the islands follows from a thousand kilometres of empty water. This is also why the reading on Day 8 uses por su aislamiento almost as a formula.
🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
Un archipiélago que se mueve

The geology is stranger than the biology and hardly anybody knows it. The Galápagos sit over a volcanic hotspot — a fixed plume of magma below the seafloor. The Nazca plate slides eastward across it, so each island was built over the hotspot and then carried away.

The consequence: the islands are lined up by age. Española and San Cristóbal in the east are roughly three to four million years old, eroded and low. Isabela and Fernandina in the west are under a million years old and still erupting — Fernandina has erupted several times in recent years, sometimes sending lava toward the coast where a colony of pink land iguanas lives on Wolf Volcano, a species not described by science until 2009.

And east of the archipelago the seafloor is dotted with drowned islands: former Galápagos, eroded and sunk. Species evolved on them and disappeared with them long before Darwin. The archipelago is not a place so much as a process — and it has been running for millions of years por the same slow reason it runs today.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA RESPONSABILIDAD

Today's grammar is the grammar of causes, and there is a discipline hidden in it. Naming a cause correctly is harder than assigning blame, and the two are constantly confused. The rats on the Galápagos did not arrive out of malice; they arrived in cargo, over two centuries, on ships belonging to people with no intention of harming anything. Responsibility that only fires when someone can be blamed is a weak kind of responsibility. The useful question is not ¿quién tiene la culpa? but ¿por qué pasó? — because only the second one tells you what to do next. Antes de exigir un cambio, entiende la causa.
