📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two sentences of encyclopedia Spanish, and every difficulty in them is a real one.
Módulo 5's reading day. The text is the opening of the Spanish-language encyclopedia entry on the Parque Nacional Galápagos — unedited, unsimplified, and much shorter than you expect a reading day to be. That is deliberate. Two sentences hold three separate skills, and rushing past them is how students stay stuck at the same level for a year.

One of them is this module's grammar doing a job no lesson listed. One is arithmetic: Spanish writes numbers differently from English, and if you read this text with English habits you will be wrong by a factor of a thousand. One is a participle from Módulo 2 quietly agreeing with a noun.

⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 6 min

Contexto y tres cosas que buscar

El contexto. This is the first paragraph of an encyclopedia article about the Parque Nacional Galápagos, in Spanish, written by volunteers for Spanish-speaking readers. Encyclopedia register is dense on purpose: it packs a date, an area, a proportion and an evaluation into two sentences and expects you to unpack them. You met the park's story in 5.06 and 5.07, so you are not reading cold.

Busca estas tres cosas. No dictionary on the first two passes:

  1. ¿Qué proporción del archipiélago protege el parque, y desde qué año?
  2. Hay un solo por en el texto. Encuéntralo. ¿Qué trabajo hace?
  3. ¿Quién dice que el archipiélago está bien conservado? La gramática te lo dice.
⭐ LEER CIFRAS EN ESPAÑOL · a technique, and a trap
Before you read: in most of the Spanish-speaking world the comma is the decimal separator, and the thousands separator is a thin space rather than a comma. So 7995,4 is a number a little under eight thousand, with four tenths. Read the comma as an English thousands comma and you get eight million.

Spanish also puts a space before the percent sign — 97 %, not 97%. That is the Real Academia's recommendation, not a typing error, and it is one of the small signals that tells you a text was edited by someone who writes Spanish professionally.

One honest complication for you specifically: Mexico uses the decimal point, like the United States. Your audio in this course is Mexican, so you will hear es-MX Spanish and read a text using the other convention. Both are correct in their own place, which is worth knowing before you meet it in a lab report.
📚 PART 2 · EL TEXTO 9 min
📖 LECTURA AUTÉNTICA · TEXTO CON LICENCIA ABIERTA
Fue fundado en 1959 y se convirtió en el primer parque nacional del país. Abarca unos 7995,4 km², un 97 % del área terrestre de las islas Galápagos, consideradas por expertos y científicos como el archipiélago volcánico mejor conservado del mundo.
Wikipedia en español, «Parque nacional Galápagos». Texto disponible bajo licencia Creative Commons Atribución-CompartirIgual 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Ayuda de vocabulario — only after your second reading: fundar = to found, to establish · convertirse en = to become · abarcar = to cover, to span · el área terrestre = the land area, as opposed to the sea · considerado = considered, held to be · el archipiélago = the island group · volcánico · conservado = preserved.

Two grammatical notes so nothing looks stranger than it is. Fue fundado is ser + participle — the passive you have been meeting in authentic texts since Módulo 2, and it is why the sentence never says who founded the park. And el área takes el despite being feminine, because a feminine noun beginning with a stressed a- takes el in the singular: el área, but las áreas.

And notice the wording: un 97 %, with the article. Spanish normally puts el or un in front of a percentage, where English usually does not. Un 97 % del área terrestre, not 97 % del área terrestre.

2. El participio que concuerda — Módulo 2, en un texto real

Look at consideradas. Feminine, plural. Now find the noun it agrees with, because there are two candidates in the sentence and only one of them fits:

CandidatoGénero y númeroLa forma sería¿Es la que está en el texto?
el archipiélagomasculino singularconsideradoNo
las islas Galápagosfemenino pluralconsideradasSí

So the ending is not decoration — it is the only thing telling you what is being evaluated. And that is Módulo 2's rule, met again where it actually matters: a participle used as an adjective agrees with its noun, unlike a participle after haber, which never changes. One letter, and a different sentence.

3. Su por, y tu para

The text gives you one por and no para at all. Supply the missing half yourself: write one sentence about the same park using para + infinitive to say what the park is for. Produce it before you open the model.

Un modelo: «El parque existe para proteger las especies endémicas del archipiélago.»

Now the two halves side by side.

• consideradas por expertos y científicos — el agente: la persona que hace la valoración, la causa del juicio, el terreno de atrás. Cámbialo a «consideradas para expertos» y dice que las islas están destinadas a los expertos, como un folleto escrito para especialistas. Gramatical, y falso.

• El parque existe para proteger las especies — el propósito. Hacia dónde apunta su existencia. Cámbialo a «existe por proteger las especies» y dice algo como «existe a causa de haber protegido», que no es una finalidad y en esta oración no significa nada.

Los dos son «for» en inglés. Ninguno de los dos es intercambiable.
🎤 PART 5 · PUENTE A LA PRODUCCIÓN 6 min

The encyclopedia describes a protected place in two sentences: what it is, how big, and why anyone rates it. Tomorrow's Tarea oral asks you to do the same for a place you have actually been, and then to go one step further than an encyclopedia can — to say what you have done and to ask something of your listener. Choose the place now:

¿Cuál es un lugar natural que conoces y que vale la pena proteger?

It does not have to be famous, or far away, or in danger. A creek behind a housing development is a better subject than a national park you have only seen photographs of, because you can describe it and you cannot describe the other one.

🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
1835, 1859, 1959 — tres fechas y un centenario

The date in the text is not an accident. The park was founded in 1959 — the centenary of the publication of El origen de las especies. Ecuador declared a national park in the year the world was marking a hundred years of the book the islands helped produce.

The visit itself was shorter and messier than the legend. Darwin arrived on the Beagle on 15 September 1835, aged 26, and spent about five weeks in the archipelago, of which only around nineteen days were ashore, on four islands. He had no revelation there. He mixed up which island his finch specimens came from, and it was the ornithologist John Gould in London, more than a year later, who realised the birds were distinct species rather than varieties. The vice-governor of the penal colony, Nicholas Lawson, casually told him that the tortoises' shells differed island by island — a remark Darwin noted and did not immediately understand.

Two conclusions worth keeping. Understanding usually arrives later than the evidence does. And the islands were a prison colony before they were a laboratory: the same place, described by different people, por different reasons.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA RESPONSABILIDAD

There is a quiet act of responsibility inside consideradas por expertos y científicos. The writer had a strong claim to make — best-preserved in the world — and instead of asserting it, attached it to somebody. That is four extra words and a completely different level of accountability, and it is the same habit that makes a footnote or a licence line worth printing. Notice that this page carries the text's licence for exactly that reason. Una afirmación honesta lleva su fuente al lado.
