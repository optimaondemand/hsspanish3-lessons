📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You cannot argue about a problem in Spanish until you can name it in Spanish.
Welcome to the second quarter. Módulo 4 was about advising a friend; Módulo 5 widens the circle to something nobody manages alone — el medio ambiente, and Ecuador's most famous piece of it, las islas Galápagos.

Today: ten words, and a small piece of word machinery that will let you turn most of them into other words. No new grammar — the module's grammar, por and para, arrives on Day 3 and is the hardest thing in the semester so far.

One practical note before you start: several of today's words carry accents that change the word if you drop them. La contaminación without its tilde is not Spanish, and this module's dictation items are graded on accents. If typing them is still slow for you, open the accent-typing guide and set up whichever method suits your keyboard.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

The register you are about to enter

Environmental Spanish is the language of news reports, science writing and public policy, and it has two features worth knowing in advance.

First, it is unusually transparent: contaminar, proteger, reciclaje, especie are all close to their English relatives, so you will recognise more in Part 2 than you expect. Second — and this is why this module owns the grammar it owns — it runs almost entirely on cause and purpose. Why is this happening, and what are we doing it for. That is por and para, and by Day 5 you will see that the vocabulary and the grammar were chosen together.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Four notes that will save you errors.

El medio ambiente is two words and masculine. Medio here means medium or surroundings, not half — the sense is “the surrounding medium”, which is a more accurate picture than the English word gives.

La especie is feminine and its plural is las especies. Do not confuse it with la especia — a spice. «Dos especias endémicas» means two native peppercorns.

La basura is normally singular even when there is a great deal of it: hay mucha basura en la playa, not «muchas basuras».

El bosque is forest or woodland in general. Ecuador uses two other words constantly and you should recognise them: el bosque nublado is the cloud forest of the Andean slopes, and la selva is the Amazon rainforest of the eastern lowlands. One small country holds Amazon rainforest, Andean cloud forest, high páramo grassland and the Pacific archipelago — which is why Ecuador appears on nearly every list of the world's most biodiverse countries.
🧠 PART 3 · DE VERBO A SUSTANTIVO 9 min

One word becomes four

This is not grammar; it is word machinery, and it is the single most efficient thing you can learn about Spanish vocabulary. Environmental writing is full of noun forms of verbs, and Spanish builds them predictably.

VerboSustantivoTerminaciónGénero
contaminarla contaminación-ciónsiempre femenino
protegerla protección-ciónsiempre femenino
conservarla conservación-ciónsiempre femenino
reciclarel reciclaje-ajesiempre masculino
cambiarel cambio—masculino

Two rules with no exceptions worth worrying about: -ción and -sión nouns are feminine (la contaminación, la decisión, la extinción), and -aje nouns are masculine (el reciclaje, el paisaje, el viaje). Learning those two endings settles the gender of several hundred words at once.

✏️ POR QUÉ EL ACENTO APARECE Y DESAPARECE
La contaminación carries a tilde because the stress falls on the final syllable of a word ending in -n. Add the plural ending and the stressed syllable is no longer final, so the tilde is no longer needed:

la acción → las acciones  ·  la región → las regiones  ·  el avión → los aviones

The pronunciation never changed. The written accent came off because the default rule now does the job on its own — exactly the logic you met in Módulo 3 with cruce and llegue, where the spelling moved to keep the sound steady. Spanish accents are not decoration; they are instructions about stress.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Un archipiélago que enseñó a mirar

Las islas Galápagos están a unos mil kilómetros de la costa de Ecuador, en medio del océano Pacífico. Nacieron de volcanes y nunca estuvieron unidas al continente. Por eso las plantas y los animales llegaron por el aire, por el agua o sobre troncos flotantes, y después cambiaron durante miles de años sin contacto con el resto del mundo.

El resultado es una naturaleza rara y frágil: tortugas gigantes, iguanas marinas, cormoranes sin capacidad de volar, pinzones con picos distintos en cada isla. Muchas de estas especies son endémicas: solo existen aquí, y si desaparecen del archipiélago, desaparecen del planeta.

En 1835 un naturalista inglés de veintiséis años, Charles Darwin, pasó cinco semanas en las islas. No entendió de inmediato el valor de sus notas; tardó años en ordenarlas. Pero aquellas cinco semanas cambiaron la biología, y hoy medio mundo asocia el nombre de un país pequeño con una idea enorme.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Two things to notice, one about content and one about grammar.

Content: the paragraph gives you the reason for the endemism, not just the fact. Isolation plus time equals species found nowhere else. Hold onto that; it is the engine of the whole module's culture strand.

Grammar: count the times por appears — por eso, por el aire, por el agua. Three uses, three slightly different jobs, and you have not been taught any of them yet. On Day 3 you will come back to this paragraph and be able to name each one.
🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
Cinco semanas de 1835

Darwin reached the Galápagos in September 1835 aboard the Beagle and stayed about five weeks. Two details usually get left out of the story, and both are worth knowing.

First, he did not have his idea there. He collected, labelled badly, and left. It was the vice-governor of the islands who told him that the giant tortoises differed from island to island, and it took Darwin years back in England — sorting specimens, corresponding with a bird expert who realised his finches were separate species — before the pattern became an argument. Origin of Species appeared in 1859, twenty-four years after the visit.

Second, the islands were not empty wilderness. Ecuador had annexed them in 1832, three years before Darwin arrived, and there was already a small penal settlement on Floreana. He ate tortoises, as everyone did.

The name in Spanish is el archipiélago de Colón officially, and las Galápagos in practice — from galápago, an old Spanish word for a saddle, which is what the tortoise shells looked like to the sailors who named them.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA RESPONSABILIDAD

This module's virtue is la responsabilidad, in its older and larger sense: stewardship — being answerable for something entrusted to you. It is easy to talk about, and today's work is the least glamorous part of it: learning the words. But vague language protects nobody. “Something should be done about the environment” commits you to nothing; la contaminación del río viene de la mina commits you to a claim you can be held to. Precision is where responsibility starts, and it is why a vocabulary day belongs to a virtue at all. Nombrar bien un problema es el primer acto de responsabilidad.
