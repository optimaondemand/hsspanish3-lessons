Lesson 5.09
★ Tarea oral — un lugar, una razón, un compromiso
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Por and para are two of the smallest words in the sentence and they are carrying your entire argument.
Today you record the Tarea oral (20 pts) — 20% of your semester grade. The content is already chosen: yesterday's reading asked you to pick a natural place you actually know.

The specific difficulty is a speaking one. English speakers flatten unstressed function words into a mumble, and in English that is harmless. Here it is not: if a listener cannot tell your por from your para, they cannot tell your cause from your purpose, and the point of the whole recording disappears into two syllables. Part 4 is about landing them.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to speak for 60–90 seconds describing a place, its importance, and my own commitment to it.
2
I will be able to pronounce por and para distinctly enough that a listener can hear which one I chose.
3
I will be able to close by asking one concrete thing of my listener, using a direct tú command.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Seis palabras, no un guion

Same method as every module: a six-word outline, never a written-out text. A read script always sounds read, and here it costs you twice — reading aloud is exactly when unstressed words like por and para get swallowed, because your eyes are ahead of your mouth.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Say these two aloud, one after the other: «Lo hago por ti.» · «Lo hago para ti.» Could somebody in the next room tell them apart?
🎤 PART 2 · LA TAREA 9 min

El tema: Describe un lugar natural que conoces, di por qué vale la pena protegerlo, cuenta qué has hecho tú y para qué, y termina con un mandato directo a la persona que te escucha.

Four moves: describe · justify · commit · ask. The place does not need to be famous or endangered. A creek behind a shopping centre, the oak in your grandmother's yard, the stretch of beach you walk in summer — anything you can actually describe beats anywhere you have only seen in photographs.

Not scored: a native accent. Comprehensibility is scored; sounding Ecuadorian is not, and nobody will deduct for a foreign accent that leaves your meaning clear.

One reassurance, because this prompt sounds bigger than it is: every idea it asks for can be said with grammar you already own. Reasons take porque + a normal verb: «Vale la pena protegerlo porque es el único bosque de la zona.» Purposes take para + infinitive: «Recojo basura para mantenerlo limpio.» Judgements take an impersonal plus an infinitive: «Es importante cuidar este lugar.» If you find yourself reaching for something more elaborate, you have left the task rather than risen to it.

Beats 3 and 6 are both assessed. Write those two words first. A recording that describes a place and never says why it matters loses points under two criteria at once, and a recording with no command has no ending — it just stops.

⭐ ENSAYA TRES VECES
Say the whole thing aloud three times from your six words. On run three, listen for two things only: whether every para came out as two clear syllables, and whether beat 6 sounded like an invitation rather than an instruction. Both are fixed by slowing down, not by changing the words.

A model closing, if you want the shape: «Ve al arroyo este fin de semana y llévate una bolsa. Hazlo por el lugar, no por mí.» Two commands, one por, and it ends on somebody other than the speaker.
🔊 PART 4 · PRONUNCIACIÓN 11 min

Click each and repeat aloud. These six are the module's hardest words to say, and four of them are almost certainly going into your recording.

  • la contaminaciónstress on the last syllable — ción
  • el cambio climáticocli-MÁ-ti-co — third from the end
  • dañarthe ñ is one sound, not n + y
  • la huellathe h is silent — it begins with a w sound
  • reutilizarfive syllables, no diphthong: re-u-ti-li-zar
  • el esfuerzo colectivoa long phrase — do not rush the two e sounds together

1. Para has two syllables and both of them are audible. PA-ra. English reduces unstressed vowels almost to nothing, so para tends to come out as “pra” or “pruh”. Spanish does not reduce vowels: the second a is as full as the first. Overdo it while you practise.

2. Por has a rounded Spanish o, not an English er. If it sounds like “per” or “purr”, the vowel has collapsed. Say por next to pon and sol until the o matches.

3. Linking across the word is correct, and you should not fight it. Por el bosque genuinely runs together as po-rel-BOS-que; para el agua comes out as pa-ra-el-A-gua. That is what fluent Spanish sounds like. The thing to protect is the number of vowels, not the gaps between words.

✅ TRY IT · what a swallowed vowel costs
You say «Recojo basura pra mantenerlo limpio», reducing para to one syllable. What is the actual risk?
✅ TRY IT · what the rubric weighs
Two takes. One has a strong American accent but all four moves, three por phrases doing different jobs and a clear closing command. The other is smooth and fluent, describes the place beautifully, and never says why it matters or asks anything of the listener. Which scores higher?
⏺ PART 5 · GRABA Y ENTREGA 6 min

Record in Canvas on the assignment itself. Listen once before submitting and check four things:

  1. Did you say why the place matters? Beat 3.
  2. Did you end with a command to your listener? Beat 6.
  3. Can you hear two syllables in every para?
  4. Did you reach 60 seconds? Short takes are almost always missing beats 4 and 5.

If all four pass, submit. Takes are unlimited, so there is no reason to submit one you already know is missing a beat.

🌎 CULTURA · ECUADOR
Jorge y Diego — dos tortugas y un programa

The Charles Darwin Research Station at Puerto Ayora is where two very different endings happened.

El Solitario Jorge — Lonesome George — was the last known tortoise of Pinta Island. For four decades biologists tried to find him a mate and failed. He died on 24 June 2012, and his subspecies died with him. He had become, entirely against his will, the most famous individual animal in conservation.

Diego is the other ending. A tortoise from Española Island, he was sent back from the San Diego Zoo in the 1970s to join a captive breeding programme when fewer than fifteen Española tortoises remained alive. He fathered somewhere around 800 offspring. In June 2020, aged past a hundred, he was retired and returned to Española — a species pulled back from the edge by a programme that outlasted most of the careers inside it.

Two tortoises, one station, sixty years. The difference between the two stories is not luck. It is how early somebody started, and how long they were willing to keep going — which is worth remembering when your own commitment sounds small.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA RESPONSABILIDAD

Beat 6 is the whole virtue in one sentence. It is easy to describe a place and stop there, admiring it; asking somebody to do something about it puts you on the hook for meaning it. That is why the task ends with a command instead of a conclusion, and why the model closing points away from the speaker: hazlo por el lugar, no por mí. Stewardship is not affection for a place. It is what you are prepared to ask, out loud, on its behalf. Cuidar no es admirar: es responder por algo que no puede hablar.
