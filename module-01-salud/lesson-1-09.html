Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The tense you have been building all week is, in speech, mostly two syllables of helper verb. Do not swallow them.
Today you record the Tarea oral (20 pts) — 20% of your semester grade, the most heavily weighted component in this course. You already have the content: yesterday's reading asked you to choose something you have learned about health in your own family.

What today adds is the part only speaking teaches: holding the present perfect across a minute and a half of unscripted talk, with the endings audible.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to speak for 60–90 seconds sustaining the present perfect.
2
I will be able to pronounce he / has / ha / hemos / han clearly — the h silent, the vowel audible.
3
I will be able to say leído, oído and caído as three syllables, not two.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Speak from six words, not from a script

Same method as Module 0, and the same reason: a read script always sounds read, and it makes the task harder rather than easier — you end up trying to recall wording instead of a story, so one forgotten phrase derails the whole take.

You will build a six-word outline in Part 3 and speak from that.

🎤 PART 2 · LA TAREA 9 min

The prompt and how it is scored

El tema: ¿Qué has aprendido sobre la salud en tu propia familia?

What have you learned about health in your own family — a remedy someone taught you, a rule your household keeps, something a grandparent always said. Yesterday's text argued that this knowledge travels through families and places; this is your version of that.

RequirementDetail
Length60–90 seconds of connected speech
FramePresent perfect carries it — he aprendido, hemos usado, me han enseñado
SwitchesAt least one imperfect for background (de niño…, siempre usábamos…)
LanguageSpanish only — no English words filled in
DeliveryFrom an outline. Do not read a script.

Present perfect accuracyhaber + participle correct and unsplit; pronouns before haber

FluencyContinuous speech. Pauses fine; long silences and restarts not
ComprehensibilityA patient listener follows without needing a repeat

Not on that list, deliberately: a native-like accent. Comprehensibility is scored; sounding Mexican is not.

⭐ ENSAYA TRES VECES · before you record
Say the whole thing aloud three times from your six words. It will come out differently each time — that is the proof you are speaking rather than reciting. If run three is not noticeably smoother than run one, do a fourth. Nobody's first take is their best.
🔊 PART 4 · PRONUNCIACIÓN DEL PERFECTO 11 min

Two sounds that decide whether you are understood

Click each, then repeat aloud.

  • he, has, ha, hemos, hanthe helper — h is silent
  • leído, oído, caídothree syllables each
  • no me he sentido bienfour words, one breath

1. The h is silent — but the vowel is not. He is pronounced “e”, ha is “a”. English speakers often either aspirate the h (which sounds foreign) or drop the whole word (which loses the tense). Say «e-a-pren-DI-do» and let the e be heard.

2. Leído is le-Í-do. Three syllables, stress on the middle. Collapsing it to “LAY-do” is the single commonest pronunciation error with these participles — and it is the audible version of the accent you learned to write on Day 3. The tilde and the syllable are the same fact.

✅ TRY IT · how many syllables?
«He oído esa historia antes.» How many syllables is oído?
✅ TRY IT · what the rubric rewards
Two takes: one has a strong American accent but holds the present perfect throughout; the other sounds smoother but keeps saying «he me sentido». Which scores higher?
⏺ PART 5 · GRABA Y ENTREGA 6 min

Record in Canvas using the media recorder on the assignment. Before submitting, listen once and check three things:

  1. Can you hear the he / has / ha in each verb, or did they vanish?
  2. Did any pronoun land after haber? Listen for «he me» and fix it.
  3. Did you reach 60 seconds? Most short takes are missing beat 6 — what you think of it.

If all three pass, submit. If one fails, you know exactly what to fix and the re-record takes ninety seconds.

🌎 CULTURA · MÉXICO
Lo que se hereda en la cocina

Yesterday's text made an argument; today's task tests whether it applies to you too. In Mexico this transmission is often explicitly credited — people say «mi abuela me enseñó» and name her, because the knowledge is understood to belong to a person and a place rather than to a book.

US families do this as well and usually without noticing: chicken soup, honey for a sore throat, a rule about going outside with wet hair. If you think you have nothing to say for today's task, you almost certainly have not looked. Ask someone at home what their mother did — you will get an answer, and it will be the most interesting ninety seconds of Spanish you record this module.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA TEMPLANZA

Rehearsing three times when one would probably pass is the whole of today's virtue. Nobody hears the rehearsals; they hear the take. La templanza is precisely the work that leaves no trace except in the quality of the result. Nadie oye los ensayos, pero se oyen en la grabación.
