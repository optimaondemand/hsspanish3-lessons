Lesson 1.03
★ El presente perfecto — lo que ha pasado hasta ahora
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A third past tense — and this one keeps one foot in the present.
You have hablé (finished event) and hablaba (scene or habit). Today you add he hablado — I have spoken — which reports what has happened up to now, in a stretch of time that is not yet closed.

It is built from two pieces and neither is difficult. The care today goes into the participio and its accents, because the participle is the piece the whole rest of the module is built on.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to conjugate haber in the present and form regular participles in -ado and -ido.
2
I will be able to keep haber and the participle together, and place no and pronouns correctly.
3
I will be able to write leído, oído and caído with their required accents and explain why.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Why a doctor asks it this way

Yesterday's doctor asked ¿Ha tenido fiebre? — not ¿Tuvo fiebre? The difference is real. ¿Tuvo fiebre? asks about a closed episode: did you have a fever (then, that time). ¿Ha tenido fiebre? asks about a stretch of time that is still open — since this illness began, right up to this moment.

That is the whole meaning of the tense: the window is not closed yet.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
In English, do you feel a difference between “Did you eat?” and “Have you eaten?”
🧠 PART 2 · HABER + PARTICIPIO 11 min

Two pieces, and the first one has five forms

haber is the helper. It carries the person; it carries no meaning of its own. Learn these five as a block:

SubjecthaberExample
yoheHe tomado la medicina.
túhas¿Has dormido bien?
usted / él / ellaha¿Ha tenido fiebre?
nosotros/ashemosHemos llamado al consultorio.
ustedes / ellos / ellashanHan pedido una cita.
⚠️ TRES REGLAS DE HIERRO
1. Nothing separates haber from the participle. Not no, not a pronoun, not an adverb. No he comido ✔ · «he no comido» ✘
2. Pronouns go before haber. Me he lastimado el tobillo ✔ · «he me lastimado» ✘
3. The participle never changes for gender or number here. Ana ha llegado, ellos han llegado — always -o. (It does agree when used as an adjective: la puerta está cerrada. Different job.)
🧠 PART 3 · EL PARTICIPIO REGULAR 10 min

Two endings — and one accent rule

Infinitive ends inDrop it, addExamples
-ar-adotomar → tomado · descansar → descansado · llamar → llamado
-er-idocomer → comido · toser → tosido · beber → bebido
-ir-idodormir → dormido · pedir → pedido · sufrir → sufrido

As in the preterite, -er and -ir share one ending. So there are two patterns, not three.

✏️ LA REGLA DEL ACENTO · this module grades accents
When the stem ends in a vowel, the i of -ido takes a written accent:

leer → leído · oír → oído · caer → caído · traer → traído · creer → creído

Why: without the tilde, the two vowels would merge into one syllable (lei-do, two syllables). The accent forces them apart into le-í-do, three syllables — which is how the word is actually said. The tilde is not decoration; it is the pronunciation.

And notice oído — the same word as the body part you learned on Day 1, and it needs the accent for the same reason.

A handful of verbs build their participle irregularly — hacer, decir, ver and about six others. Those belong to Module 2, which is built around them. Today, regular participles only.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · nothing comes between them
«Todavía ___ la medicina.» (I have not taken)
✅ TRY IT · the accent rule
«___ el artículo tres veces y todavía no lo entiendo.» (leer, yo)
✅ TRY IT · pronoun placement
«___ el tobillo jugando al fútbol.» (lastimarse, yo)
✅ TRY IT · the participle does not agree
«Mis hermanas ___ al consultorio.» (llamar)

Ahora tú: answer these three as a patient, in the present perfect — ¿Ha tomado algo? ¿Ha dormido bien? ¿Ha tenido fiebre?

—Sí, he tomado un té con miel, pero no he tomado ninguna medicina.
—No he dormido bien; me he despertado tres veces por la tos.
—He tenido fiebre solamente una noche.

Check three things in yours: no in front of he (never between) · the pronoun before haber in me he despertado · and every participle ending in -ado or -ido with no gender agreement.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Mensaje a la doctora

Buenos días, doctora. Le escribo porque no he mejorado mucho esta semana. He descansado tres días, he bebido mucha agua y he tomado el jarabe que usted me dio. La tos ha bajado un poco, pero todavía no he dormido una noche completa. Esta mañana me he despertado con dolor de garganta otra vez. Mi madre ha llamado al consultorio dos veces, pero no hemos podido conseguir una cita antes del viernes. ¿Debo esperar?

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Nine present perfects, and every one of them means the same thing: this stretch of time is still open. The illness is not over, which is exactly why the writer is not using the preterite. Find the two negatives and check where no sits; find me he despertado and check where the pronoun sits.
🌎 CULTURA · MÉXICO Y ESPAÑA
«¿Comiste?» o «¿Has comido?»

Here is something you need to know before you meet it in the wild. In much of Spain, the present perfect covers ground that Mexican Spanish gives to the preterite. For lunch eaten an hour ago, a speaker in Madrid is likely to say ¿Has comido? while a speaker in Mexico City says ¿Comiste? — same afternoon, same lunch, different tense.

Neither is an error. This course teaches the Latin-American preference because that is the Spanish most of you will hear and use, and because the distinction you are learning today — open window versus closed episode — is the one that holds across the whole language. But when you meet a Spaniard using the perfect where you expected the preterite, they are not making a mistake. Recognising that instead of correcting it is part of what Intermediate proficiency means.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL AUTOGOBIERNO

Read the message to the doctor again as a record of self-government: he descansado tres días, he bebido mucha agua, he tomado el jarabe. The writer ran the week first and then asked — neither panicking nor waiting in silence. That is why the present perfect is the right tense for it: the window is still open, so the account is of habits still being managed rather than a closed episode. Primero lo que puedo hacer yo; después lo que pido.
