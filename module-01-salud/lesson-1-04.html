★ ¿He comido o comí? — la ventana abierta
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Is the window still open, or has it closed?
You can now build the present perfect. Today you learn when to choose it over the preterite — and the good news is that you already know how to do this kind of thinking. Module 0 taught you to ask a question before conjugating (¿muevo la historia o la detengo?). Today's question is just as short:

¿La ventana de tiempo sigue abierta? If yes, present perfect. If it has closed, preterite.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to choose between the present perfect and the preterite and justify the choice.
2
I will be able to recognise which time expressions signal each tense.
3
I will be able to use all three past forms in one connected description of an illness.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Two sentences, one difference

«Esta semana he tenido fiebre dos veces.»
«La semana pasada tuve fiebre dos veces.»

Both are correct. The difference is not the fever — it is esta semana versus la semana pasada. This week is still running, so more could still happen: the window is open, and Spanish uses the perfect. Last week is finished and sealed: preterite.

Notice the tense is chosen by the time frame, not by how long ago the event was.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
It is Thursday. You want to talk about something that happened on Monday of this week. Which feels right?
🧠 PART 2 · EL CONTRASTE 11 min

Three pasts, three jobs

You now have the complete set. Each one answers a different question:

FormQuestion it answersTime frameExample
he tenido
presente perfecto		¿Qué ha pasado hasta ahora?Open — still runningEsta semana he tenido fiebre.
tuve
pretérito		¿Qué pasó?Closed — sealed episodeAyer tuve fiebre.
tenía
imperfecto		¿Cómo era? ¿Qué pasaba?No boundaries — scene or habitDe niño tenía fiebre a menudo.

A useful way to hold it: the imperfecto has no edges at all, the pretérito has both edges, and the perfecto has a beginning but no closing edge yet — which is exactly why it reaches into the present.

⭐ EL EFECTO PRESENTE · the second use, and it has no time marker
The perfect is also used when a past event still matters right now, with no time expression at all:

«No puedo correr: me he lastimado el tobillo.» — the injury happened in the past, but the point is its present consequence.

Compare «me lastimé el tobillo el año pasado» — a closed fact from last year, with no claim about today. Same injury, different relevance.
🧠 PART 3 · LAS PISTAS TEMPORALES 9 min

Expressions that lean one way

Pulls the PERFECTOPulls the PRETÉRITO

But do not learn these as a lookup table. They are evidence, not rules — every one of them works because of the open-or-closed logic underneath. Este año pulls the perfect because this year has not ended; en 2023 pulls the preterite because 2023 has. If you memorise the list without the logic, you will be helpless the moment a sentence has no time expression in it — and the efecto presente use never does.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · open or closed?
«Este mes ___ al consultorio tres veces.» (ir, yo)
✅ TRY IT · closed episode
«___ una cita el lunes pasado, pero la cancelé.» (pedir, yo)
✅ TRY IT · no time marker at all
«No puedo jugar hoy porque ___ la rodilla.» (lastimarse, yo)
✅ TRY IT · all three in one sentence
«De niña ___ muchos resfriados, pero este invierno solo ___ uno.»

Ahora tú: describe your health this year in four sentences using all three past forms at least once. Say it aloud, then compare.

De niño me enfermaba mucho en invierno y siempre tenía tos. El año pasado me lastimé el hombro jugando al baloncesto. Este año he estado bastante sano: solo he tenido un resfriado. Hasta ahora no he faltado a clase ni un día.

Check the logic, not just the forms: me enfermaba / tenía = childhood habit · me lastimé = closed episode last year · he estado / he tenido / he faltado = this year, window still open.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La conversación en la sala de espera

—¿Es la primera vez que vienes a este consultorio?
—No, he venido dos veces este año. La primera vez fue en enero, cuando me lastimé la espalda.
—¿Y ha mejorado?
—Un poco. He seguido los ejercicios que me dieron y he descansado bastante. Pero últimamente me ha dolido otra vez.
—Yo vine el mes pasado por la garganta. Tenía fiebre y no podía hablar. Ahora estoy bien.
—Menos mal.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Sort every verb into the three columns from Part 2 and justify each one from the time frame. Two to look at closely: «he venido dos veces este año» versus «la primera vez fue en enero» — the same speaker switches tense mid-thought, because the year is open but January is closed. And «últimamente me ha dolido»: no closed episode, just a state reaching into now.
🌎 CULTURA · MÉXICO
La sala de espera

The dialogue above is not an artificial exercise setting. In Mexico and much of Latin America, striking up conversation with a stranger in a waiting room is ordinary and expected — silence between neighbours in a small sala de espera can read as coldness rather than politeness. Asking a stranger «¿y ha mejorado?» is not intrusive; it is the normal minimum of friendliness.

US students often find this the hardest cultural adjustment in the whole health module, well ahead of any grammar. The tenses you are learning today are, in practice, the tenses of exactly this conversation: what has been going on with you lately is what strangers ask each other.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA TEMPLANZA

Look at what the first speaker reports: exercises done, rest taken, and honest acknowledgement that the pain has returned. No exaggeration and no minimising — an accurate account of an open window. Temperance in speech is exactly this: saying how much, not more and not less. Ni quejarse de más ni callar de menos.
