Lesson 1.05
★ Ya, todavía no, alguna vez — y los reflexivos
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Four small words that almost always bring this tense with them.
Yesterday you learned to choose the perfect. Today you learn the four expressions that live in it — ya, todavía no, alguna vez, nunca — because each one is about a window that has not closed.

Then the second half: reflexive verbs in the perfect. This is where pronoun placement stops being a rule you can recite and becomes something you have to produce, and it is the commonest error in the module's writing task.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use ya, todavía no, alguna vez and nunca with the present perfect.
2
I will be able to place reflexive and object pronouns correctly before haber, every time.
3
I will be able to ask somebody about their experience using ¿Alguna vez has…?
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Why these four belong together

Look at what they have in common. Ya = it has happened by now. Todavía no = it has not happened yet — and “yet” implies it still might. Alguna vez = at any point in a life so far. Nunca = at no point so far.

Every one of them measures from the past up to now. That is the present perfect's entire job, which is why the four of them pull it so reliably.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
«Todavía no he tomado la medicina.» What does this imply about whether you will?
🧠 PART 2 · LOS CUATRO MARCADORES 11 min
ExpressionMeansWhere it goesExample
yaalready, by nowBefore haber, or at the endYa he tomado la medicina.
todavía nonot yetBefore haberTodavía no he ido al consultorio.
alguna vezever, at any timeAfter the participle, or after haber¿Has estado alguna vez en el hospital?
nuncanever (so far)Before haberNunca he tenido una operación.

Two things to notice. Nunca before haber needs no no — nunca he tenido, not «nunca no he tenido». And ya is remarkably flexible in position, which is unusual; the other three are not, so do not generalise from it.

⭐ ¿ALGUNA VEZ HAS…? · the most useful question in this lesson
¿Alguna vez has…? is how you ask about anybody's life experience, in any topic — not just health. ¿Alguna vez has viajado a México? ¿Alguna vez has probado el mole? It is the single most conversationally valuable structure in this module, and the answer comes back in the same tense: Sí, he ido dos veces / No, nunca he ido.
🧠 PART 3 · LOS REFLEXIVOS EN EL PERFECTO 9 min

The pronoun goes first. Always.

Health talk is full of reflexive verbs — you hurt yourself, you fall, you wake up, you get sick. In the perfect, the pronoun sits in front of haber, and nothing may come between haber and the participle:

VerbCorrectNever
lastimarseme he lastimado el tobillo«he me lastimado» · «he lastimado me»
caersese ha caído en la escalera«ha se caído»
despertarsenos hemos despertado tarde«hemos nos despertado»
enfermarsete has enfermado dos veces«has te enfermado»
sentirseme he sentido mal«he me sentido»

And when a negative joins in, the order is no + pronoun + haber + participio: No me he sentido bien esta semana. Four words, one fixed order, no exceptions. Note also caerse → se ha caído — the accent rule from Day 3 is still working, because the stem ends in a vowel.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · nunca needs no «no»
«___ una operación en mi vida.» (never had)
✅ TRY IT · pronoun placement
«Mi hermano ___ dos veces este mes.» (enfermarse)
✅ TRY IT · negative + reflexive
«___ bien desde el lunes.» (I have not felt well)
✅ TRY IT · the accent again
«Mi abuela ___ dos veces este año, pero está bien.» (caerse)

Ahora tú: ask and answer three ¿Alguna vez has…? questions about health — and make at least one answer use nunca and one use a reflexive verb.

—¿Alguna vez te has lastimado jugando un deporte? —Sí, me he lastimado el tobillo dos veces, pero nunca he necesitado una operación.
—¿Alguna vez has estado en el hospital? —Sí, he estado una vez, cuando era pequeño.
—¿Ya has ido al consultorio esta semana? —Todavía no he ido; voy el jueves.

Check three things: nunca with no extra no · every pronoun sitting in front of haber · and todavía no before he, not after.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Formulario del paciente

Por favor conteste las siguientes preguntas.

¿Alguna vez ha estado usted en el hospital? — Sí, una vez, en 2019.
¿Ha tenido fiebre esta semana? — Sí, dos noches.
¿Se ha caído en los últimos seis meses? — No, nunca me he caído.
¿Ya ha tomado algún medicamento hoy? — Todavía no he tomado nada.
¿Cómo se ha sentido últimamente? — No me he sentido bien. Me he despertado con dolor de garganta varios días.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Every question on a real intake form is in the present perfect, and now you can see why: a form asks about your life up to today. Find all four markers from Part 2 in the answers, then check each reflexive — se ha caído, me he caído, se ha sentido, me he despertado — and confirm the pronoun is in front of haber every single time. Note the questions use usted: this is a clinic, not a friend.
🌎 CULTURA · MÉXICO
El formulario y el usted

Notice that the form addresses the patient as usted throughout — ¿ha estado usted…?, conteste. In Mexico this is not about age or status; it is the default register of any institution speaking to an individual, and a sixteen-year-old filling in a clinic form will be addressed exactly this way.

Worth knowing because the reverse also holds: a Mexican teenager who addressed a nurse as tú would sound noticeably rude, in a way that has no real equivalent in English. English has no grammatical way to be impolite by pronoun choice — Spanish does, and health settings are among the places it matters most.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA TEMPLANZA

A patient form is an exercise in temperance nobody thinks of as one: it asks you to report your own body accurately, without dramatising and without brushing things off. Todavía no he tomado nada is a small, exact truth. Medicine works on precisely such truths. La exactitud también es una virtud.
