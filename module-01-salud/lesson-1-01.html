★ El cuerpo — y lo que te duele
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You cannot ask for help with something you cannot name.
Módulo 1 opens the year's real work. The theme is la salud — health, the body, and how you talk to someone about what is wrong. It is the module where a lot of Spanish stops being academic: if you are ever unwell in a Spanish-speaking country, this vocabulary is the difference between being understood and pointing.

Today: ten parts of the body, and the verb doler — which behaves exactly like gustar, a pattern you already own. New words on grammar you have; the new grammar arrives on Day 3.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name ten parts of the body with their correct articles.
2
I will be able to say what hurts using me duele / me duelen correctly.
3
I will be able to explain why Spanish says me duele la cabeza and not me duele mi cabeza.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

A pattern you already have

In Spanish 1 you learned to say me gusta el café — literally “coffee is pleasing to me.” The thing you like is the subject; you are just the person it happens to. Doler works identically: me duele la cabeza = “the head hurts to me.”

So you are not learning a new structure today. You are pointing an old one at new vocabulary.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
If me gustan los libros is plural because libros is plural, what do you predict for “my feet hurt”?
📚 PART 2 · EL CUERPO 12 min

Diez partes del cuerpo

Click the 🔊 and repeat aloud. Learn each word with its article — la espalda, not espalda. Gender is not decoration here: you will need it the moment you say me duele el hombro.

  • la cabezahead
  • la gargantathroat
  • el estómagostomach
  • la espaldaback
  • el pechochest
  • el hombroshoulder
  • la rodillaknee
  • el tobilloankle
  • el oídoinner ear
  • la pielskin
✏️ ESCRIBIR CON TILDES · you will be graded on this
Two of today's words carry a tilde: el estómago and el oído. In this course accents are graded, including in typed short answers — estomago will be marked wrong.

El oído is worth a second look. The tilde on the í is there to break the vowels apart, so it is three syllables — o·í·do — not two. Without it you would read “OI-do.” That is also why el oído (the inner ear, the sense of hearing) is a different word from la oreja (the visible outer ear): if it hurts inside, it is el oído.

Fix your typing now rather than in the middle of a quiz: Cómo escribir las tildes y la ñ — Windows, Mac, phone and Chromebook.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Two worth a second look. El oído is the inner ear — the part that hurts and hears; la oreja is the outer ear you can see. A doctor asks about el oído. And el estómago carries its accent on the first o — es-TÓ-ma-go. Three syllables back from the end, so the tilde is required.
🧠 PART 3 · DOLER · LO QUE TE DUELE 8 min

Two forms cover almost everything

Who it hurtsOne thing hurtsTwo or more hurt
a míme duele la cabezame duelen los pies
a tite duele la gargantate duelen los oídos
a él / ella / ustedle duele el estómagole duelen las rodillas
a nosotrosnos duele la espaldanos duelen los hombros
a ellos / ustedesles duele el pecholes duelen los tobillos

The rule that trips English speakers: Spanish uses el / la, not mi / tu. You say me duele la cabeza, never «me duele mi cabeza» — because me has already told your listener whose head it is, and repeating it sounds redundant in Spanish the way “my own head hurts me” would in English. Same logic in me lavo las manos.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · agreement
«A mi hermana ___ los oídos desde el viaje en avión.»
✅ TRY IT · article, not possessive
How does a Spanish speaker say “My throat hurts”?
✅ TRY IT · which body part?
You have been coughing all night and it hurts to swallow. ¿Qué te duele?

Ahora tú: describe three different aches — one singular, one plural, and one belonging to somebody else. Say them aloud before revealing.

Me duele la espalda porque dormí mal. Me duelen las rodillas después de correr. A mi padre le duele el hombro desde el sábado.

Check yours against three things: duele or duelen matching the body part · el/la/los/las rather than mi · and le when it is somebody else.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Un mal domingo

El domingo me levanté y supe inmediatamente que algo no iba bien. Me dolía la cabeza y tenía la garganta seca. Intenté desayunar, pero me dolía el estómago y no comí casi nada. Por la tarde me dolían también los oídos, así que mi madre me tomó la temperatura. Al final llamamos al consultorio y pedimos una cita para el lunes.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Notice that doler appears three times, twice singular and once plural, and each time it agrees with the body part rather than with the narrator. Notice too that it is in the imperfect — me dolía, not me dolió — because an ache is a state you were in, not an event. Module 0's job, still doing work.
🌎 CULTURA · MÉXICO
Dos medicinas, un paciente

This module's country is México, and its theme is one most US students find genuinely unfamiliar: in much of Mexico, la medicina tradicional and hospital medicine are not rivals — many families use both, often for the same illness. A person may see a physician and also take a té de manzanilla prescribed by a grandmother, and see no contradiction in it.

Mexico's health ministry has formally recognised traditional medicine, and the practice is old: much of it descends from Nahua and Maya knowledge that predates 1519. You will read about it properly on Day 6 and meet a real text on it on Day 8. For now just notice that «¿qué te duele?» may be answered in more than one medical language.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL AUTOGOBIERNO

This module's virtue is el autogobierno (self-government) — governing yourself: your appetites, and also your sleep, your training, your recovery and the whole set of small daily decisions nobody else is going to make for you. A module about the body is its natural home, because your body is the one system you cannot delegate. And it begins with the words you learned today: me duele la espalda is information you cannot act on until you can name it. Someone who ignores pain is not being tough; they are declining information about the one thing they are responsible for running. No se gobierna lo que no se sabe nombrar.
