★ En el consultorio — el guion de una consulta
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A medical appointment follows a script. Learn the script and you can hold your side of it.
Yesterday you learned to name what hurts. Today you learn the words around it — la fiebre, la tos, la receta — and something more useful than vocabulary: the predictable shape of a medical appointment in Spanish. Four moves, in the same order, nearly every time.

No new grammar today. Tomorrow brings the present perfect, which is the tense a doctor's questions are actually asked in.

I will be able to use ten health expressions, including la receta and enfermarse.
2

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Three ways to be unwell, and Spanish keeps them apart

English blurs these; Spanish does not, and picking the wrong one is immediately noticeable:

  • tener + a symptom you possess — tengo fiebre, tengo tos, tengo dolor de cabeza
  • estar + a condition you are in right now — estoy resfriado, estoy enfermo, estoy mareado
  • enfermarse — the moment of becoming ill — me enfermé el jueves
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which of the three feels least natural to you as an English speaker?

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Two false-friend traps. La receta means both prescription and recipe — context decides, and a Spanish speaker finds nothing odd about that. And sano means healthy as a lasting quality (una dieta sana, un niño sano), whereas estar bien is how you say you feel fine today. Do not use sano to mean “I'm feeling better.”
🧠 PART 3 · EL GUION DE UNA CONSULTA 9 min

Four moves, nearly always in this order

This is not grammar — it is the shape of a real interaction, and knowing it means you are never guessing what comes next.

MoveThe doctor saysYou say
1. La apertura¿Qué le pasa? / ¿En qué puedo ayudarle?Me duele… / Tengo…
2. Los síntomas¿Desde cuándo? ¿Tiene fiebre?Desde el lunes. Sí, tengo tos también.
3. El diagnósticoEs un resfriado. No es grave.¿Es contagioso? ¿Cuánto dura?
4. El tratamientoLe doy una receta. Descanse y beba agua.¿Cada cuántas horas? Gracias, doctora.

Notice the doctor addresses you as usted — ¿qué le pasa?, ¿tiene fiebre? — which is standard in a professional setting throughout the Spanish-speaking world regardless of your age. Note also move 4: descanse, beba. Those are commands, and you will own them in Module 3. Recognise them today; you are not asked to produce them yet.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 8 min
✅ TRY IT · tener or estar?
«No puedo ir a clase porque ___ resfriada.»
✅ TRY IT · the false friend
Your doctor hands you una receta. In this context, what is it?
✅ TRY IT · which move is this?
The doctor says: «¿Desde cuándo le duele la garganta?» Which stage of the appointment?

Ahora tú: play the patient. Answer these three aloud in Spanish — ¿Qué le pasa? ¿Desde cuándo? ¿Tiene fiebre?

—Buenos días, doctora. Me duele mucho la garganta y tengo tos.
—Desde el domingo, más o menos. Tres días.
—Sí, un poco de fiebre anoche, y estoy muy cansado.

Notice what the model does not do: it does not answer in single words. Even at move 2, a patient gives a small amount of extra information — tres días, y estoy muy cansado. That habit is what the Honors Tarea oral rewards.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La cita del lunes

—Buenos días. ¿Qué le pasa?
—Buenos días, doctora. Me duele la garganta y tengo tos desde el domingo.
—¿Tiene fiebre?
—Anoche sí, treinta y ocho. Hoy estoy mejor, pero muy cansado.
—¿Y el estómago? ¿Come normal?
—Casi nada. No tengo hambre.
—Bien. Es un resfriado fuerte, no es grave. Le doy una receta para la tos. Descanse tres días y beba mucha agua. Si sigue con fiebre el jueves, vuelva al consultorio.
—Muchas gracias, doctora.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find all four moves from Part 3 in this dialogue and mark where each begins. Then count how many of the ten new words appear — there are six. Finally notice the doctor's last three verbs: descanse, beba, vuelva. Commands, in the usted form. You will build those in Module 3, and when you do, you will already have heard them working.
🌎 CULTURA · MÉXICO
La farmacia de la esquina

In Mexico, and across much of Latin America, the pharmacist is a more central figure in everyday health than in the United States. Many pharmacies have a consultorio attached — a small doctor's office, often charging a very low fee — so that a minor complaint can be seen and prescribed for in one visit without an appointment. Chains like Farmacias Similares built their model on precisely this.

Two consequences worth knowing. A Mexican friend may say «voy a la farmacia» and mean I'm going to see a doctor. And the word consultorio you learned today is more likely to name a small storefront office than a hospital wing — which is why it is the word this module teaches rather than hospital.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA TEMPLANZA

The patient in today's dialogue does something temperate and easy to miss: they answer honestly. Not estoy bien out of politeness, and not a dramatised account either — just what is true, including hoy estoy mejor. Temperance is as much about not exaggerating as about not indulging. Decir lo que hay, sin adornarlo ni esconderlo.
