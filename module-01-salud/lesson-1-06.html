⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Where health paragraphs go wrong

Not in the verbs. In the drift: a student starts in the present perfect, slips into the preterite for no reason halfway through, and the reader loses track of whether the illness is over. The tense is doing work — if it changes, something must have changed.

So today's discipline is: choose your frame first, then hold it, and change only on purpose.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
You are describing an illness you still have. Which frame should the paragraph mostly sit in?
🧠 PART 2 · LA ARQUITECTURA DEL PÁRRAFO 10 min

A shape for describing a health situation

SentenceJobTenseUseful opener
1State the situation nowperfectoÚltimamente no me he sentido…
2When it startedpretéritoMe enfermé el…
3–4What you have done about itperfectoHe tomado… / He descansado…
5What you have not done yetperfectoTodavía no he…
6Background — how it usually isimperfectoNormalmente no me enfermaba…
7–8Where things stand / what is nextperfecto or presentHasta ahora… / Voy a…

Sentence 2 is the only compulsory preterite: the moment the illness began is a closed event even though the illness is not over. That single switch is what makes the paragraph read as competent rather than mechanical.

⭐ LA REGLA DE LOS CONECTORES · carried from Module 0
Do not reuse a connector. You have plenty that suit health writing: últimamente · desde entonces · por eso · aunque · sin embargo · hasta ahora · menos mal que · al final. Four or five different ones is enough for eight sentences.
🧠 PART 3 · MODELO ANOTADO 10 min

Estas dos semanas

1 Últimamente no me he sentido bien. 2 Me enfermé el domingo pasado, después de un partido bajo la lluvia. 3 Desde entonces he tenido tos y dolor de garganta, y dos noches he tenido fiebre. 4 He descansado todo lo posible y he bebido mucha agua con limón, porque en mi casa siempre usamos remedios así. 5 Sin embargo, todavía no he ido al consultorio: mi madre ha pedido una cita para el viernes. 6 Normalmente no me enfermaba casi nunca; de niño era el más sano de la familia. 7 Hasta ahora he faltado solamente un día a clase. 8 Menos mal que la fiebre ha bajado y ya me he despertado dos mañanas sin dolor.

Count the frames: ten present perfects carrying the open situation, one preterite for the moment it began (me enfermé), two imperfects for background (me enfermaba, era). Five different connectors, none repeated. That distribution is what the rubric rewards.

✅ TRY IT · background tense
«Normalmente no ___ casi nunca.» (enfermarse — describing how things usually were)

Ahora tú — this is tomorrow's graded task, rehearsed. Write 6–8 sentences about your health in the last two weeks, following Part 2's shape. Keep it; you will revise rather than restart tomorrow.

Before you call it finished:
• Most sentences in the present perfect, with the frame held throughout.
• Exactly one preterite for the moment it began — no unexplained drift.
• At least one imperfect for background (normalmente…, de niño…).
• One todavía no and one ya or hasta ahora.
• Every pronoun in front of haber: me he sentido, never «he me sentido».
• Four or more different connectors, none repeated.
• Accents checked — including any leído / caído / oído.
• No irregular participles yet (hecho, dicho, visto arrive in Module 2) — stick to -ado and -ido.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La cocina de mi abuela es media farmacia

En casa de mi abuela, en Puebla, hay un estante que no es de comida. Hay manzanilla para el estómago, árnica para los golpes, hojas de eucalipto para la tos y miel para la garganta. Cuando alguien se enferma, ella prepara un té antes de preguntar si hace falta un médico.

Mi abuela no está en contra de los doctores — al contrario. Cuando mi primo se lastimó el brazo, ella misma llamó al consultorio. Pero para un resfriado, una tos o un dolor de estómago, primero prueba lo que aprendió de su madre, que lo aprendió de la suya. Dice que esos remedios «no curan todo, pero acompañan».

Yo he crecido entre las dos cosas y no me parecen contrarias. He tomado antibióticos cuando los he necesitado, y he tomado té de manzanilla casi toda mi vida.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Look at the last paragraph: four present perfects in three lines, and every one of them is about a life still in progress — he crecido, he tomado. Then look at the middle paragraph and notice it is preterite (se lastimó, llamó, aprendió), because those are closed events. The writer switches frames on purpose, exactly as Part 2 asks you to.
🌎 CULTURA · MÉXICO
«No curan todo, pero acompañan»

The grandmother's line is the most useful thing in today's lesson for understanding this culture. La medicina tradicional in Mexico is generally not framed as an alternative to hospital medicine or a rejection of it — it is what you do alongside, and often first, for ordinary complaints.

The plants named are real and widely used: manzanilla (chamomile) for digestion, árnica for bruises, eucalipto for coughs. Much of this knowledge descends from Nahua and Maya practice that long predates 1519, and it survived partly in kitchens rather than in books — passed between women across generations, exactly as the reading describes.

On Day 8 you will read a real Spanish-language text about this, written by Spanish speakers for Spanish speakers rather than for learners.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA TEMPLANZA

The grandmother is temperate in a way worth naming: she does not overreach with her remedies (she phones the clinic for a hurt arm) and she does not abandon them either. Temperance is not a middle point between two beliefs — it is knowing the proper scope of each thing. Cada remedio tiene su medida; conocerla es la virtud.
