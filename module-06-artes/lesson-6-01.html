📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The average museum visitor looks at a painting for about seventeen seconds. This module asks you for three minutes.
Módulo 5 was about the natural world and how we look after it. Módulo 6 is about the made world: las artes, and one museum in particular — el Museo del Prado in Madrid.

The module's virtue is la atención, and art is where that virtue is easiest to practise and hardest to fake. You cannot skim a painting. Either you looked, or you did not.

Today: ten words for works of art and the people who make them. No new grammar — the module's grammar arrives on Day 3, and it is the single most useful thing this course will give your writing.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name ten kinds of artwork and artist with their correct articles.
2
I will be able to distinguish la obra, el cuadro and el lienzo, which English mostly collapses into one word.
3
I will be able to say what a work is, who made it, and where it is, in Spanish I already have.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Seventeen seconds

Museum researchers have measured how long people actually stand in front of a painting. The numbers vary by study, but they are all short — a matter of seconds, often fewer than thirty. Long enough to read the label. Not long enough to see anything.

That is not a failure of taste. It is a failure of attention, and attention is trainable. So is the vocabulary that makes it possible: you cannot notice la sombra or el marco or la mirada until you have words to hold them in. This module gives you twenty of those words and then a grammar that lets you say two things about one of them at once.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
English says “painting” for three different things; Spanish separates them, and the separation is genuinely useful.

La obra is the widest word — any work of art, of any kind, and also an artist's whole output: la obra de Velázquez. El cuadro is the physical framed thing hanging on the wall. El lienzo is the canvas itself, the woven cloth stretched on a frame — so a painter can stand frente al lienzo before there is any picture at all. And la pintura is either the paint in the tube or painting as an art form.

Two more. El marco also means “framework” in the abstract sense — el marco legal, the legal framework. And la mirada has no single English equivalent: it is the noun of mirar, so it covers a look, a gaze, an expression in the eyes. Una mirada triste, la mirada del pintor. Keep it; you will need it on Day 8.
🧠 PART 3 · QUÉ ES, DE QUIÉN ES, DÓNDE ESTÁ 8 min

Three facts, no new grammar

Before you can say anything interesting about a work, you have to be able to say the dull things about it. These five frames use only Spanish you have had since the first year.

To say…FrameExample
what kind of work it isser + kindLas meninas es un cuadro, no una escultura.
who made itser de + painterEl cuadro es de Velázquez.
who it showsun retrato de + personEs un retrato de la infanta Margarita.
where it isestar en + museumEstá en el Museo del Prado.
when it is fromser de + date or centuryEs de 1656, del siglo XVII.

Note that ser de is doing two jobs — authorship and date — and context separates them without difficulty, exactly as it does with origin (es de Sevilla).

🔄 REPASO DEL MÓDULO 5 · por y para
Art writing is full of por and para, and Módulo 5 gave you the contrast, so use it here rather than avoiding it:

Velázquez trabajó para el rey Felipe IV. — para: the person you work for
Pintó el cuadro para el palacio. — para: destination, purpose
Es famoso por su uso de la luz. — por: the reason, the cause
Pasó por Italia dos veces. — por: movement through

One pair worth holding onto: trabajar para alguien (you are their employee) against trabajar por alguien (you are covering their shift). Velázquez did the first for thirty-seven years.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Una tarde en el Prado

El Museo del Prado abrió sus puertas en 1819, en Madrid. El edificio no nació como museo: Juan de Villanueva lo diseñó para ser un gabinete de historia natural, y solo después se llenó de cuadros.

Hoy el Prado guarda más de ocho mil obras, pero solo una parte está en las salas. El resto espera en los depósitos, y las exposiciones temporales van sacando unas cuantas cada año.

Si tienes una sola tarde, no intentes verlo todo. La gente entra con un mapa, camina cuatro kilómetros y sale con dolor de pies y ningún recuerdo claro. Escoge cinco cuadros. Siéntate delante de cada uno cinco minutos. Mira el marco, las manos, la mirada, el fondo. Después lee la etiqueta, no antes.

Es un plan que parece pobre y funciona mucho mejor. Cinco obras vistas de verdad valen más que trescientas vistas de paso.

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
El Prado, y el pintor del rey

This module's country is España, and its two anchors are one museum and one painter.

The Museo del Prado opened to the public in 1819 under Fernando VII, built out of the Spanish royal collection — which is why it is unusually deep in a few painters rather than broad across all of them. It holds the great Spanish trio: El Greco (1541–1614), who worked in Toledo; Francisco de Goya (1746–1828); and Diego Velázquez.

Velázquez (1599–1660) was born in Seville and spent most of his working life as court painter to Felipe IV, whom he served for roughly thirty-seven years. He was not only a painter at court: by 1652 he had been given the post of aposentador mayor del palacio, an administrative job involving royal lodgings and furniture. Some of his time went on that instead of on painting, which is one reason his surviving output is relatively small.

The Prado holds close to fifty of his works — the largest gathering of Velázquez anywhere — including Las meninas, finished in 1656. You will spend Day 4 and Day 8 with that painting, and it will repay every minute.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA ATENCIÓN

This module's virtue is la atención — attentiveness. It is the least dramatic virtue on the list and the one the rest depend on: you cannot be just, or courteous, or careful about a text you have not actually looked at.

Today's version of it is small and concrete. Ten words, said aloud with their articles, slowly enough that you notice which ones you do not really know yet. Skimming a vocabulary list is the same failure as walking past a painting in seventeen seconds. Nadie quiere de verdad lo que no ha mirado despacio.
