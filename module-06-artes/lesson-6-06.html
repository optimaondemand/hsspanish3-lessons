⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Cuatro herramientas, un solo trabajo

The whole module rests on one idea, so read it once more before you use it: a relative pronoun lets you say two things about one noun in a single sentence instead of two. It is a combining tool. That is the entire Honors payoff — connected paragraphs are built out of sentences that carry more than one fact each.

📖 PART 2 · A LEER 11 min

Un museo hecho de gustos

Read it twice before looking at anything else on this page. First pass: what is the paragraph telling you about the museum? Second pass: mark every bold word and ask what it is attaching to what.

You already met most of these facts in English, in 6.01 and 6.02 — and that is deliberate. Today the facts are not the point. Knowing the content already frees you to watch the eight relative clauses that are holding it together, which is what you could not do in an unfamiliar text.

📖 LECTURA CULTURAL · EL MUSEO DEL PRADO
El Museo del Prado abrió sus puertas en 1819, pero no nació como los museos que conocemos hoy. Nació de la colección privada de los reyes de España, quienes durante tres siglos compraron y encargaron cuadros con un gusto muy personal. Eso explica lo que más sorprende al visitante actual: el Prado no cuenta la historia completa del arte europeo. Cuenta lo que le gustaba a una familia.

Felipe IV, cuyo reinado duró de 1621 a 1665, fue el rey que más pintura compró. Y el pintor cuyas obras llenan hoy la sala más visitada del museo trabajaba a su lado casi todos los días: Diego Velázquez (1599–1660), a quien el rey nombró pintor de cámara en 1623, cuando Velázquez tenía veinticuatro años.

Velázquez no era solo pintor. En la corte también colgaba los cuadros del palacio, organizaba habitaciones y viajaba a Italia para comprar obras. Lo que hoy nos parece una carrera doble era entonces lo normal: el pintor del rey vivía dentro del palacio y servía al rey en muchas cosas a la vez.

El Prado guarda además a los otros dos pintores cuyos nombres el mundo asocia con España. El Greco, cuya carrera empezó en Creta y pasó por Venecia antes de terminar en Toledo. Y Goya, cuyos cuadros del 2 y del 3 de mayo de 1808 son la memoria visual de la guerra contra Napoleón.

Y lo que casi nadie olvida al salir es la sala 12. Allí está Las meninas: más de tres metros de alto, colocado al fondo, de modo que el visitante entra y se encuentra de frente con la mirada de la infanta Margarita.

Ayuda de vocabulario — only after your second reading: encargar = to commission · el gusto = taste · el reinado = a reign · pintor de cámara = court painter, painter to the king's own household · colgar = to hang · guardar = here, to hold or to keep · de modo que = with the result that.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Three things the paragraph says, in case you want to check yourself. (1) The Prado was a private royal collection first and a public museum second, which is why it is uneven: overwhelming in some directions, thin in others. (2) Velázquez was not a visiting artist but a household official who happened to paint. (3) The three painters everyone associates with Spain — El Greco, Velázquez, Goya — are in one building, and two of them were never in fashion outside Spain until long after they died.
🧠 PART 3 · LA GRAMÁTICA EN EL TEXTO 9 min

Ocho relativas, ocho decisiones

En el textoUne…Por qué esa palabra y no otra
los museos que conocemos hoymuseos + los conocemosCosa, sin comas. English would say “the museums we know” and drop the word; Spanish cannot
los reyes de España, quienes compraron…los reyes + compraron cuadrosPersonas, y la información va entre comas — el terreno propio de quienes
lo que más sorprende al visitantenada — no hay sustantivo detrásSe refiere a una idea entera, no a una cosa. Intenta señalar el sustantivo: no existe
Felipe IV, cuyo reinado duró…Felipe IV + su reinadoConcuerda con reinado (masc. sing.). Felipe no manda en la concordancia
el pintor cuyas obras llenan la salael pintor + sus obrasConcuerda con obras (fem. pl.), aunque el poseedor es el pintor
a quien el rey nombró…Velázquez + el rey lo nombróDetrás de preposición y con persona: a quien
el rey que más pintura compróel rey + compró más pinturaSin comas: selecciona a un rey entre varios
El Greco, cuya carrera empezó…El Greco + su carreraConcuerda con carrera (fem. sing.)

And now the commas, which 6.04 Part 3 showed you in isolation. Here they are doing it inside a real paragraph, in two rows of that table. Los reyes de España, quienes compraron… adds a fact about all of them. El rey que más pintura compró, with no commas, sorts one king out of several — and the sentence would be false with commas, because there was more than one king. Same tool, same grammar; the commas carry the truth of the sentence. The names for the two kinds are explicativa (between commas) and especificativa (no commas), and you will use both in tomorrow's paragraph whether you name them or not.

⭐ UN TRUCO DE LECTURA
When a long Spanish sentence stops making sense, find the relative pronoun and put your finger on the noun immediately in front of it. That noun is what the whole clause is about. Ninety per cent of long-sentence confusion is attaching the clause to the wrong noun — and it is the one problem a dictionary cannot help you with.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · ¿hay un sustantivo detrás?
«___ más me llamó la atención del Prado fue el silencio de la sala 12.»
✅ TRY IT · concordancia de cuyo
«Goya es el pintor ___ cuadros del 3 de mayo están en el Prado.»
✅ TRY IT · lo que hacen las comas
Las dos oraciones son correctas: «Las obras que Velázquez pintó en Italia son pocas» y «Las obras, que Velázquez pintó en Italia, son pocas». ¿Cuál dice que todas las obras de las que hablamos se pintaron en Italia?

El borrador de Lengua en acción

Tomorrow's prompt, so you can write it today: Describe una obra del Museo del Prado y explica qué te hace mirarla dos veces. Six to eight connected sentences. Here is a shape that forces the module's grammar rather than allowing you to avoid it.

Sentences 3 and 5 are the assessed ones. A paragraph with no cuyo and no lo que can still be good Spanish and will still lose the criterion that this module exists to teach. Write those two first, then build around them.

Write it now, in Spanish, and keep it. Tomorrow you revise this — you do not start again.

Before you call it finished:
• At least three relative clauses, and at least one of each: a que, a cuyo-form, a lo que.
• Every cuyo checked against the thing possessed, not the person. Read the noun that follows it and nothing else.
• No que deleted anywhere. Reread any sentence that sounds like English with “that” left out.
• Every que followed by an ordinary present or past verb — que tiene, que representa, que pintó.
• Commas used on purpose: added information between commas, sorting information without them.
• Four or more different connectors, none repeated.
• At least five words from this module's twenty.
• Accents read one at a time: Velázquez, la exposición, la época, el significado.
🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
Lo que el Prado no tiene

You know from 6.01 that the Prado grew out of the royal collection. Here is the consequence nobody mentions on the way in: a museum built from what one family liked has holes in it, and the holes are as informative as the walls.

Most great national galleries were assembled later, by committees deliberately buying to fill gaps in a survey of European art. The Prado was not. So its strengths are ferocious and narrow — Titian and the Venetians, because Carlos V and Felipe II collected them personally; Rubens and Flemish painting, because the Spanish crown ruled Flanders; Velázquez and Goya, because both were employed on the premises. And its absences are just as systematic. Dutch seventeenth-century painting, the great rival tradition of exactly the same decades, is thinly represented, because Spanish kings were at war with the Dutch Republic and were not buying its Protestant merchants' pictures. Nineteenth-century French painting and Impressionism barely register, because by then the royal collection had stopped being the engine.

That is worth knowing before you write about a Spanish painting, because it changes what “the Prado” means. It is not a survey of art. It is the visible taste of one dynasty, and every gap in it is a war, a marriage or a preference.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA ATENCIÓN

A relative clause makes a specific demand on a reader: hold this noun in mind while a second fact about it arrives. Two sentences do not ask that. One sentence with a relative clause does — and that is why the long-sentence trouble in Part 3 is an attention problem rather than a vocabulary problem. You already noticed today that when a sentence collapsed, the fix was to look again at which noun the clause belonged to. La atención begins there, not in the museum: in the willingness to reread rather than to guess. Mirar dos veces no es mirar más: es mirar de otra manera.
