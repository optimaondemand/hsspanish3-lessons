Lesson 6.05
★ Lo que y cuyo — la idea entera y el dueño
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two more relatives. One you will use every week for the rest of your life in Spanish; the other you mainly need to be able to read.
Lo que is the useful one, and it is genuinely useful — it is how Spanish says “what” in the middle of a sentence. «Lo que más me impresiona es la luz.» You will confuse it with que at first, and Part 2 gives you a one-second test that settles it.

Cuyo means “whose”, and it does something no English speaker expects: it agrees with the thing owned, not with the owner. It is also formal and mostly written, so the honest goal is that you recognise it reliably and produce it once or twice — not that you force it into speech.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to choose between que and lo que by asking whether there is a noun in front.
2
I will be able to use lo que to refer back to a whole idea, not just a noun.
3
I will be able to make cuyo agree with the thing possessed, and recognise it in formal writing.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

One word of difference

El detalle que más me impresiona es la mirada de la niña.
Lo que más me impresiona es la mirada de la niña.

Both sentences are correct, and they are not the same sentence. The first says a detail impresses you and then names it. The second names no category at all — it points at whatever it is, an impression with no noun attached.

Look at what actually differs: the first has the noun el detalle sitting in front of que. The second has nothing in front, so lo steps in to hold the place. That is the whole distinction, and Part 2 turns it into a test you can run in one second.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
In English, which word would you use to start “___ impresses me most is the light”?
🧠 PART 2 · LO QUE · UNA IDEA ENTERA 12 min

The one-second test

💡 ¿HAY UN SUSTANTIVO DELANTE?
Yes → que. The clause describes that noun.
  el cuadro que vi · la luz que entra · el pintor que trabajó allí

No → lo que. There is no noun, so the clause refers to a thing, a fact or an idea you are not naming.
  Lo que vi · Lo que más me impresiona · Lo que hizo Velázquez

That is it. One question, one second, and the choice is settled every time.

Lo que then does three jobs, and all three are worth having:

JobExampleEnglish
1. Subject, opening the sentenceLo que más me impresiona es la luz.What impresses me most…
2. Object of a verbNo entiendo lo que hace el hombre del fondo.…what the man is doing
3. Referring back to a whole clauseSe pintó a sí mismo, lo que era poco frecuente.…which was unusual
After a prepositionPiensa en lo que ves, no en la etiqueta.Think about what you see…
  • Lo que más me impresiona es la luz. El pintor cuyas obras vimos.listen for «cuyas» agreeing with «obras» — not with the painter

Job 3 is the one that most improves an Honors paragraph. English does it with a comma and “which”; Spanish does it with a comma and lo que, and it lets you comment on the sentence you have just written without starting a new one.

✏️ NO CONFUNDIR · lo que y qué
English uses “what” for both of these. Spanish does not.

Pregunta → qué, with a tilde: ¿Qué te impresiona más?
Afirmación → lo que, no tilde: Lo que más me impresiona es la luz.

And in a reported question the two can sit side by side, which is worth seeing once: Me preguntó qué veía (reporting the question) against Me dijo lo que veía (reporting the answer). Same tilde diacrítica logic as dé/de from Módulo 3 — the accent marks the question word.
🧠 PART 3 · CUYO · EL DUEÑO Y LO POSEÍDO 11 min

It agrees with the wrong thing — on purpose

Cuyo means “whose” and joins two sentences that share an owner. Everything about it is straightforward except one point, which catches everybody: it does not agree with the owner. It agrees with the thing owned — the noun that comes immediately after it.

el pintor cuyas obras vimos
↑ not cuyo (pintor, m.s.) but cuyas — because of obras, feminine plural, which follows it

El dueñoLo poseídoFormaEjemplo
el pintor (m.s.)las obras (f.pl.)cuyasel pintor cuyas obras vimos
la infanta (f.s.)el retrato (m.s.)cuyola infanta cuyo retrato está en el centro
el museo (m.s.)los cuadros (m.pl.)cuyosel museo cuyos cuadros son famosos
los pintores (m.pl.)la obra (f.s.)cuyalos pintores cuya obra estudiamos

Read the last row twice. The owner is masculine plural and the form is feminine singular, and it is right. Look to the right of cuyo, never to the left.

⚠️ TRES COSAS MÁS, Y UNA ES UNA PROHIBICIÓN
1. Nunca lleva artículo. The noun after cuyo goes bare:
  el pintor cuyas obras vimos · el pintor cuyas las obras vimos

2. Nunca es una pregunta. To ask “whose is this?” Spanish uses ¿De quién…?:
  ¿De quién es este cuadro? · ¿Cuyo es este cuadro?
  This is genuinely different from English, where “whose” does both jobs.

3. Es formal, y casi solo escrito. Nobody says cuyo in conversation. In speech people split the sentence or go round it:
  formal: el pintor cuyas obras vimos ayer
  hablado: el pintor de quien vimos las obras ayer, or simply two sentences.

So the reasonable goal is this: recognise cuyo every time you read it — museum labels, catalogues and essays are full of it — and produce it once or twice in the module's written work, where formality is appropriate. Do not go hunting for chances to say it aloud.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 7 min
✅ TRY IT · the one-second test
«___ más me gusta de este cuadro es el perro.»
✅ TRY IT · cuyo looks to the right
«Velázquez es el pintor ___ obras llenan tres salas del Prado.»
✅ TRY IT · commenting on a whole idea
«Velázquez se pintó a sí mismo dentro del cuadro, ___ era poco frecuente en su época.»
✅ TRY IT · asking, not stating
You are standing in front of an unlabelled painting and want to ask who painted it. What do you say?

Ahora tú: write three sentences about any painting or photograph you know — one opening with lo que, one using lo que to comment on a whole clause, and one with cuyo in the right form. Write all three before revealing.

Lo que más me llama la atención de esta foto es la sombra de la pared. Mi abuelo se puso el traje de los domingos para la foto, lo que no era normal en él. Es un retratista cuyas fotografías todavía cuelgan en media familia.

Check yours: no noun in front of either lo que · the second lo que after a comma, pointing back at the whole clause before it · cuyas agreeing with fotografías and not with retratista · and no article after cuyas.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Notas de sala — sala 12

Lo que casi nadie espera al entrar en la sala 12 es el tamaño. En las reproducciones Las meninas parece un cuadro normal; delante del original, las figuras son casi del tamaño de una persona, y el suelo del cuarto pintado parece continuar el suelo del museo.

Lo que Velázquez hizo aquí no tiene muchos antecedentes. Un pintor de corte, cuyo trabajo consistía en retratar a los demás, se colocó a sí mismo dentro del cuadro, con el pincel en la mano y la mirada hacia fuera. Se pintó trabajando, lo que en 1656 era poco frecuente.

Y hay un detalle que muchos visitantes pasan por alto: la infanta, cuya figura ocupa el centro de la composición, es también la figura más iluminada. Todo lo demás — las meninas, el perro, el hombre de la puerta — está en penumbra o a media luz. La luz no es decorativa: organiza el cuadro.

Lo que discuten los especialistas no es esto, sino el espejo del fondo. Pero eso ya lo sabes.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Four lo que, two cuyo and one plain relative que in twelve lines — the ordinary density of a museum text, which is why the Vocabulary Check and the Grammar Quiz both draw on writing like this.

Check the two cuyo forms against the rule: «un pintor cuyo trabajo» — masculine singular, agreeing with trabajo, not with pintor. «la infanta cuya figura» — feminine singular, agreeing with figura. Both times, look right.

Then find the one plain que: «un detalle que muchos visitantes pasan por alto». There is a noun in front — un detalle — so lo is not needed. The test works on real prose, not only on exercises.
🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
El cuadro que otros pintores no dejan en paz

Some paintings are admired. A very small number get argued with by other painters, and Las meninas is the clearest case in Spanish art.

In 1957, Pablo Picasso — Spanish, born in Málaga in 1881 — worked through the painting in a single sustained run of a few months and produced 58 variations on it: the whole composition, then individual figures, then the dog on its own. He kept the series together and gave it to the Museu Picasso in Barcelona, where it still is. He was seventy-five, and he was rewriting a canvas painted three hundred years before he was born.

Now a licensing point, because it explains something about this course you may have noticed. Velázquez is in the public domain — his work is centuries out of copyright — which is why the Museo del Prado publishes his paintings in high resolution for anyone to download and use freely. Picasso died in 1973 and his work is still under copyright, so we can describe his 58 meninas in words but cannot reproduce them here.

That is not a technicality. It is why the images you meet in school skew heavily towards art made before about 1930, and it is worth knowing that the gap exists rather than assuming the older work is simply the more important.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA ATENCIÓN

Notice what «lo que más me impresiona es…» actually demands of you. It is a frame with one slot, and it will not accept a list. You have to decide which single thing mattered most and say it out loud.

That is harder than describing everything, and it is a better test of whether you looked. Vague admiration costs nothing; naming the one detail commits you. Quien no sabe decir lo que más importa, todavía no ha mirado bastante.
