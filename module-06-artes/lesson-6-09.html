★ Tarea oral — una obra, un detalle, una razón
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You cannot describe a detail you have not looked at.
Today you record the Tarea oral (20 pts) — 20% of your semester grade. One named painting, one detail inside it, and the reason that detail holds you. Then one direct instruction to whoever is listening.

The specific difficulty today is not grammar, it is breathing. A relative clause is long, and spoken relative clauses fall apart when the speaker does not pause where the commas are — or pauses where there are none. Part 4 is about landing them out loud.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to speak for 60–90 seconds about one work of art, using at least three relative clauses.
2
I will be able to pause where a relative clause needs a pause, so a listener keeps hold of the main sentence.
3
I will be able to justify a judgement about a painting by pointing at something visible in it.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Un minuto entero, mirando

Before anything else: open a good image of your chosen painting and look at it for one full minute without writing. Time it. It will feel much longer than it is, and that reaction is the whole reason this module's virtue is la atención.

There is research on how long museum visitors actually stand in front of a painting, and the answer is unflattering: typically well under a minute, often a few seconds, famous works included. A minute is already unusual. Two minutes and you will start noticing things nobody told you about — which is exactly the material a good recording is made of.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
You have just looked for a minute. What did you notice that you had not noticed before?
🎤 PART 2 · LA TAREA 9 min

El tema: Elige una obra concreta del Museo del Prado. Describe qué representa, di cuál es el detalle que más te llama la atención y explica por qué. Termina con un mandato directo a quien te escucha: dile qué tiene que mirar primero.

Una obra concreta, con su nombre. Not “a painting in the Prado” and not “Spanish art”. Any of these works, all of them in the Prado and all freely reproducible:

🧠 PART 3 · TU GUION DE SEIS PALABRAS 9 min

Same method as every module: six words, not a text. A read script always sounds read, and here it costs you twice — a written relative clause read aloud comes out flat and unpaused, which is precisely the thing Part 4 is trying to fix.

BeatYour wordExampleLa herramienta
1. La obra, con su nombre meninasUna relativa sin comas
2. Qué representa infantarepresentar, el retrato
3. El pintor y algo suyo tallercuyo / cuya / cuyos / cuyas
4. El detalle espejola mirada, la luz, la sombra, el marco
5. Lo que te hace pensar nosotroslo que…

Beats 3, 5 and 6 are all separately assessed. Write those three words first. A recording with no cuyo, no lo que or no command loses named criteria however good the rest of it is.

⭐ ENSAYA TRES VECES
Say the whole thing aloud three times from your six words. On run three, listen for two things only: whether your cuyo agreed with the noun straight after it, and whether your longest sentence had a pause in it anywhere. Those are the two things a listener will notice, and they are the two things the rubric weighs most.
🔊 PART 4 · PRONUNCIACIÓN Y RESPIRACIÓN 11 min

Click each and repeat aloud.

  • la exposiciónstress on the last syllable
  • la épocaÉ-po-ca — stress on the first
  • el significadofive syllables, none of them rushed
  • la miradathe -d- is soft, almost English «th»
  • el lienzo-nzo, not -nso
  • la obratwo syllables: O-bra

1. The -ción words carry their stress at the end. la exposiCIÓN. And notice what happens in the plural: las exposiciones loses the written accent, because the stress is no longer on the final syllable. Same word, same stress pattern by ear, different spelling — the accent was only ever marking the exception.

2. La época is the trap in this set. English speakers say e-PO-ca, following the default. The tilde is telling you the stress sits on the first syllable, three from the end: É-po-ca. Exaggerate it more than feels reasonable.

3. Cuyo is two syllables: CU-yo. Not “kwai-oh”. The y sounds like the y of English “yes”, and the u is a plain Spanish u. And lo que runs together fast and unstressed, almost one word: loque.

4. And the one that decides your comprehensibility score: pause where the commas are. Compare these two aloud:

OraciónCómo suena
El cuadro que está en la sala 12 es enorme.Sin comas → un solo grupo, de un tirón, sin pausa
Velázquez, quien vivía en el palacio, se pintó dentro del cuadro.Con comas → tres grupos, con dos pausas pequeñas

The commas are instructions for your voice. Ignore them and the listener hears one long undifferentiated stream and loses which verb belongs to Velázquez. Put a pause where no comma exists and you split a noun off from the clause that identifies it. Neither is an accent problem; both are comprehension problems, and both are fixed by reading your outline aloud once with the pauses marked.

✅ TRY IT · sin pausas
You say «Velázquez quien vivía en el palacio se pintó dentro del cuadro» in one breath, with no pauses at all. What is the risk?
✅ TRY IT · what the rubric weighs
Two takes. One has a strong foreign accent but three clean relative clauses, a specific detail and a closing command. The other is smooth and fast but is a list of separate facts with no relative clause in it. Which scores higher?
⏺ PART 5 · GRABA Y ENTREGA 6 min

Record in Canvas on the assignment. Listen once before submitting and check four things:

  1. Is there a cuyo-form, and did it agree with the noun straight after it?
  2. Is there a lo que, referring to an idea rather than a thing?
  3. Did you end with a command to your listener?
  4. Did you reach 60 seconds? Short takes are almost always missing beat 5 — the reason.

If all four pass, submit. If you have time for one more take, do it for the pauses rather than for the words.

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
El cuarto que ya no existe

Something to know before you describe the room in Las meninas: that room is gone.

It was in the Real Alcázar de Madrid, the old royal palace, where Velázquez lived and worked. On Christmas Eve 1734 the Alcázar caught fire and burned for days. Much of the royal collection was lost. Las meninas was carried out — accounts say it had to be cut from its frame to get it clear — and it was damaged in the process and restored afterwards. The inventory drawn up of what had survived is the 1734 document that gives the painting its other name.

The palace was not rebuilt. The Palacio Real that stands on the site today is a different building, put up over the following decades in a different style. So the tall shadowed room in the painting, with its picture-hung walls and its open door at the back, exists nowhere except in the painting.

Which changes what you are looking at. It is not a picture of a room you could go and stand in; it is the only surviving view of a space that burned seventy-eight years after it was painted, saved by whoever thought to carry that canvas out first. When you record your ninety seconds, that is worth one sentence — and it is the kind of thing la atención finds: not in the painting, but in what the painting turns out to be for.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA ATENCIÓN

Ninety seconds of speech needs about two minutes of looking, and there is no way around that arithmetic. Students who record a weak version of this task have almost never run out of Spanish — they have run out of things they noticed. That is why Part 1 asked for a full minute with nothing to write: attention has to come first and it cannot be hurried at the end. No se puede describir lo que no se ha mirado.
