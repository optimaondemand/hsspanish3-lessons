★ Describir una obra — la luz, la sombra y el detalle
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
«Es bonito» is not a description. It is a way of ending the conversation.
Yesterday you learned what to call the objects. Today you learn what to say about them — the ten words that let you name light, shadow, style, detail and meaning instead of stopping at bonito.

You also get a four-step shape that works on any work of art, in any language: what it is, what you see, how it is painted, what you make of it. Follow it and you cannot run out of things to say.

Still no new grammar. Tomorrow's lesson is the one that changes how your paragraphs read.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use ten words for how a work is made and what it means.
2
I will be able to choose correctly between representar and retratar.
3
I will be able to describe any painting in four steps without running out of things to say.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Two students, one painting

«Me gusta. Es muy bonito.»
«La luz entra por la izquierda y deja la mitad del cuarto en sombra. La niña del centro es la única figura completamente iluminada.»

The second student is not more artistic than the first. She has more nouns. La luz, la sombra, la mitad, la figura — with those four words the sentence writes itself, and without them there is nowhere to go after bonito.

This is generally true of description in a foreign language. What limits you is almost never grammar. It is the absence of a word for the thing you can plainly see.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Representar y retratar are the pair worth getting right. Retratar comes from el retrato and takes a person: Velázquez retrató a la infanta muchas veces. Representar takes a scene or an idea: el cuadro representa una escena del palacio. Representar also means to stand for something, and to perform a play.

Exponer conjugates exactly like poner — expongo, expuso, and the irregular participle expuesto, from Módulo 2's list. So «el cuadro ha estado expuesto en Madrid desde 1819». It also means to set out an argument: exponer una idea.

Apreciar has a second sense English speakers never expect: to make out, to be able to perceive. «Apenas se aprecia la firma en la esquina» — you can barely make out the signature. Careful, though: to thank someone you use agradecer, not apreciar.

And el significado is the meaning of a word or a work; el sentido is a sense or a direction. A museum label discusses el significado.
🧠 PART 3 · LOS CUATRO PASOS 9 min

Four steps, always in this order

The order matters more than the content. Most people begin at step 4 — the opinion — and then have nothing left. Begin at step 1 and the opinion arrives with something under it.

PasoLa preguntaEjemplo
1. Identificar¿Qué es, de quién y de cuándo?Es un retrato de Velázquez, de la época de Felipe IV.
2. Describir¿Qué se ve?El cuadro representa a una niña en un cuarto grande.
3. Analizar¿Cómo está pintado?La luz entra por la izquierda y el fondo queda en sombra.
4. Interpretar¿Qué te parece a ti?Para mí, el significado está en la mirada de la niña.

Step 3 is where the module's vocabulary earns its place, and it is the step students skip. It is also the step that makes step 4 believable: an opinion about a painting is only worth reading if it points at something in the painting.

🔄 REPASO DEL MÓDULO 5 · por y para en el paso 3
Step 3 asks why the painter did that, and that question runs on por and para. Use them:

El pintor dejó el fondo oscuro para destacar la figura. — para + infinitive: purpose
La luz entra por la izquierda. — por: through, along
Por la sombra del fondo, el cuarto parece más profundo. — por: cause
Pintó este retrato para el rey. — para: the recipient

If you can write those four sentence types about a painting, you have step 3 covered.

Ahora tú: take any image you have to hand and write four sentences — one for each step, in order. Use at least three words from today's list and one por or para.

Es una fotografía de mi tía, de la época en que trabajaba en Sevilla. Representa a una mujer joven delante de una puerta blanca. La luz viene de arriba y le deja media cara en sombra. Para mí, el significado está en el detalle de las manos: están cerradas, y el resto de la cara está tranquila.

Check yours: all four steps, in order · at least one sentence in step 3 about light, shadow or detail · la época with its tilde · and representar for a scene rather than retratar.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Lo que se ve en Las meninas

Un cuarto grande y en penumbra, en el antiguo Alcázar de Madrid. En el centro, una niña de unos cinco años, vestida de blanco y plata: la infanta Margarita. A su lado, dos meninas — sus damas de compañía — y una de ellas está arrodillada y le ofrece algo en una bandeja.

A la derecha, dos personas de baja estatura, Mari Bárbola y Nicolasito, y un perro grande y tranquilo. Detrás, en la sombra, dos figuras que conversan. Al fondo, una puerta abierta y un hombre en el umbral, con la luz a la espalda.

Y a la izquierda, delante de un lienzo enorme, está el pintor. Es Velázquez, con el pincel en la mano, y no mira a la niña: mira hacia fuera del cuadro, hacia ti.

En la pared del fondo hay un espejo, y en el espejo aparecen dos figuras más. Casi todos los especialistas las identifican como el rey Felipe IV y la reina Mariana de Austria. Lo demás — dónde están exactamente esas dos personas, y qué refleja el espejo en realidad — se discute desde hace siglos. Mañana vuelves a este cuarto.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
This paragraph deliberately stops after step 2 and half of step 3. It tells you what is there and almost nothing about what it means — because the meaning is the argument, and the argument is Day 8.

Two things to notice for tomorrow. First, count the figures: with Velázquez himself, the reflection and the man in the doorway, there are eleven people in the room. Second, look at how often the paragraph has to say «una de ellas está arrodillada y le ofrece algo» in two clumsy halves. Tomorrow you learn the one word that joins them: «una de ellas, que está arrodillada, le ofrece algo».
🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
Un pintor que fue aclarando su paleta

If you walk the Velázquez rooms of the Prado in order, you can watch the light change.

His earliest surviving works are Sevillian bodegones — kitchen and tavern scenes, painted before he was twenty: dark backgrounds, hard edges, a single strong light falling across a jug or an egg. That heavy darkness has a Spanish name, el tenebrismo, from las tinieblas (darkness), and it was the fashionable manner across southern Europe at the time.

Velázquez reached the court of Felipe IV in Madrid in 1623, and afterwards made two long journeys to Italia — the first in 1629–1631, the second in 1649–1651 — where he saw Venetian painting at first hand. His later work is lighter, looser and far less interested in hard outlines. Stand close to a late Velázquez and the brushstrokes come apart into marks; step back and the cloth reassembles itself.

That is a real change in estilo, visible in one afternoon in one building, and it is the sort of thing you can only see if you compare deliberately rather than drift.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA ATENCIÓN

Notice the order the four steps impose. You are not allowed to reach your opinion until you have said what is actually there — and that restraint is what la atención mostly consists of. Not concentration exactly. More a willingness to postpone the verdict.

Today's reading obeys the same discipline: it describes eleven people and one mirror, and refuses to tell you what any of it means. Quien nombra lo que ve, ve el doble.
