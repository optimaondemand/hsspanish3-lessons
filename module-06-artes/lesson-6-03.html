Lesson 6.03
★ Que y quien — dos oraciones en una
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
This is the lesson that turns a list of short sentences into a paragraph.
Up to now your Spanish writing has probably been made of complete, correct, separate sentences. That is exactly what Spanish 2 asked for. It is also why Honors writing sounds young: a paragraph of short sentences reads like a list, no matter how accurate each one is.

A relative pronoun is the repair. It lets you say two things about one noun inside a single sentence — and that is the whole idea. Everything today follows from it.

One warning up front, because it is the error English speakers make most: English can drop the word “that”. Spanish can never drop que.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to join two sentences about the same noun into one using que.
2
I will be able to explain why que can never be left out, even where English leaves out “that”.
3
I will be able to use quien and quienes after a preposition, with the preposition in front.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Two sentences, one noun

Vi un cuadro en el Prado. El cuadro es de Velázquez.
↓
El cuadro que vi en el Prado es de Velázquez.

Look at what happened. The noun el cuadro appeared twice; the second mention was replaced by que; and the two sentences became one, with one main statement (es de Velázquez) and one piece of extra information tucked inside it.

That is the entire mechanism. The rest of today is which word goes in the slot, and the rest of the module is doing it well.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which version would you be more likely to write, right now, without thinking about it?
🧠 PART 2 · QUE · EL RELATIVO POR DEFECTO 12 min

One word where English has four

English distributes this job across that, which, who and whom, and then makes you choose. Spanish uses que for all four. It does not change for gender, it does not change for number, and it works for people and for things alike.

Dos oracionesUna sola
Vi un cuadro. El cuadro es de Velázquez.El cuadro que vi es de Velázquez.
El pintor era sevillano. El pintor trabajó en Madrid.El pintor que trabajó en Madrid era sevillano.
La luz entra por la izquierda. La luz ilumina a la niña.La luz que entra por la izquierda ilumina a la niña.
Compré un libro. El libro habla del Prado.El libro que compré habla del Prado.
Las meninas acompañan a la infanta. Son dos.Las dos meninas que acompañan a la infanta…
  • el cuadro que vi; el pintor con quien estudiónotice there is no pause before «que» — it glues, it does not separate

And que does not care which grammatical job the noun does inside its own clause. Four combinations, one word:

The noun is the subject of its clauseThe noun is the object of its clause
Cosasel cuadro que está en el Pradoel cuadro que vi ayer
Personasel pintor que trabajó allíel pintor que conocí ayer
⚠️ LA REGLA MÁS IMPORTANTE DEL MÓDULO
English lets you throw the word away:
  the painting that I saw  ·  the painting I saw — both correct

Spanish does not:
  el cuadro que vi  ·  el cuadro vi — the second is not Spanish at all

The test, and it is quick: if you can slip “that” into your English sentence and it still works, Spanish requires que.

“The book I bought” → “the book that I bought” works → el libro que compré
“The painter he admired” → “the painter whom he admired” works → el pintor que admiraba

This single habit removes more errors from Honors writing than anything else in the module.

One further note, small but worth having now: que as a relative pronoun never carries an accent. Qué with a tilde is the question word — ¿Qué cuadro? — and it is a different word doing a different job. Same tilde diacrítica family as dé/de and tú/tu from Módulo 3.

🧠 PART 3 · QUIEN Y QUIENES 9 min

Only people, and mostly after a preposition

Quien (plural quienes) refers only to people. It has two ordinary homes, and outside them que is the better choice.

Where quien belongsExample
1. After a preposition — the main useel rey para quien Velázquez trabajó
la infanta de quien hablamos ayer
los maestros con quienes estudió en Sevilla
2. Inside commas, adding informationVelázquez, quien llegó a Madrid en 1623, tenía 24 años.
💡 LA PREPOSICIÓN VA DELANTE, SIEMPRE
English parks its prepositions at the end of the clause. Spanish never does.

“the king he worked for” → el rey para quien trabajó
“the girl we were talking about” → la niña de quien hablábamos
“the friend he went with” → el amigo con quien fue

If your English has a stranded preposition, move it to the front before you translate. This is the second half of today's lesson and it is worth as much as the first.
⚠️ DÓNDE NO VA QUIEN
el pintor quien trabajó para el rey → el pintor que trabajó para el rey

This is the trap. English “the painter who worked…” tempts you into quien, but in a clause with no commas and no preposition, Spanish wants que — even for a person.

The reassuring version of the rule: if you default to que for people, you will almost never be wrong. Quien is the specialist; que is the workhorse. Learn quien properly for the two places it belongs, and let que handle everything else.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +4 min · el que / la que
After a preposition, quien handles people. For things in the same position, Spanish uses el que / la que / los que / las que, which agree with the noun:

la sala en la que está el cuadro · el museo del que hablamos · los años en los que vivió en Italia

Two easier alternatives exist and you may prefer them for now: la sala donde está el cuadro for places, and simply en que for time (la época en que pintó). Recognise en la que when you read it; produce it only if it feels comfortable. It is not tested in this module.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 9 min
✅ TRY IT · join the two sentences
«La exposición termina en marzo. La exposición está en el Prado.» Which single sentence says both?
✅ TRY IT · the word English throws away
“The portrait she painted in 1656 is in Madrid.”
✅ TRY IT · the preposition moves to the front
“Felipe IV is the king Velázquez worked for.”
✅ TRY IT · que or quien for a person?
«El pintor ___ retrató a la infanta se llamaba Velázquez.» No commas, no preposition.

Ahora tú: join each pair into one sentence. Write all three before revealing.
1. Compré un libro en el museo. El libro habla de Velázquez.
2. Conocí a una guía. La guía estudió en Madrid.
3. Velázquez pintó para un rey. Ese rey se llamaba Felipe IV.

1. El libro que compré en el museo habla de Velázquez.
2. La guía que conocí estudió en Madrid.
3. El rey para quien Velázquez pintó se llamaba Felipe IV.

Check yours: que present in 1 and 2, never dropped · que and not quien in 2, because there are no commas and no preposition · and in 3 the preposition para in front of quien, not stranded at the end.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Ficha de sala

Diego Velázquez (Sevilla, 1599 — Madrid, 1660)
La familia de Felipe IV, conocido como Las meninas, 1656
Óleo sobre lienzo, 320,5 × 281,5 cm

La niña que ocupa el centro del cuadro es la infanta Margarita, hija de Felipe IV y de Mariana de Austria. Las dos jóvenes que la acompañan son sus meninas, es decir, las damas jóvenes que servían a las infantas. De ellas viene el título, pero solo desde el siglo XIX: antes esta obra se llamaba simplemente La familia.

El hombre que aparece a la izquierda, con el pincel en la mano, es el propio pintor. Felipe IV, para quien Velázquez trabajó casi cuarenta años, aparece en el espejo del fondo junto a la reina.

Velázquez lleva en el pecho la cruz roja de la Orden de Santiago, un honor que recibió en 1659 — tres años después de terminar el cuadro. Cómo llegó esa cruz al lienzo es una de las preguntas que los especialistas todavía discuten.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Six relative que and one para quien in four short paragraphs. That density is normal in writing about art, and it is why this module pairs the two.

Two things to test yourself on. First, find the one where English would drop the word: «un honor que recibió en 1659» — “an honour he received in 1659”. In English the word vanishes; in Spanish it cannot. Second, notice that para quien is set inside commas and follows a preposition — both of quien's homes at once, which is why it is the natural choice there.
🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
La niña del centro, y por qué hay tantos retratos de ella

The child at the centre of Las meninas is Margarita Teresa (1651–1673), daughter of Felipe IV and Mariana de Austria. In 1656 she was about five.

There is a very practical reason so many portraits of her exist. From early childhood she was intended for her mother's brother, Leopoldo I, Holy Roman Emperor in Vienna — a marriage between uncle and niece, ordinary in the Habsburg family and eventually ruinous for it. Portraits of Margarita were sent to Vienna at intervals so the Austrian court could follow her growing up. That is why several Velázquez portraits of her hang today in the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna rather than in Madrid: they left Spain as diplomatic documents, long before anyone thought of them as museum pieces.

She married Leopoldo in 1666 and left Spain that same year. She died in Vienna in 1673, aged twenty-one. She had four children; one survived infancy.

Knowing that changes what you are looking at. The painting is a court scene, and it is also a picture of a five-year-old whose future had already been arranged by other people. None of that is visible on the canvas — which is a fair reminder that attention to a painting and knowing something about it are two different skills, and you need both.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA HONESTIDAD

Joining two sentences forces a decision most writers never make out loud: which of these two facts is the main one? The clause that survives as the main clause is your point; the one that goes inside que is support.

Two separate sentences let you avoid choosing. A relative clause does not — which makes this grammar an honesty tool and not only a style tool. Subordinating a fact is a claim that it matters less, and a claim can be false. Look again at the sentence you built in Part 4 and ask whether the fact you left in the main clause is really the one you think is central. La frase que escribes dice también lo que crees.
