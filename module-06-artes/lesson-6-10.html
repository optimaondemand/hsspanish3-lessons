Two things worth knowing before you start. Several items give you two sentences and ask you to write the combined version, not to pick it — recognising a relative clause is much easier than building one, and this module is assessed on building. And at least one item has no signal word at all: both options are perfectly grammatical and only the meaning differs. If you have been passing this module by matching cues, that item is where it shows.

⭐ CÓMO USAR DOS INTENTOS
Take attempt 1 seriously — its value is telling you which of the eleven areas above is weak — then reopen that lesson before attempt 2. Students who take both attempts back to back usually score about the same twice, having learned nothing in between. Reviewing between attempts is the entire reason there are two.
🧠 PART 3 · PRÁCTICA FINAL 12 min

Five mixed items in quiz format. Ungraded — if you miss one, reopen the lesson named in the feedback before you take the quiz.

✅ PRÁCTICA 1 · combinar dos en una
Une en una sola oración: «El cuadro está en la sala 12. El cuadro mide más de tres metros.»
✅ PRÁCTICA 2 · tras preposición
«Palomino es el biógrafo de ___ viene la fecha de 1656.»
✅ PRÁCTICA 3 · ¿hay un sustantivo detrás?
«Velázquez se pintó a sí mismo dentro del cuadro, ___ todavía sorprende a los visitantes.»
✅ PRÁCTICA 4 · concordancia de cuyo
«Es un pintor ___ obras están casi todas en un solo museo.»
✅ PRÁCTICA 5 · sin palabra pista
Las dos son correctas: «Los cuadros que compró Felipe IV están en el Prado» y «Los cuadros, que compró Felipe IV, están en el Prado». ¿Cuál dice que Felipe IV compró solo algunos de los cuadros de los que hablamos?

➡️ PART 5 · LO QUE VIENE 8 min

Módulo 7 is the one this semester was built for

Módulo 7 is Recomendaciones y deseos: recommending, requesting, saying what you want someone else to do. The culture is Perú, in more depth than any country has had so far, and the reading comes from Ricardo Palma's Tradiciones peruanas — a nineteenth-century Peruvian writer who collected the stories Lima told about itself. The virtue is la prudencia, which is the right virtue for a module about advising other people.

💡 Y AHORA LO IMPORTANTE
In 3.10 this course told you something and then left it alone for three months: the command forms you learned in Módulo 3 and Módulo 4 are also used for something else. Módulo 7 is where that comes due.

What it means in practice is that Módulo 7 adds no new endings. The forms are the ones you already produce automatically for Ud. commands and for negative tú commands. You are not going to be handed a new conjugation table — you are going to be shown a second job for a set of forms you have owned since October. That is why the command modules came first, and it is the single best piece of news about the hardest thing in Spanish grammar.

What is new is when and why — which is a question about meaning rather than about morphology, and one trigger will be introduced at a time.

And one warning that belongs to this module specifically. Módulo 7 is full of the word que. It is not the que you have spent ten days on. This module's que is a relative pronoun: it points back at a noun and puts a second fact about it into the same sentence. Módulo 7's que points back at nothing — it simply opens a clause after certain verbs. Telling the two apart is real work, and you are in a far better position to do it than a student who arrived at Módulo 7 having never looked closely at a relative clause. That is not a coincidence either.

🌎 CULTURA · ESPAÑA
Lo que te llevas de este módulo

Six things from Spain worth keeping, in the order you met them.

The Prado is a family's taste, not a survey — opened 1819 out of the royal collection, which explains both its ferocious strengths and its systematic gaps. Velázquez (1599–1660) was a palace official as much as a painter, and his output is small partly because of it. His paint got looser as he got older, and you can watch it happen by walking the rooms in order.

Two things about that room are unsettled or gone. Specialists still disagree about what the mirror at the back reflects, and say so openly rather than picking a side. And the room itself burned with the Alcázar in 1734; the canvas was carried out, and the inventory of the survivors gave the painting its second name.

And almost everything we know rests on very little: one biography by Antonio Palomino, written some sixty years later by a man born the year before the painting was finished, plus a series of royal furniture lists. That is why the Spanish encyclopedia writes «según Palomino» and «aceptada por la crítica» instead of simply stating the date.

Ten days on one museum, one painter and one painting — which is the opposite of how courses usually do culture, and the whole argument of this module. Breadth gives you facts. Depth gives you the ability to tell knowledge from repetition.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA ATENCIÓN

Ten days on the grammar of qualifying what you have just named, in a module whose virtue is attentiveness — and the two turned out to be the same skill in different materials. A relative clause is a demand to keep looking at a noun. A parenthetical is a demand to hold a sentence open. A minute in front of a painting is a demand to stop moving. In every case the work is the refusal to move on at the first available moment, and in every case what it buys is something you would otherwise never have seen. Next module the virtue becomes la prudencia, and it needs this one first: nobody advises well who has not looked carefully. La atención es el único regalo que se puede dar sin perderlo.
