Cómo escribir acentos en español

Reusable component for all Spanish courses · link from lesson X.01 and from every typed graded item · v1 · 2026-08-03
Why this page exists. Spanish 3 Honors grades accent accuracy in typed answers, including auto-graded dictation where pasó is correct and paso is wrong. That is the right standard — but it is only fair if students know how to produce the characters. French 1 has had a typing component since its build; the Spanish courses never did. This closes that gap and is written to be reused by Spanish 1, 2, 3 and 4 without modification.

Why the tilde is not decoration

In Spanish the accent mark carries meaning. It is not a flourish added to a finished word — in many pairs it is the difference between two different words:

Sin tildeCon tildeWhat changed
paso — a step / I passpasó — he/she went, it happenedPerson and tense
hablo — I speakhabló — he/she spokePresent → preterite
donde — where (relative)dónde — where? (question)Statement → question
esta — thisestá — he/she/it isDeterminer → verb
se — oneselfsé — I knowPronoun → verb
anecdota — not a wordanécdota — anecdoteWithout the tilde it is misspelled
So “I knew the word, I just didn't put the accent” is not quite true. Writing hablo when you mean habló does not produce a slightly untidy right answer — it produces a different, wrong one. This is exactly why the dictation items require the tilde.

Windows — the fastest method

Install the US-International keyboard once, then accents take two keystrokes forever. This is the method to use if you write Spanish regularly.

  1. Open Settings → Time & Language → Language & Region.
  2. Next to English (United States) click … → Language options.
  3. Under Keyboards, click Add a keyboard and choose United States-International.
  4. Switch keyboards any time with Win + Space.

One thing to expect. With this keyboard, ' and " become “waiting” keys — press ' then a and you get á. To type a plain apostrophe (as in don't), press ' then space. It feels strange for about a day and then becomes invisible.

Mac

Nothing to install. Hold Option+e, release, then the vowel:

To typePress
á é í ó úOption+e, release, then the vowel
ñOption+n, release, then n

Alternatively, press and hold a vowel key and a small menu of accented options appears — click one or press its number. Slower, but nothing to memorise.

Chromebook

  • Unicode method: Ctrl+Shift+u, release, type the code, then Enter or space. Codes: á e1 · é e9 · í ed · ó f3 · ú fa · ñ f1 · ü fc · ¿ bf · ¡ a1
  • Easier: Settings → Advanced → Languages and inputs → Inputs → add US International, then switch with Ctrl+Shift+Space. Behaves like the Windows method above.

Phone or tablet

Press and hold the vowel and slide to the accented version. For ñ, hold n. For ¿ and ¡, hold ? and ! on the symbols keyboard.

Worth doing once: add Spanish as a second keyboard language on your phone. You then get Spanish autocorrect and accent suggestions, which stops your phone “correcting” está to esta — a genuinely common source of lost points on typed submissions.

Practice — five seconds each

Type these before your first graded typed item. If all five come out right, you are ready:

  1. ¿Qué pasó anoche?
  2. Mi abuela cocinaba mientras yo estudiaba.
  3. ¡Qué anécdota tan buena!
  4. No sé dónde está mi mochila.
  5. El niño español se llama Iñigo.
Never paste accents from a translator. It is slower than the two-keystroke method, it often brings invisible formatting that breaks auto-graded matching, and on a translation task it is academic dishonesty. Learn the shortcut once — it takes about ten minutes and serves you for the rest of the course.
