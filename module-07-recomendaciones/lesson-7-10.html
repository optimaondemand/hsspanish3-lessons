★ Repaso del módulo — y el Grammar Quiz
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The hardest thing in Spanish grammar, and you got here without learning a single new ending.
Last day of Módulo 7. Work the review, take the Grammar Quiz (20 pts, two attempts, includes listening), and then read Part 5 properly — because one module remains in this semester, it carries the gate, and it is built entirely on what you did this fortnight.

One instruction about the quiz before anything else. Most of it is not a conjugation test. You learned these forms in Módulo 3 and the quiz assumes it. What it tests is the decision: one subject or two? Read every stem for who is doing what before you look at the options.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to decide between an infinitive and que + subjuntivo without stopping to think about it.
2
I will be able to explain why que is never the trigger, using two examples from one 1896 sentence.
3
I will be able to say what Módulo 8 asks of me, and why it is the module the whole semester was building toward.

💡 QUÉ TIPO DE EXAMEN ES ESTE
More than a third of this quiz gives you a sentence and asks you to work out whether the subject changes. Some answers are infinitives. If you go in expecting to fill twenty blanks with subjunctives, you will lose points on the ones that are not.

Three items are built so that both options are correct Spanish and only the stated meaning decides between them. There is no signal word to scan for in those, on purpose: a quiz you can pass by spotting que would certify pattern-matching, and pattern-matchers hit a wall in Módulo 8 where the whole task is connected discourse.

One item is typed, and it needs a tilde. Dé and de are different words; the accented one is the only accepted answer.
⭐ CÓMO USAR DOS INTENTOS
Take attempt 1 seriously — its whole value is telling you which of the thirteen rows above is weak — then reopen that lesson before attempt 2. Students who take both attempts back to back usually score about the same twice, having learned nothing in between. Review between the attempts, not around them.

➡️ PART 5 · LO QUE VIENE 8 min

Módulo 8 is the last one, and it is the one everything else was for

Módulo 8 is Dar consejos — advising other people. The culture is Chile, in the same depth Perú had here. The virtue is not a new one: it is a synthesis of the whole semester's virtues, which is the honest way to end a course that has spent eighteen weeks arguing that they belong together.

And the grammar is not new either. It is this module's subjunctive of influence, in use — not in single sentences but across connected discourse: a paragraph of advice, a conversation, a piece of counsel that has to hold together for ninety seconds without falling into a list. That is a genuinely different skill from getting one clause right, and it is the last thing Semester 1 asks of you.

⚠️ DOS COSAS QUE SOLO TIENE EL MÓDULO 8
1. El Módulo 8 lleva la puerta del semestre. Its Grammar Quiz is the semester gate: you need 16 out of 20 to move on, and unlike every other quiz this year that score actually locks what follows. You get two attempts, as always, so the way through is the way it has always been — take the first one honestly, find out what is weak, go and fix it, then take the second. Nothing about the gate is a surprise and nothing about it is new material; it tests this module and the one before it.

2. El Módulo 8 lleva el proyecto de RV. It also carries the semester's VR project — El consultorio, a hundred points, in which you give advice inside a virtual space rather than into a microphone. There is a preparation briefing, the session itself, and a written reflection afterwards, and it is the single largest piece of work in Semester 1. Details come on day one of the module; do not plan your fortnight around only the five usual items.

One warning that belongs to this module specifically. Módulo 8 will not hand you a second trigger family. Emotion, doubt and the rest genuinely are later material — so if you are hoping that the “real” subjunctive is still to come, it is not, and that is good news. Everything Módulo 8 asks for is something you did this fortnight, done for longer and in a row. Which means the single most valuable thing you can do between now and then is take the shaky row from Part 1 and fix it this week. A gap here does not stay the same size in Módulo 8; it gets used four times a page.

🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Lo que te llevas de este módulo

Six things from Perú worth keeping, in the order you met them.

Three official languages — Spanish, Quechua and Aymara — and Inca walls in Cusco still carrying colonial ones that the earthquakes keep damaging. The potato is Andean, with over three thousand native varieties still grown, and a genebank in Lima working on the ones no market rewards. The Nazca lines are unexplained rather than mysterious, and Maria Reiche spent decades with a broom keeping them. Arequipa is built out of a volcano — white sillar, thick walls, low vaults, because the ground moves. Fray Gómez's convent is a real address in a working city centre where it almost never rains. And the cajón in flamenco is Peruvian, carried to Spain in 1977.

Two more, because Perú does not fit in six. Caral, on the coast north of Lima, was a city with monumental platforms roughly five thousand years ago — contemporary with the earliest Egyptian pyramids and among the oldest urban sites in the Americas, which relocates “ancient Peru” several thousand years earlier than Machu Picchu. And about sixty per cent of Perú is Amazon rainforest: the country most people picture as mountains is mostly jungle, with the Andes as a strip down its western side and the desert coast a strip narrower still. Three completely different countries under one flag, which is also why el huayno, la marinera and the cajón sound nothing like each other.

Ten days on one country is the opposite of how courses usually do culture. Breadth gives you facts about everywhere. Depth gives you the ability to notice when somebody is repeating a cliché.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL VALOR

Ten days on the grammar of telling people what you want them to do, in a module whose virtue is el valor — and the two are the same problem. Spanish gives you a ladder: sugiero, recomiendo, pido, exijo, and beneath all of them the bare command. Every rung is correct grammar. None of them does anything until somebody decides to speak.

Jeromo asked again after the whole city had refused him. The mother in 7.03 said the fussy thing about the photocopy. The market seller in 7.02 risked a sale to warn a stranger about customs. None of them was reckless and none of them was timid; each said the unwelcome thing and then chose the strength with care.

Next module gathers the whole semester — las siete, juntas — and this is the one the rest wait on: courtesy, honesty and service stay silent until somebody is willing to speak first. El valor no está en subir el tono; está en hablar.
