Lesson 7.04
★ El subjuntivo en uso — la regla a lo largo de un párrafo entero
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
One sentence lets you stop and count. A paragraph does not — the subject changes under you while you write.
Yesterday's rule is easy to state and slow to apply: one subject → infinitivo; dos sujetos → que + subjuntivo. You asked the question once per sentence and got it right. Today you ask it eight times in a row without breaking stride, because that is what giving somebody advice actually is.

Real advice changes subject constantly. I want, you go, my parents insist, we eat, nobody in particular carries sunscreen. Each switch re-poses the question, and the answer is not always the clause — sometimes it is the infinitive, and reaching for the subjunctive out of habit is its own error.

Three things also happen inside the clause once it opens: pronouns take a slot, negation takes a side, and one trigger can govern two verbs. And you will meet the trap of the module: a que that changes nothing at all.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to hold the two-subject rule across a connected paragraph, switching between infinitive and clause as the subject changes.
2
I will be able to place object and reflexive pronouns correctly inside a que clause.
3
I will be able to recognise a que that does not trigger the subjunctive, and say why.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Three lines, three different answers

Read these three in order and watch the machinery change under an unchanging topic:

«Quiero que llegues el miércoles.»  →  yo quiero, tú llegas. Dos sujetos.
«Prefiero recogerte en el aeropuerto.»  →  yo prefiero, yo recojo. Un sujeto.
«La ventaja es que el aeropuerto está cerca.»  →  nobody is pushing anything. Indicativo.

Three sentences, three constructions, and the only thing that decided each one was who was doing what. That is a paragraph — and a paragraph is what Día 7 will ask you to write.

🧠 PART 2 · EL PÁRRAFO ANOTADO 11 min

Eight sentences of advice, and the rule applied eight times

A note to a friend who is going to Arequipa. Read it once for sense, then read the table underneath, which shows the decision behind every line.

Una nota para un amigo que va a Arequipa

1 Quiero que llegues con calma; el vuelo desde Lima dura una hora, pero la ciudad está a más de dos mil trescientos metros y el cuerpo lo nota. 2 Yo prefiero caminar el primer día sin plan, sin subir a ningún mirador. 3 Te recomiendo que reserves el hotel con antelación, porque en agosto no queda una cama libre en el centro. 4 Es necesario llevar bloqueador y gorra todos los días, incluso cuando el cielo está gris. 5 Mis tíos insisten en que pruebes el rocoto relleno, y te advierto una cosa: pica de verdad. 6 No quiero que te pierdas el mirador de Yanahuara; desde ahí puedes ver el Misti entero. 7 La ventaja es que puedes recorrer casi todo el centro a pie. 8 Y te pido que me mandes una foto de la plaza al amanecer, cuando el sillar todavía está casi blanco.

OraciónLos sujetosLo que escribíPor qué
1yo / túque lleguesTwo subjects. The clause is obligatory — querer has no escape route.
2yo / yocaminarOne subject. Infinitive, and it would be an error to open a clause here.
3yo / túque reservesTwo subjects. Recomendar would also allow «te recomiendo reservar»; the clause is more explicit.
4nadie en concretollevarImpersonal, and true for anybody. Nobody is named, so no clause. Día 5 gives you the other half of this frame.
5mis tíos / túque pruebesTwo subjects — and note the wanter is not yo. It never has to be.
6yo / túque te pierdasThe no belongs to the trigger; the pronoun te goes inside the clause, before the verb.
7—que puedesIndicativo. Ser is not a verb of influence. This que reports.
8yo / túque me mandesTwo subjects, and me sits before mandes — the same slot it takes in «no me mandes».
  • Quiero que estudies.the pattern behind sentences 1 and 6
  • Recomiendo que lo intentes.the pattern behind sentence 3

Count them: five clauses with the subjunctive, two infinitives, one que with the indicative. That mixture is what a real paragraph looks like, and it is why a page of nothing but subjunctives would have taught you nothing.

🧠 PART 3 · DENTRO DE LA CLÁUSULA 9 min

Once the clause opens, three things happen

💡 1 · LOS PRONOMBRES VAN DELANTE
Object and reflexive pronouns sit before the conjugated verb inside the clause — exactly where they sit in a negative command:

Quiero que me llames. ··· No quiero que te preocupes. ··· Te pido que me lo mandes.

Compare Módulo 4: no me llames, no te preocupes, no me lo mandes. Same forms, same slot, same order — indirect before direct. You already own this; nothing about the clause changes it.
💡 2 · UN DISPARADOR PUEDE GOBERNAR DOS VERBOS
«Te pido que descanses el primer día y bebas mucha agua.»

One trigger, two subjunctive verbs. The second que is optional — «y que bebas» is equally good and slightly more emphatic — but the mood is not optional. What you cannot do is open a clause and then slide back to an infinitive: «te pido que descanses y beber agua» is broken Spanish.

This matters more than it looks. Once you are writing paragraphs, most of your subjunctives will arrive in pairs.
⚠️ 3 · DÓNDE PONER EL «NO» — Y EL DISPARADOR NUNCA CAMBIA
The negative can go on either half, and it means two different things:

«No quiero que vayas.» — I don't want you to go. This is the normal one.
«Quiero que no vayas.» — I want you not to go. Heavier, more insistent, and used deliberately.

English blurs these; Spanish does not, and negating the trigger is what you want nine times out of ten.

And one thing that never moves: the trigger verb itself stays in the indicative. Quiero, prefiero, te recomiendo, insisten — all indicative, always. Only the verb after que shifts mood. Students who start putting the whole sentence into the subjunctive have over-learned the rule, and it is worth catching early.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min

✅ TRY IT · el pronombre dentro de la cláusula

✅ TRY IT · busca el error

✅ TRY IT · un disparador, dos verbos
Complete both: «Te pido que ___ el primer día y ___ mucha agua.» (descansar, beber)

Ahora tú: a cousin is coming to stay with your family for two weeks. Write five connected sentences of advice and wishes. Include at least three que clauses with the subjunctive, at least one infinitive where the subject does not change, and one que with the indicative.

—Quiero que llegues antes del jueves, porque el viernes tenemos una cena en casa. Mi madre insiste en que traigas ropa ligera; aquí hace más calor de lo que piensas. Te recomiendo que duermas en el cuarto de arriba: la ventaja es que casi no entra ruido de la calle. No quiero que te preocupes por el transporte, porque yo prefiero recogerte en la estación. Y te pido que me mandes el número del tren por mensaje.

Check yours line by line. Four clauses with the subjunctive (llegues, traigas, duermas, te preocupes, me mandes — five, in fact), one infinitive where the subject holds steady (prefiero recogerte), and one reporting que (la ventaja es que casi no entra ruido).

Then check the small things: te before preocupes, me before mandes, and the trigger verbs all left in the indicative — quiero, insiste, recomiendo, prefiero, pido.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

En la puerta del monasterio

—Antes de entrar les explico dos cosas. El monasterio ocupa una manzana entera y por dentro es una ciudad pequeña: calles con nombre, plazas, cocinas, un cementerio. Lo que más sorprende a la gente no es el tamaño; es el color.
—¿Se puede tomar fotos?
—Sí, pero les pido que no usen flash en las salas de pintura. Es necesario cuidar los pigmentos; algunos tienen cuatro siglos. Y les pido también que dejen los trípodes en la consigna.
—¿Cuánto tiempo necesitamos?
—Dos horas si van con calma. Yo prefiero empezar por el claustro de los naranjos, que es la parte más antigua, y terminar en la azotea. La ventaja es que desde arriba entienden el plano entero de una sola mirada.
—Mi hermana quiere que compremos las entradas dentro.
—Hoy no hay problema. Pero les advierto que en temporada alta la fila da la vuelta a la manzana, y entonces conviene comprarlas antes.
—Una última cosa: ¿hace frío ahí dentro?
—En los pasillos, sí. Es necesario llevar algo de manga larga. El sol de la calle no dice nada sobre la temperatura de estos corredores; Arequipa engaña así todo el año.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Sort every italicised verb into three piles and the lesson falls out of the conversation.

Clauses with the subjunctive — que no usen, que dejen, que compremos: two subjects each time, and in the first one the negative sits inside the clause because the guide is not refusing photographs, he is asking for no flash specifically.

Infinitives — cuidar, empezar, terminar, llevar: either impersonal (es necesario cuidar, true for everybody) or one subject (yo prefiero empezar… y terminar, where one trigger governs two infinitives, exactly as it can govern two subjunctives).

Indicative after que — que… entienden and que… da. The first is la ventaja es que again. The second is worth a second look: advertir is one of this module's ten triggers, but here it is reporting a fact — the queue really is that long — rather than pushing anybody. When advertir pushes, it opens a clause with the subjunctive; when it merely informs, the indicative. The verb's job in the sentence decides, never the word que.

Two spirals: «lo que más sorprende a la gente» and «que es la parte más antigua» are Módulo 6's relative pronouns, and the guide uses the por of route — «empezar por el claustro».

One more thing to notice, because it is the shape of tomorrow: every impersonal es necesario in this conversation is followed by an infinitive. The guide is briefing a whole group, so nothing he says has to be aimed at one person. Tomorrow you learn what changes when it does.
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Arequipa, la ciudad construida con un volcán

Peru's second city is built almost entirely out of sillar — a pale volcanic rock, welded together from ash flows, that is quarried in the ravines just outside town. It is soft enough to be cut with a hand saw and hard enough to hold a carved façade for four centuries, which is why Arequipa's churches are covered in stone flowers and pumas and grape vines. The historic centre has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2000, and the nickname la Ciudad Blanca comes from the material, not from the light.

The volcanoes are not scenery. Three of them stand over the city — el Misti, el Chachani, el Pichu Pichu — and in 1600 the eruption of Huaynaputina, a few valleys south, buried the region in ash and darkened skies as far as Europe; it remains the largest volcanic eruption recorded in South America. Arequipa has also been flattened by earthquakes repeatedly and rebuilt each time low, thick-walled and vaulted. The architecture is a response to a known risk.

Inside the city sits the Monasterio de Santa Catalina, a walled convent occupying a full block — a small town of ochre and blue streets that stayed effectively closed to outsiders from 1579 until 1970. And a word on the arequipeños themselves: their regional pride is a national joke, complete with talk of an independent República de Arequipa and its own passport. They tell the joke first, which is the surest sign it is half serious.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL VALOR

Look at where the eight-sentence note puts its hardest sentence. The one thing that cannot be fixed later — arriving too fast at 2,300 metres — is sentence one. The photo of the plaza is sentence eight.

That order takes nerve, and the alternative is tempting. You can put the unwelcome instruction fourth, wrapped in two pleasant ones, where a reader in a hurry will skim past it — and then you have technically said it and can tell yourself you warned them. Leading with it is the braver version, because it cannot be missed and cannot be softened afterwards. Courage exercised with judgement is not saying more; it is putting the difficult thing where it will actually be read. Arequipa rebuilt itself low and thick-walled because it knew which risk was the real one. El aviso que va al final es el aviso que nadie leyó.
