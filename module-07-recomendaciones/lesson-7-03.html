Lesson 7.03
★ La regla de los dos sujetos — un infinitivo o «que» + subjuntivo
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Count the subjects. One means an infinitive. Two mean que plus the subjunctive.
Today has exactly one new idea in it, and you have already been given everything else it needs.

You own the forms — since Módulo 3, as commands. You own the triggers — since yesterday, as vocabulary. What is left is knowing when a sentence needs them, and Spanish answers that with a question you can ask in about a second: how many people are in this sentence?

«Quiero salir.» — one person. I want, I leave. Infinitive.
«Quiero que salgas.» — two people. I want, you leave. Que plus the subjunctive.

That is the rule. The rest of the lesson is practice at applying it fast, plus a look at the forms you already have — this time under their real name.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

I will be able to decide, in one step, whether a sentence needs an infinitive or que + subjunctive.
2
I will be able to build present subjunctive forms from the yo form of the present tense.
3
I will be able to write sentences with querer que, recomendar que and es necesario que.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Where English gives up

English has one way to say both of these, and it is the reason this structure feels foreign:

I want to leave.  →  «Quiero salir.»
I want you to leave.  →  «Quiero que salgas.»

English keeps the infinitive both times and simply drops a person in front of it — want you to leave. Spanish cannot do that. There is no such structure; «quiero tú salir» is not a sentence. The moment a second person appears, Spanish opens a new clause with que and conjugates the verb inside it.

So the error to watch for is not choosing the wrong ending. It is writing an infinitive where Spanish needs a whole clause — carrying the English structure across intact.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Read «Quiero que salgas» aloud. What does it feel like?
🧠 PART 2 · LA REGLA DE LOS DOS SUJETOS 12 min
💡 LA REGLA
One subject → infinitive. Two subjects → que + subjuntivo.

Ask: is the person who wants also the person who acts?
• Yes → Quiero estudiar más.
• No → Quiero que estudies más.
El disparadorUn solo sujeto · infinitivoDos sujetos · que + subjuntivo

preferirPrefiero esperar aquí.Prefiero que esperemos aquí.
necesitarNecesito hablar con ella.Necesito que hables con ella.
pedir— (pedir aims at someone by nature)Te pido que me escuches.

es necesarioEs necesario reservar con antelación.Es necesario que reserves con antelación.
  • Quiero que estudies.two people, one sentence
  • Es necesario que llegues temprano.the impersonal frame — nobody is named as the wanter
  • Recomiendo que lo intentes.advice, one step less blunt than a command

Look at the last two rows again. Recomendar and es necesario accept either construction and the meaning barely shifts — te recomiendo descansar and te recomiendo que descanses are both good Spanish. That is genuinely useful: when you are unsure, the infinitive is often available as an escape route. But it is not always, and the next box is the reason.

⚠️ DONDE EL INFINITIVO NO SIRVE
Querer gives you no escape route. With two subjects it takes que + subjuntivo and nothing else. «Quiero que vengas» — and «quiero tú venir» or «quiero venirte» are not Spanish at all, they are English wearing Spanish words.

So there is one sentence pattern you should over-practise until it is automatic: quiero que + subjuntivo. It is the most common way anyone expresses a wish about another person, and there is no way around it.

Two smaller notes. Es necesario que takes the subjunctive because it is impersonal — nobody is named as the one wanting, but somebody is still being pushed. And «prefiero que esperemos» counts as two subjects even though I am inside the nosotros: the wanter is yo, the actors are we, and those are different subjects.
🧠 PART 3 · CÓMO SE FORMAN · repaso 10 min

Nothing new here — but now it has a name

You have been building these since Módulo 3. The recipe, in case it was never said out loud: take the yo form of the present tense, drop the -o, and swap the vowel. -ar verbs take e; -er and -ir verbs take a.

  • coma, comas, comamos, coman; viva, vivas, vivamos, vivan-er and -ir verbs take «a»
  • dé, esté, vaya, sea, sepathe same recording as Módulo 3 — the same five forms
💡 POR FIN, LA EXPLICACIÓN DE LOS IRREGULARES DEL MÓDULO 3
Because the subjunctive is built from the yo form, every oddity in the yo form is inherited — and that retro-explains a list you had to take on trust in Módulo 3:

tener → tengo → tenga ··· venir → vengo → venga ··· decir → digo → diga
hacer → hago → haga ··· salir → salgo → salga ··· conocer → conozco → conozca

They are not irregular subjunctives at all. They are perfectly regular subjunctives built on an irregular yo form. One rule, not a list.

Which leaves genuinely just six that come from nowhere: dé, esté, vaya, sea, sepa, haya. You already produce five of them as Ud. commands. So the true memorisation load of “the hardest structure in Spanish” is one verb — haber — and you will not need it until Semester 2.

✅ TRY IT · la forma

✅ TRY IT · dos sujetos escondidos
“I prefer that we wait here.” Does this count as two subjects, given that I am one of we?

Ahora tú: a friend is about to travel to Perú. Say three sentences aloud — one with quiero que, one with te recomiendo que, one with es necesario que. Then say a fourth about yourself, using an infinitive, and notice how much shorter it is.

—Quiero que me escribas cuando llegues a Lima. Te recomiendo que pases dos días en Cusco antes de subir. Es necesario que compres el boleto con antelación, porque no se vende en la puerta. Yo prefiero viajar en la temporada seca.

Check yours: three que clauses with subjunctive verbs (escribas, pases, compres) and one infinitive (viajar) where the subject does not change. Every subjunctive form there is one you have written before as a negative command — no escribas, no pases, no compres.

One thing to notice and leave alone for now: «cuando llegues» in that first sentence is also a subjunctive, and it is not one this module teaches. Cuando pointing at a future event takes the subjunctive in real Spanish, which is why it is written that way here — but that rule belongs to a later course. Read it, do not build with it.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

La llamada del domingo

—Mamá, ya compré el boleto. Salgo el martes.
—¿El martes? Quiero que me mandes el número del vuelo.
—Te lo mando ahora.
—Y prefiero que no viajes de noche, hija. Ya sabes lo que pienso de eso.
—Es el único vuelo directo.
—Bueno. Entonces te pido que me escribas al aterrizar, aunque sea tarde. Dos palabras me bastan.
—Dos palabras. Prometido.
—Y una cosa más: es necesario que lleves la copia del pasaporte aparte, no en la misma mochila. Tu tío perdió todo en Arequipa por no hacer eso.
—Lo sé, lo sé. Me lo has dicho cuatro veces.
—Te lo digo una quinta y ya está. Quiero dormir tranquila esta semana.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Five que clauses, and one infinitive. Find the infinitive and say why it is different from the others: «Quiero dormir tranquila» — the mother wants, the mother sleeps. One subject. Every other wish in the conversation is about her daughter, so every other verb goes into a clause.

That last line is the whole lesson in one exchange, and it is doing something else too: she has spent the call telling her daughter what to do, and she ends by saying what she wants for herself. That is why the infinitive lands.

Two spirals to spot. «Me lo has dicho cuatro veces» is the present perfect with an irregular participle and a double object pronoun — Módulos 1 and 2. And «por no hacer eso» is por + infinitive marking cause, from Módulo 5: the uncle lost everything because of not doing it.

You will also have noticed «aunque sea tarde». Same situation as cuando llegues above — a real subjunctive, correctly used, belonging to a rule this course teaches later. Recognise it; do not copy it yet.
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Las líneas de Nazca y la mujer que las cuidó

In the desert south of Lima, the Nazca people scraped away the dark surface stones to expose the pale ground beneath, making figures — a hummingbird, a monkey, a spider, a great many straight lines — some of them hundreds of metres across. They are roughly two thousand years old, and they have survived because almost no rain falls there.

They are also barely visible from the ground, which is part of why they were not widely studied until aircraft began crossing the pampa in the twentieth century. Maria Reiche, a German-born mathematician who settled in Peru, spent decades surveying and measuring them, mostly alone, often on foot with a broom to sweep the lines clear. She campaigned for their protection and lived beside them; the site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994.

What the lines were for is genuinely unsettled. Astronomical calendar, ritual pathways, something connected to water — these are competing hypotheses, not a mystery with a known answer being withheld from you. Be suspicious of any source that sounds certain, and especially of the ones that reach for visitors from elsewhere: the Nazca had the engineering and the reason, and the condescension in assuming otherwise is worth noticing.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL VALOR

Maria Reiche did the least dramatic version of a brave thing. No single discovery, no audience, decades of measuring and sweeping and coming back — and the lines are still there partly because of it. Courage is easy to recognise when it lasts an afternoon; committing forty years to work whose value nobody had agreed on yet is the same virtue with the applause taken out.

The mother on the phone is doing a much smaller version of it. Quiero que lleves una fotocopia del pasaporte en otra bolsa is a sentence that risks sounding like fussing, and she says it anyway. Most unwelcome advice is unwelcome for that reason rather than because it is wrong: it makes the speaker sound anxious, and the speaker knows it. She said it. Hace falta valor para decir algo que suena a poca cosa.
