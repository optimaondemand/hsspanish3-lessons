Lesson 7.06
★ Un párrafo de consejos — de principio a fin, con cuatro instrumentos
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Four instruments, one paragraph, and every choice made on purpose.
Last full practice day. You now have four ways to move somebody — the command, quiero que, te recomiendo que, and the impersonal frame — and today you hold all of them across six to eight connected sentences aimed at one real person about one real situation. That is the Honors standard, and it is exactly what tomorrow's Lengua en acción asks for.

There is one error that appears only at paragraph length, and it will very likely appear in your first attempt: over-applying the subjunctive. After three days of drilling que clauses, the clause starts to feel like the right answer everywhere, and you find yourself writing «quiero que yo llegue temprano» — two subjects where there is only one of you. The paragraph that shows you have understood this module is the one thatdrops back to the infinitive at the right moment.

The reading is the road that ran an empire of four thousand kilometres without a wheel or a horse.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to write a 6–8 sentence paragraph of advice to a named person, using at least three different triggers.
2
I will be able to justify every choice — command, infinitive, que + subjunctive, impersonal frame — by naming the reason.
3
I will be able to explain what the Qhapaq Ñan was and how the chasquis worked.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Cuatro instrumentos, en una página

InstrumentoMóduloEjemploSu trabajo en un párrafo
el mandatoM3 · M4No cargues tanto peso.The blunt one. Best for a single sharp instruction.
querer / preferir queM7Quiero que lleves dos pares.Your own stake in it. Warm, and personal.
pedir / recomendar queM7Te recomiendo que camines detrás.Advice with a reason behind it.
el marco impersonalM7Es imprescindible que duermas allí.The non-negotiable one, stated without ego.

A paragraph that uses only one of these reads like a list. A paragraph that uses all four reads like a person talking — and each switch tells the reader something about how much the item matters.

🧠 PART 2 · LA ARQUITECTURA 10 min

A shape for a paragraph of advice

OraciónSu trabajoInstrumentoCómo empieza
1The situation — no advice yetindicativoSé que… / El lunes sales…
2The one thing that cannot be fixed latermarco impersonalAntes de nada, es imprescindible que…
3Your own stake in itquerer / preferir queQuiero que… / Prefiero que…
4Advice with its reasonrecomendar / pedir queTe recomiendo que… porque…
5One sharp warningmandato negativoNo… (tú)
6The gentlest itemes mejor queEs mejor que no…
7Something about yourselfinfinitivoYo espero… / Quiero…
8Your judgement, reportedindicativoTodos los que… dicen…

Sentence 7 is the one that marks a writer who has understood the module. Everything before it changed subject, so everything before it needed a clause. Sentence 7 does not — you are talking about yourself — so it takes an infinitive, and putting a subjunctive there is the day-6 error. It is also the sentence most students get wrong, precisely because by sentence 7 the clause has become a habit.

⭐ LA REGLA DE LOS CONECTORES · still in force
No connector twice. For a paragraph of advice you have: antes de nada · primero · sobre todo · además · por eso · sin embargo · aun así · al final. Eight, and eight sentences need at most five.

And one more piece of order: the reason goes after the advice, not before it. «Te recomiendo que camines detrás del guía, porque el paso que él marca parece lento y no lo es» — advice first, justification second. Reversing them buries the instruction.
🧠 PART 3 · MODELO ANOTADO 10 min

Written to one person, about one situation: Marcos leaves on Monday to walk three days of an Inca road with his school group.

Para Marcos, antes del lunes

1 Sé que sales el lunes con el grupo y que caminan tres días por un tramo del Qhapaq Ñan. 2 Antes de nada, es imprescindible que duermas dos noches a esa altura: lo demás tiene solución, la altura no. 3 Quiero que lleves dos pares de medias de lana y que las cambies todos los días, porque los pies deciden si el tercer día es bueno o malo. 4 Te recomiendo que camines detrás del guía las primeras horas; el paso que él marca parece lento y no lo es. 5 Sobre todo, no cargues la mochila con comida que no vas a comer: cada kilo pesa el doble después de veinte kilómetros. 6 Es mejor que no bebas agua de los arroyos sin pastillas; el agua clara también engaña. 7 Yo espero verte el jueves con las piernas destrozadas y una sonrisa. 8 Al final, todos los que han hecho ese camino dicen lo mismo: cansa el cuerpo y despierta la cabeza.

Trace the instruments: 1 indicativo (situation, and two reporting ques) → 2 marco impersonal (the non-negotiable) → 3 querer que, one trigger governing two clause verbs, with las in the pronoun slot → 4 recomendar que plus its reason → 5 negative tú command from Módulo 4 → 6 es mejor que, the gentlest → 7 infinitivo, because yo hope and yo see → 8 judgement, reported.

Four connectors, none repeated (antes de nada, sobre todo, al final, plus porque twice as a reason marker rather than a connector). Five subjunctive clauses, one infinitive, one command, three reporting ques. Spirals: «el paso que él marca» and «todos los que han hecho» (M6 relatives), «han hecho» (M1 present perfect with an irregular participle), «por un tramo» (M5's por of route).

Ahora tú — tomorrow's graded task, rehearsed. Write a 6–8 sentence paragraph of advice to one named person about one specific situation — a trip, an audition, a first job, a difficult conversation — following Part 2's shape. Keep it; tomorrow you will revise it rather than start again.

Before you call it finished:
• One named person, one concrete situation. Generic advice to nobody scores badly and reads worse.
• At least three different triggers, drawn from at least two of Día 2's families (deseo · petición · permiso).
• At least one impersonal frame — es necesario / importante / mejor / imprescindible que.
• At least one infinitive where the subject does not change — sentence 7's job. Do not skip it, and do not let it become «quiero que yo…».
• At least one que with the indicative, reporting rather than pushing.
• Every trigger verb left in the indicative: quiero, te recomiendo, espero.
• Pronouns before the conjugated verb inside the clause: que me escribas, que las cambies.
• Every piece of advice that could sound arbitrary carries its reason, and the reason comes second.
• Accents checked, including dé and esté if you used them, and every -ción noun.
• Four or more different connectors, none repeated.
• At least three words from this module's twenty.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

El camino y los chasquis

Los incas no tenían rueda ni caballos, y aun así gobernaron un territorio de más de cuatro mil kilómetros de largo. Lo hicieron a pie, por el Qhapaq Ñan: una red de caminos empedrados que subía a pasos de más de cuatro mil metros, cruzaba desiertos de arena y bajaba hasta el borde de la selva, con escaleras talladas en la roca y puentes colgantes donde no había otra forma de pasar. Hoy quedan tramos en seis países —Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador y Perú— y la UNESCO los inscribió en conjunto en 2014.

Por ese camino corrían los chasquis, mensajeros jóvenes que trabajaban en relevos. Cada uno cubría un tramo corto a toda velocidad y entregaba el mensaje —a veces un quipu, un manojo de cuerdas anudadas que guardaba cifras y cuentas— al siguiente, que ya esperaba en un puesto junto al camino. Los cronistas escribieron que así una noticia recorría en pocos días distancias que a un jinete le costaban semanas. Ninguno corría mucho; todos corrían rápido.

Y hay una parte del sistema que todavía existe. Sobre el cañón del río Apurímac, cuatro comunidades tejen cada año el puente de Q'eswachaka con paja trenzada a mano, igual que hace cinco siglos. No lo reparan: lo hacen entero de nuevo, cada junio, y el puente viejo lo dejan caer al río. Un guía de la zona lo resume así para los que van a caminar el tramo: «Te recomiendo que camines despacio y que mires atrás de vez en cuando. Mucha gente sube mirando solo el suelo y no ve por dónde ha venido.»

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
The guide's two sentences are today's grammar in miniature. «Te recomiendo que camines… y que mires…» — one trigger governing two clause verbs, exactly Día 4 Part 3 — and then «mucha gente sube… y no ve…», which pushes nobody and stays firmly in the indicative. He advises in one sentence and reports in the next, and the mood changes with the job.

Then count the spirals, because this passage is dense with them: escribieron, recorría, costaban, corrían (preterite against imperfect, Módulo 0), «ha venido» (Módulo 1's present perfect), «que subía… cruzaba… bajaba» and «el siguiente, que ya esperaba» and «los que van a caminar» (Módulo 6's relatives), and two uses of por — «por ese camino», route, and «por dónde ha venido», also route (Módulo 5).

One line to sit with: «Ninguno corría mucho; todos corrían rápido.» That is the whole design of a relay in nine words, and it is also a reasonable description of how a language gets learned.
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Caral: una ciudad tan antigua como las pirámides

The Inca empire that built the Qhapaq Ñan lasted about a century. Peruvian urban life is roughly five thousand years old.

In the Supe valley, a couple of hours north of Lima in a dry coastal landscape, stands Caral — occupied from around 2600 BCE, which makes it contemporary with the building of the Egyptian pyramids and with the cities of Sumer, and one of the oldest urban centres in the Americas. Six stepped pyramidal platforms, sunken circular plazas, residential quarters of different sizes, an irrigated agricultural hinterland. It was excavated from 1994 under the Peruvian archaeologist Ruth Shady Solís, and inscribed by UNESCO in 2009.

Two finds are worth knowing. Thirty-two flutes made from the wing bones of condors and pelicans were recovered from one of the temples — the city had music before it had writing. And a knotted cord of the quipu type was found there, which pushes that recording technology back some four thousand years before the Inca clerks who are usually credited with it.

One claim you will meet often, stated carefully: no fortifications, weapons or mutilated remains have been found at Caral so far, which has led archaeologists to argue that it was organised around trade, agriculture and ritual rather than warfare. That is an argument from absence, and absence can be overturned by the next season's excavation. It is a strong reading of the current evidence, not a settled fact — and the difference between those two things is worth more to you than the headline.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PRUDENCIA

The four communities on the Apurímac do not wait for the bridge to fail. They weave a new one every June, while the old one is still standing, and then let the old one go. Nobody crosses on a bridge that has been tested by breaking.

That is the version of la prudencia this whole module has been building toward: prudence exercised on someone else's behalf, in advance, and at the right strength. Your paragraph today did the same work in eight sentences — the unfixable thing first, the reason attached to every instruction, one sharp warning and no more, and the softest instrument reserved for the smallest item. Advice is not prudent because it is complete. It is prudent because somebody thought about the crossing before the person got to the river. Prudente no es quien nunca cruza el río, sino quien teje el puente cada año.
