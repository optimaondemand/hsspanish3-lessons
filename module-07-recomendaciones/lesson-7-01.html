★ Deseos, riesgos y decisiones — y una revelación sobre lo que ya sabes

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You have been conjugating the subjunctive since Módulo 3. You just called it something else.
Módulo 7 is where this course has been heading since week 7, and it opens with a piece of good news that sounds too good to be true.

The present subjunctive has a reputation. It is the structure people mean when they say Spanish gets hard. And you already produce it — fluently, correctly, dozens of times — because tome, coma, no hables, no vayas are subjunctive forms. Módulos 3 and 4 taught you the entire conjugation under a different name.

So this module teaches you almost no new morphology. It teaches you a second place those forms live. Today: ten words for wanting, risking and deciding — and the proof that you already own the hard part.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 7 min

Two kinds of sentence

Spanish sorts verbs by a question English almost never asks out loud: am I reporting the world, or acting on it?

«Estudias mucho» reports. It is a claim about reality, and reality takes the indicative — every tense you have learned so far.

«Quiero que estudies mucho» does not report. Nobody is claiming you study. I am pushing at the world, trying to move it. Spanish marks that push by changing the verb — and the mood it changes into is the subjunctive.

English used to do this everywhere and has mostly stopped. It survives in a handful of frozen phrases: I insist that he be present — not is. If I were you — not was. You have met the subjunctive in your own language; it just does not announce itself.

✏️ ESCRIBIR CON TILDES · you will be graded on this
Today's la decisión and la precaución take the -ción tilde you banked in Módulo 4. In this course accents are graded, including in typed short answers.

But this module raises the stakes, because two of its verb forms exist only as accents. Módulo 3 taught you the Ud. commands dé and esté, and you noted then that these carry a tilde diacrítica — an accent whose only job is to separate two different words:

«de» = of ··· «dé» = the subjunctive of dar
«este» = this ··· «esté» = the subjunctive of estar

You will write both this fortnight. Drop the tilde and you have not made a spelling slip — you have written a different word. If your typing is not yet quick: Cómo escribir las tildes y la ñ.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Imprescindible is a five-syllable word that earns its length. Take it apart: im- (not) + prescindir (to do without). Something imprescindible is a thing you cannot do without — stronger than English “important,” and used far more freely: el paraguas es imprescindible en Lima en invierno.

Prudente is worth a warning of its own, because it is a false friend in tone. English “prudent” sounds stiff, cautious, faintly disapproving. Spanish prudente is warm praise — it means someone who thinks before acting, and calling a friend prudente is a compliment. That gap matters, because la prudencia is this module's virtue.

And note the pair la ventaja / la desventaja: Spanish builds the opposite with des-, exactly as it does in agradable / desagradable and conocido / desconocido. When you meet a new noun, it is worth testing whether des- gives you a second one free.

💡 POR QUÉ ESTE CURSO ENSEÑA LOS MANDATOS PRIMERO
This was not an accident of the calendar. Most courses introduce the subjunctive as a new conjugation to be memorised in one heavy week, and students spend that week fighting the endings instead of learning what the mood means.

This course spent Módulo 3 and Módulo 4 putting those endings into your hands under a name you already understood — a command. Four weeks of daily practice later, the endings are automatic. Now the only thing left to learn is the interesting part: when Spanish reaches for this mood.

A command is the simplest possible act of influence — you want someone to do something, and you say so directly. The subjunctive of influence is the same act, one step less blunt: quiero que lo hagas instead of hazlo. Same forms, because it is the same job.
👈 DÓNDE VIVE LA OTRA MITAD
A command stands alone: «No hables.»

The new place these forms live is after que, in the second half of a longer sentence:

«Quiero que no hables de esto todavía.»
«Es necesario que vayas al consultorio.»

Same forms. New address. That is the whole of Módulo 7, and Día 3 is where you start building the sentences — so if the second half of each example above looks familiar and the first half looks new, you are exactly where you should be today.

✅ TRY IT · el vocabulario
Your friend cancels a hike because a storm is forecast. In Spanish, that decision is…

Ahora tú: think of a decision you made recently. Say aloud, in Spanish, one ventaja and one desventaja of what you chose — then say whether it was prudente. Use the indicative throughout; you are reporting, not pushing.

—Decidí dejar el equipo de natación este año. La ventaja es que ahora tengo dos tardes libres para estudiar. La desventaja es que veo mucho menos a mis amigos del equipo. Creo que fue una decisión prudente, pero no fue fácil.

Check yours: la ventaja es que… and la desventaja es que… both take the indicative — they report facts. Notice that que appears twice here and changes nothing. Que alone never triggers the subjunctive; what triggers it is the verb in front of it, and ser is not a verb of influence.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Antes de subir

—Mañana subimos temprano. ¿Ya compraste el boleto?
—Hace un mes. ¿Es imprescindible reservar con tanta antelación?
—Aquí sí. Entra un número limitado de personas al día y no se compra en la puerta.
—¿Y el riesgo del soroche? Me dijeron que la altura pega fuerte.
—Por eso te recomiendo dos días en Cusco antes de subir. La ventaja es que te acostumbras; la desventaja es que pierdes dos mañanas. Y hay un detalle que casi nadie sabe: Cusco está más alto que Machu Picchu. Si aguantas la ciudad, aguantas la subida.
—Entonces pierdo las dos mañanas.
—Decisión prudente. Una precaución más: lleva agua y bloqueador. El sol de la sierra engaña, sobre todo cuando hace frío.
—¿Y tú? ¿Vienes?
—Quiero que subas tú primero. Yo ya fui dos veces. Mañana te espero abajo con un mate de coca.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Read the last line again: «Quiero que subas tú primero.»

You have seen subas before. It is the form you would use to tell a friend not to do it — «no subas hoy», a negative tú command from Módulo 4. Identical form, and here it is doing something entirely different: not forbidding, but wanting.

Everything else in the dialogue is indicative, and that is the point of today. La ventaja es que te acostumbras — reporting. Te recomiendo dos días — influence, but with a noun after it, so nothing changes. Only when recomendar and querer take a whole clause with que does the second verb shift, and building those sentences is Día 3.

El soroche is the Andean word for altitude sickness — you will not find it in a Spain-published dictionary as the everyday term, but from Cusco to La Paz it is the only word anyone uses.
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
Un país con tres idiomas oficiales

This module's country is Perú, and it is a useful corrective to the idea that “Spanish-speaking country” means “country where people speak Spanish.” Peru's constitution makes Spanish, Quechua and Aymara all official — Quechua wherever it is predominant — and several million Peruvians speak Quechua, many as a first language.

You have been speaking Quechua for years without knowing it. Cóndor, puma, llama, quinua, pampa and papa — the ordinary Spanish (and English) word for potato — all came from it. So did the name of the city: Cusco is Qosqo, and it was the capital of the Inca empire before it was a Spanish city.

That history is still visible in the stonework. Walk the centre of Cusco and you will see colonial walls sitting directly on Inca foundations, the enormous fitted blocks below and the Spanish masonry above — the Convento de Santo Domingo was built straight onto the Qorikancha, the Inca temple of the sun. Earthquakes have repeatedly damaged the upper storeys. The Inca walls underneath have not moved.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PRUDENCIA

This module's virtue is la prudencia, and it is the right one for the grammar. Prudence is not caution — a merely cautious person says no to everything and calls it wisdom. Prudence is judging well what a particular situation asks for, which sometimes means waiting two mornings in Cusco and sometimes means going anyway.

The subjunctive of influence is prudence made grammatical. It is the difference between hazlo and quiero que lo hagas — between issuing a command and voicing a wish that leaves the other person room to answer. Both are correct Spanish. Knowing which one a moment calls for is not grammar; it is judgement. La prudencia no es decir no; es saber qué pide cada momento.
