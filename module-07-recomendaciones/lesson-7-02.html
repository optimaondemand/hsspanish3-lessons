★ Los verbos que mueven el mundo — desear, pedir, exigir, permitir
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Ten verbs. Every one of them is an attempt to move someone.
Yesterday you learned that Spanish changes a verb's mood when a sentence stops reporting and starts pushing. Today you collect the ten verbs that do the pushing — the ones that will trigger everything you build from tomorrow onward.

Learn them today as ordinary vocabulary, used the simple way: with a noun, or with an infinitive. «Te recomiendo dos días en Cusco.» «Me pidió ayuda.» Nothing new grammatically.

That is deliberate. This course never gives you new words and new grammar on the same day. Tomorrow, when the grammar arrives, these ten will already be familiar — and you will only have one thing to think about.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

Degrees of push

Influence comes in strengths, and Spanish is precise about them in a way English often is not. Compare:

«Te sugiero otra ruta.» — a possibility offered, easy to decline.
«Te recomiendo otra ruta.» — a considered opinion, based on something.
«Te pido otra ruta.» — a request; there is now something at stake for me.
«Te exijo otra ruta.» — a demand. Rare between equals, and it will land as such.

Same sentence shape, four very different social acts. Choosing the wrong strength is not a grammatical error; it is a much more visible kind of mistake.

📚 PART 2 · DIEZ VERBOS DE INFLUENCIA 12 min

Click the 🔊 and repeat aloud. Say each one in a short sentence of your own before moving on.

  • desearto wish, want (formal register)
  • esperarto hope — and also to wait, and to expect
  • preferirto prefer
  • pedirto ask for, request
  • recomendarto recommend
  • insistir ento insist on
  • exigirto demand, require
  • permitirto allow, permit
  • prohibirto forbid, prohibit
  • advertirto warn
⚠️ DOS TRAMPAS DE TRADUCCIÓN
Esperar is three English words at once — to wait, to hope and to expect — and context does all the work. «Te espero abajo» = I'll wait for you downstairs. «Espero verte pronto» = I hope to see you soon. Spanish does not feel this as ambiguity; the three meanings are one idea about the future being open.

Advertir does NOT mean “to advertise.” It means to warn. Advertising is la publicidad or el anuncio. This is one of the more consequential false friends in the language: «me advirtieron» means somebody warned me, not that somebody marketed at me.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Three structural notes worth banking now, because tomorrow you will be busy with something else.

Insistir keeps its en, exactly as confiar did in Módulo 4: insisto en la misma idea, never «insisto la misma idea».

Pedir and preguntar are both “ask” and they are not interchangeable. Pedir asks for a thing — pido un café. Preguntar asks a question — pregunto la hora. Getting these backwards is the single most common intermediate error with this pair.

Four of these are stem-changing in the present: preferir → prefiero, pedir → pido, recomendar → recomiendo, advertir → advierto. You already handle that pattern; note it now because tomorrow those altered stems reappear somewhere new.
🧠 PART 3 · TRES FAMILIAS 9 min

Wanting, asking, allowing

Ten verbs is a lot to hold. Three families is not.

La familiaLos verbosQué haceEjemplo (con sustantivo o infinitivo)
1. Querer · el deseodesear, esperar, preferirYou want an outcome. Nobody is being addressed.Prefiero salir temprano.
2. Pedir · la peticiónpedir, recomendar, insistir en, exigir, advertirYou aim the want at a person.Te recomiendo el ceviche.
3. Permitir · el permisopermitir, prohibirYou open or close a door for someone.No permiten mochilas grandes.

Family 2 almost always carries an indirect object — te, le, les, nos — because the influence has to land on somebody. That is why «te recomiendo» feels incomplete without the te.

👉 POR QUÉ ESTAS FAMILIAS IMPORTAN MAÑANA
All three families share one property, and it is the reason they are grouped: none of them reports anything. Every one is an attempt on the world — wanting it different, asking someone to change it, permitting or forbidding a change.

Tomorrow you will learn that when any of these verbs takes a whole clause instead of a noun or an infinitive, the verb inside that clause shifts mood. Today's «Prefiero salir temprano» becomes «Prefiero que salgamos temprano» — and the shape of salgamos will not surprise you.

So there is nothing to memorise tonight beyond the ten words. The list you just learned is the list of triggers.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

En el mercado de San Pedro

—¿Es tu primera vez en Cusco? Te lo noto en la cara.
—¿Tanto se ve?
—Todos llegan con la misma cara. Mira, te recomiendo el caldo de gallina antes que cualquier otra cosa. Es lo que tomamos aquí cuando el cuerpo no responde.
—Me advirtieron del soroche, pero pensé que exageraban.
—Nadie exagera con la altura. Yo prefiero caminar despacio los primeros días; no es debilidad, es sentido común. Y te pido un favor: no subas mañana. Espera un día más.
—¿Y el mate de coca?
—Ayuda, sí. Pero mucha gente espera milagros de una hoja. Lo que de verdad funciona es tiempo y agua.
—¿Aquí permiten llevarse las hojas?
—Aquí sí. Fuera del país, muchos aeropuertos lo prohíben. Insisto en eso porque cada año alguien se lleva un problema en la mochila.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Seven of today's ten verbs are in that conversation. Find them, and notice what follows each one: a noun (el caldo, un favor, milagros), an infinitive (caminar, llevarse), or a preposition plus a noun (del soroche, en eso). Not one of them takes a que clause. That is a real limitation and you can already feel it: the market seller cannot yet say «te pido que no subas», so she says «te pido un favor» and then gives a command — «no subas mañana».

Tomorrow she gets to say it in one sentence.

Two things worth keeping: «el cuerpo no responde» is how you say your body is not cooperating, and prohíben carries a written accent because -hi- would otherwise merge into one syllable. The h is silent but it does not stop the vowels needing separating.
🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
El país de las tres mil papas

The potato is Peruvian. Not “popular in Peru” — domesticated in the Andes, thousands of years ago, and Peru still grows over three thousand native varieties in colours, shapes and textures that no supermarket outside the region stocks. The Centro Internacional de la Papa in Lima holds one of the world's largest potato genebanks, and part of its work is exactly the conservation problem you met in Módulo 5: keeping varieties alive that no market currently rewards.

Every potato eaten in Europe descends from that Andean stock. So does the tomato's neighbour on the same plate, and la quinua, and the chili. It is worth sitting with how strange that is: a great deal of what the rest of the world considers its own ordinary food came from this one region within the last five centuries.

Peruvian cooking has become one of the most admired in the world on the strength of that inheritance plus a long history of arrival — Spanish, West African, Japanese, Chinese and Italian cooking all folded in. El ceviche is the national dish and has its own national day; lomo saltado is stir-fried beef with soy sauce and chips on the same plate, which tells you the whole story of Chinese-Peruvian chifa cooking in one mouthful.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PRUDENCIA

Look again at the market seller. She has ten ways to push and she uses the gentlest ones — recomiendo, prefiero, te pido un favor — and she reserves her one firm moment (insisto en eso) for the thing that could actually get someone into trouble at an airport.

That is la prudencia operating on vocabulary. Prudence is not being soft; it is spending force where force is warranted and nowhere else. A person who demands everything has no way left to signal that something matters. Quien exige por todo, no puede insistir en nada.
