Lesson 7.09
★ Tarea oral — el consejo que le darías a alguien de verdad
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The first speaking task this semester that is allowed to ask you for the subjunctive.
Today you record the Tarea oral (20 pts) — 20% of your semester grade.

Something is different about this one, and it is worth knowing. Every earlier module's speaking prompt was built so that you could answer it completely without the subjunctive: Módulo 5 asked you to describe and justify and commit, because “recommend that somebody do something” was not yet yours. From today it is. This is the first prompt in Semester 1 that may legitimately require quiero que, te recomiendo que, es mejor que — and it does require them.

The other half of the task is the half students forget: at least one sentence about yourself, with an infinitive and no que. That is scored too, and for a good reason. Anyone can put subjunctives everywhere. Only somebody who has understood the module knows where they do not go.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to speak for 60–90 seconds advising one real person about one real decision.
2
I will be able to pronounce the final vowel that carries the mood, so hable does not arrive as habla.
3
I will be able to use an infinitive out loud, on purpose, in the middle of a task about the subjunctive.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Seis palabras, y una persona real

Same method as every module: a six-word outline, never a written-out script. A read script always sounds read, and with advice it is worse than usual — the whole force of a recommendation comes from sounding like somebody who has thought about this person.

Which is why the task asks for a real one. Not a character, not “a friend” in general: someone who genuinely has a decision in front of them this month. You will find you have more to say, and the Spanish gets easier when you do.

🎤 PART 2 · LA TAREA 10 min

El tema: Piensa en una persona real que tiene que tomar una decisión pronto. Explica cuál es la decisión, dile qué quieres que haga, recomiéndale dos cosas, adviértele de un riesgo, y termina diciendo por qué tu consejo te parece prudente.

Note that the prompt itself is written in the grammar of the two modules before this one: recomiéndale and adviértele are affirmative tú commands with the pronoun attached and the tilde holding the stress — Módulo 4. Your recording will not be commands, though. It will be the polite version of them.

🧠 PART 3 · TU GUION DE SEIS PALABRAS 9 min
BeatYour wordEjemploEstructura
1. La persona y la decisión Valeria · becapresente, indicativo
2. Lo que tú quieres tiempoquiero que + subjuntivo
3. Recomendación 1 hablarte recomiendo que + subjuntivo
4. Recomendación 2 listaes mejor / importante que + subjuntivo
5. La advertencia prisael riesgo · la precaución · advertir
6. Tú mismo esperarinfinitivo, sin que

Beats 5 and 6 are both assessed, and both are the ones people cut when they run short. Write those two words first.

—Valeria, sé que tienes que decidir esta semana si aceptas la beca, y sé que estás contentísima y aterrada a la vez. Yo quiero que te tomes unos días antes de contestar, porque una decisión así no se toma en una tarde. Te recomiendo que hables con dos personas que ya estudiaron allí, y también que escribas las ventajas y las desventajas en papel, porque en la cabeza todo pesa lo mismo. Es importante que decidas tú y no tu familia. El riesgo aquí es la prisa: si decides con prisa, decides por miedo, y eso no es una decisión prudente. Yo prefiero esperar y perder una semana antes que arrepentirme un año.

Check yours against four things. Four clauses off three triggers — quiero que te tomes, te recomiendo que hables, …y también que escribas, es importante que decidas. One infinitive in the last sentence — yo prefiero esperar, where the preferrer and the waiter are both me. The warning names a riesgo rather than gesturing at a worry. And the module vocabulary is doing work: las ventajas, las desventajas, el riesgo, la decisión, prudente.

Two things in that model are worth copying deliberately. The third clause reuses the second trigger without repeating it — te recomiendo que hables… y también que escribas… — which is what Spanish does instead of saying te recomiendo twice, and it is exactly the move Palma's pedlar made yesterday when pido y ruego shared one que. And every trigger is in the present. Keep them there. Advice you are giving now is present tense; if you want to mention advice somebody gave you in the past, say it with a noun instead — me recomendó dos días en Cusco, me pidió ayuda, nos advirtió del riesgo. All three are natural, and all three keep you inside the grammar you own.

One more guard rail. If, while you are speaking, you find yourself reaching for a subjunctive after a vague person — “somebody who…”, “anyone who…” — stop and name a definite person instead, as the model does with dos personas que ya estudiaron allí. Vague antecedents follow a different rule that belongs to a later course, and you never need it: a named person takes the indicative and says the same thing.
⭐ ENSAYA TRES VECES
Say the whole thing aloud three times from your six words. On run three, listen for exactly two things: did beat 6 survive, and can you hear the final vowel on every subjunctive. Those are the two criteria most takes lose points on, and both are fixed in the rehearsal rather than in the recording.
🔊 PART 4 · LA VOCAL QUE LLEVA EL MODO 10 min

Click each and repeat aloud. Then read the three notes underneath, because this module's pronunciation problem is unlike any other module's.

  • Quiero que estudies.hear the «que», and hear the final -s
  • Recomiendo que lo intentes.advice, one step less blunt than a command
  • Es necesario que llegues temprano.the impersonal frame
  • hable, hables, hable, hablemos, hablen-ar verbs take «e» — that vowel is the grammar
  • coma, comas, comamos, coman; viva, vivas, vivamos, vivan-er and -ir verbs take «a»

1. The mood is one unstressed vowel, and English throws those away. Habla and hable differ in exactly one sound, at the end, unstressed. So do estudias / estudies, llega / llegue, come / coma. English reduces almost every unstressed final vowel to a neutral “uh” — and if you do that in Spanish, the entire grammatical distinction disappears. Your listener hears a description where you meant a recommendation. Say the final vowel as a clean, full e or a, even though it is unstressed. This is the one place in the course where a vowel carries a whole mood.

2. Do not swallow que. It is short, unstressed and sits in the middle of the sentence, which is exactly the profile of a word that disappears. But que is the hinge: without it the listener hears two verbs collide. Keep it audible and put the tiniest break in front of it — quiero · que llegues temprano.

3. Dé and esté are stressed on that final vowel. These two carry a written tilde precisely because the stress lands there, against the default. Said with the stress in front — “DE”, “ES-te” — they turn into the preposition de and the demonstrative este, and the sentence stops meaning anything. If your advice uses quiero que le dé las gracias or es mejor que estés en casa, land those.

🌎 CULTURA · PERÚ
El huayno, la marinera, y un cajón que se fue a España

A recording day is a good day to talk about Peruvian sound, and Peru has three musical traditions rather than one — Andean, coastal criollo, and Afro-Peruvian — which map roughly onto the sierra, the cities of the coast, and the towns south of Lima.

El huayno is the Andean one, and it is not folklore in the museum sense: it is the most listened-to music across much of highland Peru, played on quena, mandolin, violin and the Peruvian Andean harp, sung in Quechua, in Spanish, or in both inside one song. Its dance is a couple dance with handkerchiefs and small quick steps. La marinera is the coastal counterpart, a courtship dance also danced with handkerchiefs, and the national dance of Peru; the annual national contest in Trujillo is a serious institution, with hundreds of couples competing in age categories from small children upward.

The detail worth carrying away is Afro-Peruvian. El cajón — literally “the box” — is a wooden box you sit on and play with your hands, developed by enslaved and free Black communities on the Peruvian coast. In 1977 the Spanish guitarist Paco de Lucía heard one in Lima, was given one, and took it back to Spain. Within a decade it had become standard in flamenco, where it now sounds as though it had always belonged.

So if you have ever heard flamenco, you have heard a Peruvian instrument, and probably assumed it was Spanish. Instruments travel faster than the credit for them.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA PRUDENCIA

The task asks for two recommendations, not five, and one risk, not every risk. That limit is the prudence in it. Advice fails by volume more often than by error: a person facing a hard decision can act on two things and will act on none if you give them nine.

And notice the last beat. You end by saying what you would do — an infinitive, one subject, no clause — which quietly admits that your advice is your view and not a verdict. That is the difference between advising and deciding for somebody, and Spanish marks it on the verb. El buen consejo es corto, y deja la decisión donde estaba.
