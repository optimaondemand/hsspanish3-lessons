★ Lectura interpretativa — un texto sin un solo mandato
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Three sentences of real advice about money in the Dominican Republic — and not one command in them.
Módulo 4's reading day, and the opposite situation from Módulo 3. In 3.08 a travel guide handed you three commands in a single sentence. Today's text, from a guide to the same website's Dominican Republic page, gives genuinely useful guidance entirely in statements. El peso es… El dólar es… El euro es…

So today's grammar work runs in the other direction: you will turn description into advice. That is a harder skill than spotting an imperative, and it is much closer to what you actually do when a friend asks you what they should do.

The graded Lectura interpretativa (15 pts, two attempts) opens today.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to say which currency the text recommends for which setting, and why.
2
I will be able to rewrite the text's information as advice to a friend, using affirmative and negative tú commands.
3
I will be able to explain why frecuentadas ends in -as, and what that proves about participles.
⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 6 min

Contexto y tres cosas que buscar

El contexto. This is from the Comprar section of a collaboratively written, openly licensed Spanish-language travel guide to the Dominican Republic. Three currencies circulate in the country in practice — the Dominican peso, the US dollar and the euro — and the passage explains where each one actually works. It is the kind of information a friend who has been there tells you at the airport.

Busca estas tres cosas. No dictionary on the first two passes:

  1. ¿Qué moneda conviene en los lugares de los dominicanos, y qué moneda en las zonas turísticas?
  2. ¿Cuántos mandatos hay en el texto? Cuéntalos con cuidado.
  3. ¿Qué palabra describe los lugares y las actividades, y por qué termina en -as?
⭐ UNA ESTRUCTURA DE TRES · a technique
The passage has an obvious skeleton: three sentences, each opening with a currency and the verb es. El peso es… El dólar es… El euro es… Read the three openings first and you have the shape of the whole thing before you translate a word of the detail.

Question 2 above is not a trick. The answer is a number, and the number matters — hold onto it until Part 4.
📚 PART 2 · EL TEXTO 10 min
📖 LECTURA AUTÉNTICA · TEXTO CON LICENCIA ABIERTA
El peso es la mejor moneda para utilizar en lugares o actividades frecuentadas por los dominicanos (Autobuses, tiendas de la capital...). El dólar es la moneda de los resorts y las zonas turísticas, los precios de base se ponen en dólares y después se cambian a otras monedas. El euro es la moneda de los bancos, el mejor cambio se puede obtener con estos.
Wikivoyage, «República Dominicana», sección Comprar. Texto disponible bajo licencia Creative Commons Atribución-CompartirIgual 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Ayuda de vocabulario — only after your second reading: la moneda = currency (also a coin) · utilizar = to use · frecuentadas por los dominicanos = frequented by Dominicans · los resorts = the resorts, borrowed straight from English in Dominican tourism · la zona turística = tourist area · el precio de base = the base price · el cambio = the exchange, the exchange rate · obtener = to obtain, to get.

Three forms are not yours yet: se ponen, se cambian, se puede obtener. Read them as “are set”, “are changed” and “can be obtained” and move on. This is the impersonal se, a construction you meet properly in Semester 2 of this course; nothing on this page or in the graded item asks you to use it. Authentic texts always contain grammar from further along the road, and reading past an unfamiliar form is itself a skill.

Two other things you may notice, both genuine features of a collaboratively written text rather than errors on your part: the parenthesis begins with a capital letter, and the second and third sentences join two clauses with a comma where an editor would have used a semicolon or y. Real Spanish, written by volunteers, is not copy-edited prose — and being able to read it anyway is the point of this strand.

🧠 PART 4 · DE LA DESCRIPCIÓN AL CONSEJO 9 min

Zero commands in, six commands out

The answer to question 2 is none. Not one imperative in three sentences. Now do what the text does not: say the same things to a friend.

What the text saysSaid to a friend — afirmativoSaid to a friend — negativo
El peso es la mejor moneda… frecuentadas por los dominicanosUsa pesos en los autobuses.No pagues el autobús en dólares.
El dólar es la moneda de los resorts y las zonas turísticasPaga el hotel en dólares.No uses dólares fuera de las zonas turísticas.
El euro es la moneda de los bancos, el mejor cambio…Cámbialos en un banco.No los cambies en el aeropuerto.

Look at the last row, because it contains the whole module. Cámbialos: affirmative, pronoun attached, three syllables, tilde required. No los cambies: negative, the same pronoun in front, no tilde, and the verb built from the Ud. command no cambie plus -s. One pair of sentences, both halves of Days 4 and 5.

⭐ POR QUÉ ESTE TEXTO NO MANDA
This is worth a moment, because it is a real difference in genre and not an accident. A safety paragraph instructs — Módulo 3's Bogotá sentence was three commands in a row, because the writer wanted one specific behaviour from you. A money paragraph informs, because what to do with the information depends on where you are going and what you are buying.

So the register tells you what the writer assumes about the reader. Commands assume there is one right action. Statements assume you will decide. Notice which one your own advice tends to use, and whether the person you are advising would agree with the assumption.
💡 FRECUENTADAS · el Módulo 2, otra vez
Question 3. Frecuentadas is a participle — the same kind of form as hablado, hecho, escrito — and here it ends in -as. Apply Módulo 2's test: is there a haber in front of it?

There is not. So it is not part of a perfect tense; it is working as an adjective, and adjectives agree with what they describe. Actividades is feminine plural, so the participle is frecuentadas. Compare «un lugar frecuentado», «unas actividades frecuentadas» — and then compare «han frecuentado», which never changes because it follows haber.

One honest note about this particular sentence. The text says «lugares o actividades frecuentadas», and lugares is masculine while actividades is feminine. A careful editor would write frecuentados, since the masculine plural is what covers a mixed pair. The source agrees with the nearer noun instead — which is extremely common in real writing, and which you now know enough grammar to have noticed.
🎤 PART 5 · PUENTE A LA PRODUCCIÓN 6 min

That passage is practical knowledge handed from someone who knows to someone who does not. Tomorrow's Tarea oral asks you to do the same thing out loud, as a voice note to a friend. Choose your situation now:

Un amigo o una amiga te ha pedido consejo. ¿Sobre qué, y qué le vas a decir?

Two kinds of situation work well. One is a decision — a job, a team, a class, a conversation they are dreading. The other is practical knowledge you happen to have and they do not, exactly like the currency passage: how to get somewhere, how to handle a particular teacher, what to do first in a place you know well.

🌎 CULTURA · REPÚBLICA DOMINICANA
Dos economías en una isla

The country's currency is the peso dominicano, written RD$ — and the dollar sign in that abbreviation is why prices sometimes look, to a visitor, ten times cheaper or dearer than they are.

What the passage records without comment is genuinely how the country works. Tourism is one of the Dominican Republic's largest industries, concentrated in resort areas such as Punta Cana and Bávaro that are among the biggest in the Caribbean, and inside those areas prices are quoted in dollars. A few kilometres away, in a colmado or on a guagua, the peso is simply the money. Both economies are real; the text is telling you which one you are standing in.

A third flow matters just as much and is invisible in the passage: remesas — money sent home by Dominicans living abroad, above all in the United States — are one of the country's major sources of foreign income. That is the economic fact behind the hermano mayor in 4.05 advising a younger sibling from another country. The advice and the money travel down the same phone line.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO

Somebody volunteered three sentences about currency for readers they will never meet, and those three sentences quietly save a stranger from being overcharged in a taxi. It is unglamorous, unsigned and genuinely useful — which is most of what service looks like. Notice too that the writer trusted the reader with the information rather than issuing orders. Servir también es decirle a alguien lo que a ti nadie te dijo.
