★ Problemas y soluciones — cómo se arregla algo
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Advice is easy when nothing is wrong. This is the vocabulary for when something is.
Yesterday you learned to offer advice. Today you learn the words for the situations that actually need it: a misunderstanding, an argument, an apology, a repair.

Plus a four-part shape for the conversation itself — because in any language, the difference between fixing something and making it worse is largely a matter of order.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use ten words for problems, disagreement and repair.
2
I will be able to use disculparse and perdonar correctly, and explain who does what in each.
3
I will be able to name the four moves of a repair conversation.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

One word English speakers reliably get wrong

Discutir does not mean “to discuss.” It means to argue. If you tell a Spanish speaker «discutí con mi profesora», you have said you had an argument with her — not that you talked something over.

For a calm conversation you want hablar de, comentar or tratar. Retire “discutir = discuss” today; it causes real misunderstandings, which is fitting for a lesson about misunderstandings.

⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
El problema is masculine despite ending in -a — it comes from Greek, like el tema, el sistema, el programa. So un problema grave, never «una problema».

And note tener razón: Spanish says you have reason rather than are right. Tienes razón = you're right. «Eres correcto» is not how this is said.
🧠 PART 3 · QUIÉN HACE QUÉ 9 min

Apologising and forgiving are opposite jobs

VerbWho does itStructureExample
disculparseThe person at faultReflexive + con + personMe disculpé con ella.
perdonarThe person wrongedTakes a direct objectElla me perdonó.
pedir perdónThe person at fault+ a + personLe pedí perdón a mi hermano.
hacer las pacesBoth — mutualOften + conHicimos las paces el viernes.

The error to avoid: «te disculpo» does not mean “I apologise to you.” Disculparse is reflexive — the person apologising is the subject and the pronoun: me disculpo. If you want to say you forgive someone, that is te perdono.

💬 LOS CUATRO MOVIMIENTOS DE UN ARREGLO
1. Nombrar el problema — say what happened, without blame. Creo que hubo un malentendido.
2. Escuchar — ask, then stop talking. ¿Cómo lo viste tú?
3. Reconocer — concede what is true. Tienes razón en eso.
4. Proponer — offer a way forward. ¿Hacemos las paces?

Move 3 is the one people skip, and skipping it is why step 4 fails. Conceding one true thing costs almost nothing and changes the whole conversation.

Ahora tú: write a four-line repair conversation, one line per move from Part 3. Say it aloud before revealing.

—Creo que hubo un malentendido con lo del sábado.
—¿Cómo lo viste tú?
—Tienes razón en que no te avisé a tiempo. Eso fue mi culpa y me disculpo.
—Está bien. ¿Hacemos las paces y lo dejamos ahí?

Check yours: un malentendido (masculine) · tener razón rather than «ser correcto» · me disculpo reflexive · and no discutir anywhere, because nobody is arguing.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

El grupo de la presentación

Marcos y yo discutimos el martes. Él había hecho toda la parte de las diapositivas sin avisarme, y yo había escrito el guion de la misma sección. Perdimos dos días de trabajo.

El miércoles no nos hablamos. El jueves le escribí: «Creo que hubo un malentendido y prefiero hablarlo antes de la presentación». Me contestó en dos minutos.

Resulta que la profesora le había dicho una cosa y a mí otra. Tenía razón en que él no lo sabía; yo tenía razón en que debíamos habernos escrito antes. Nos disculpamos los dos, hicimos las paces y rehicimos la sección en una tarde. La presentación salió bien.

Ahora, antes de empezar cualquier trabajo en grupo, mandamos un mensaje que dice quién hace qué. Es la solución más aburrida del mundo y funciona.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find the four moves from Part 3. Then notice the module-2 grammar doing real work: three past perfects (había hecho, había escrito, había dicho) all establishing what had happened before the argument — which is precisely why the argument happened. Remove them and the story stops making sense.

And notice discutimos in line 1 is used correctly: they really did argue.
🌎 CULTURA · REPÚBLICA DOMINICANA
Directo no es descortés

Dominican conversational style is noticeably direct and loud by US standards — overlapping speech, raised voices, strong opinions stated plainly. Visitors regularly misread an ordinary lively exchange as an argument.

It is worth knowing for this module specifically. In a Dominican context, saying «no estoy de acuerdo» flatly is not aggressive, and volume is not anger. Conversely, the very indirect Anglo style — hedging, softening, never quite saying the thing — can read as evasive rather than polite.

So the four moves in Part 3 are not a script for being gentle. They are a script for being clear, which in most of the Spanish-speaking world is the more courteous option.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO

The most serviceable act in that reading is the least dramatic one: the message on Thursday. Somebody had to write first, and writing first costs pride. El servicio often means absorbing a small loss so that a shared thing can work — and then, as the last line shows, building the boring system that stops it happening again. Servir es escribir primero, sin esperar tu turno.
