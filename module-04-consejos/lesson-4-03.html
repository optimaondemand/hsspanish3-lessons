Lesson 4.03
★ Mandatos afirmativos con tú — y ocho cortos
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The easiest command form in Spanish — you already know it without knowing it.
In Módulo 3 you built formal commands from scratch: tome, visite, no lleve. The tú affirmative command is far simpler, and Spanish 2 gave you a first pass at it.

The rule is one line: take the él/ella form of the present tense. Habla. Come. Escribe. That is it.

Then there are eight short irregulars — and because they are the eight most useful verbs in the language, they carry most of the advice friends actually give each other.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to form the affirmative tú command of any regular verb from its él/ella present form.
2
I will be able to produce the eight irregulars — di, haz, ve, pon, sal, sé, ten, ven — from memory.
3
I will be able to explain why sé carries an accent when the others do not.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

You have been reading these all module

«Escúchame un segundo.»
«Confía en tu preparación.»
«Dime a ver.»

All three appeared in Lesson 4.01, and all three are affirmative tú commands. You understood them without being taught them — which tells you the form is close to something you already own.

It is: the él/ella present tense. Él confía → ¡Confía! Same word, different job.

Predict: if él come is “he eats,” how do you tell a friend to eat?

⭐ COMPARA CON EL MÓDULO 3
Two registers, side by side:

To a stranger: «Hable con la recepcionista.» — the Ud. command from Module 3
To a friend: «Habla con ella hoy.» — the tú command from today

One letter apart, and the whole relationship changes. Note which direction the vowel moves: the Ud. form takes the “opposite” vowel (-ar verb → e), while the tú form keeps its own (-ar verb → a).

✏️ EL ACENTO DE SÉ · this module grades accents
Only one of the eight carries a written accent, and it is doing a job: sé (be!) must be distinguished from se, the pronoun in se llama, se fue.

This is a tilde diacrítica — an accent used purely to separate two identical-looking words. You already know others: tú (you) vs tu (your) · mí (me) vs mi (my) · él (he) vs el (the).

So «Sé sincero» means be sincere. Without the accent it is a different word entirely.

One more thing worth noticing: ve is the command of ir (go), and it is also the él/ella form of ver (he sees). Context separates them without trouble — «ve al médico» can only mean go.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · the rule
Tell a friend to sleep: «___ bien esta noche.» (dormir)
✅ TRY IT · an irregular
«___ la verdad, aunque cuesta.» (decir)
✅ TRY IT · the accent that carries meaning
«___ paciente con él.» (ser — be patient)
✅ TRY IT · register check
A nurse tells you to sit down. Which would she say?

Ahora tú: give a nervous friend four pieces of advice as commands, using at least three of the eight irregulars.

Ten paciencia contigo mismo. Haz una lista de lo que ya sabes. Sal a caminar veinte minutos antes de estudiar. Y sé sincero con la profesora si necesitas más tiempo.

Check yours: single-syllable irregulars (ten, haz, sal, sé) · the accent on sé and on nothing else · and tú forms throughout, since this is a friend.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Nota en la puerta del refrigerador

Hermanito:

Me fui temprano. Unas cosas:

Desayuna algo de verdad, no solo café. Pon los platos en el lavaplatos, no en el fregadero. Si mamá llama, dile que llego a las seis. Ve a la farmacia antes de las cinco y trae el jarabe de la receta.

Y una cosa más: ten paciencia con el profesor de mates. Sé amable aunque no te gusta mucho. Habla con él después de clase y pide ayuda con el tema cinco — te va a ir mejor de lo que crees.

Nos vemos.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Eleven commands in a note on a fridge — this is what the form is actually for. Sort them: which come straight from the él/ella present (desayuna, trae, habla, pide) and which are from the eight irregulars (pon, di, ve, ten, sé)?

Then find dile and notice the pronoun stuck onto the end. That is Day 6's lesson, and it is already happening naturally in front of you.
🌎 CULTURA · REPÚBLICA DOMINICANA
Mandar no es mandonear

English speakers often feel that giving a bare command sounds bossy, and soften everything into questions: could you maybe…? In Spanish, and markedly in the Caribbean, the plain imperative between people who know each other is normal and friendly — «dime», «ven acá», «pásame eso».

Dominicans in particular use the imperative constantly as an expression of closeness, not authority. Over-softening can actually create distance: hedging with someone who considers you a friend can read as oddly formal.

What does soften a command everywhere is the same thing as in English — a name, a por favor, or a reason attached: «Ve a la farmacia, que cierran a las cinco». Note how the fridge note does exactly that in its last line.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO

Read the fridge note again and notice what kind of document it is: an older sibling leaving early, thinking about somebody else's breakfast, prescription and difficult maths teacher. Eleven commands, every one of them in the reader's interest. El servicio is frequently indistinguishable from ordinary household instructions — which is what makes it easy to miss and hard to fake. Servir se parece mucho a una nota en el refrigerador.
