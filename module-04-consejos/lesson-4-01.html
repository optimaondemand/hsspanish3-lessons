★ Dar y pedir consejos — el vocabulario de la amistad
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Module 3 taught you to address a stranger. This module is about talking to a friend.
In Módulo 3 you learned formal commands — tome, visite, no lleve — the forms an institution uses on you. Useful, and slightly distant.

Módulo 4 turns that around: consejos entre amigos. Same job, completely different register. And the grammar you need for it is the piece Spanish 2 only half-gave you: the tú command, including its negative forms.

Today: ten words for advice and support. No new grammar — you already have more than enough to give advice with.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use ten words for advice, help and trust.
2
I will be able to distinguish aconsejar, sugerir and recomendar and use each correctly.
3
I will be able to explain when Spanish uses tú and when it uses usted, and why choosing wrong matters.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

The choice English does not make you make

Every time you address someone in Spanish you choose a relationship. Usted marks distance or respect — a doctor, a shopkeeper, an older stranger, an official form. Tú marks closeness — a friend, a classmate, a sibling, someone your own age.

English gives you no way to get this wrong. Spanish does, and the wrong choice is audible: tú to a nurse can sound rude, usted to a friend can sound cold or sarcastic.

✏️ ESCRIBIR CON TILDES · you will be graded on this
Today's la recomendación and tomorrow's la solución both end in -ción, and that ending always carries a tilde in the singular. In this course accents are graded, including in typed short answers.

There is a pattern worth banking here, because it costs you nothing to learn and pays out for the rest of the language: the tilde on -ción disappears in the plural. La recomendación → las recomendaciones. Adding the syllable moves the stress to where it would fall naturally, so the written mark is no longer needed.

Fix your typing now rather than in the middle of a quiz: Cómo escribir las tildes y la ñ — Windows, Mac, phone and Chromebook.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Two structural notes. El consejo is countable in Spanish where English “advice” is not: te doy un consejo = I'll give you a piece of advice, and muchos consejos is perfectly normal. And confiar keeps its en: confío en ti, never «confío ti». Note also ti has no accent — unlike mí, which does.
🧠 PART 3 · TRES VERBOS DE CONSEJO 8 min

Advise, suggest, recommend — and how strong each one is

VerbStrengthTypical useExample
sugerirSoftest — an idea offeredYou are floating a possibilityTe sugiero una idea.
recomendarMiddle — a considered opinionYou have experience of itTe recomiendo ese libro.
aconsejarStrongest — personal counselYou think they should actTe aconsejo paciencia.

All three take an indirect object — te, le, les — because advice goes to someone. And notice all three are stem-changing: sugerir → sugiero, recomendar → recomiendo, while aconsejar is regular.

💡 UNA NOTA PARA EL MÓDULO 7
These three verbs have a second construction you will meet in Módulo 7: te aconsejo que…, te sugiero que…. What follows que is the subjunctive, and it is one of the main reasons this course teaches commands first.

For now, use them the simple way — with a noun (te aconsejo paciencia) or an infinitive (te sugiero esperar). Both are correct, natural Spanish and they need nothing you do not already have.

Ahora tú: a friend tells you they are nervous about an exam. Give three responses — one with each verb from Part 3. Say them aloud before revealing.

—Te sugiero estudiar con alguien; a mí me ayuda mucho. Te recomiendo el resumen que hizo la profesora, es muy claro. Y te aconsejo dormir bien la noche anterior: es lo que más se nota.

Check yours: each verb with an indirect object (te) · the infinitive construction rather than que · and the strength rising from suggestion to counsel.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Mensajes a medianoche

—No puedo dormir. Mañana tengo la entrevista y creo que lo voy a hacer mal.
—Escúchame un segundo. ¿Cuántas veces has practicado las respuestas?
—Como diez. Pero igual estoy nervioso.
—Entonces ya lo tienes. Confía en tu preparación. Te aconsejo cerrar el ordenador y dormir.
—¿Y si me preguntan algo que no sé?
—Les dices la verdad. Nadie te va a pedir saberlo todo. Te sugiero una frase: «No lo sé, pero puedo averiguarlo».
—Gracias. De verdad.
—Para eso estamos. Mañana me cuentas.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Every line is tú — this is a friendship, not a consultation. Find the two commands hiding in it: escúchame and confía. Those are tú commands with a pronoun attached, and they are exactly what Days 3 to 6 will teach you to build. Notice escúchame carries a written accent that escucha does not — there is a reason, and you will learn it on Day 6.

Notice too what the friend does not do: no lecture, no minimising. One question, one instruction, one useful phrase.
🌎 CULTURA · REPÚBLICA DOMINICANA
«Dime a ver»

This module's country is the República Dominicana, and one of its most characteristic phrases is a command: «dime a ver» — roughly “go on then, tell me.” Dominicans answer the phone with it. It is warm, direct, and grammatically exactly what this module teaches: a tú command with a pronoun attached.

Dominican Spanish is famously fast and it drops final s — los dos can arrive as lo' do', and ¿cómo estás? as ¿cómo etá?. That is a feature of Caribbean Spanish, not carelessness, and it is one reason this course trains your ear on clearer central-Mexican audio first: you need a stable centre before the variations become interesting rather than alarming.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO

This module's virtue is el servicio — service. Notice where it appears in tonight's messages: the friend who is awake at midnight is not solving anything, and offers no wisdom. They ask one question and stay on the line. Most service looks like that rather than like rescue. Servir casi nunca es resolver; casi siempre es quedarse.
