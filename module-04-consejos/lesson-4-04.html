★ Dímelo — el pronombre se pega igual que en usted
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You already learned this rule in Módulo 3. Today it costs you nothing.
Yesterday you built affirmative tú commands. Today you attach pronouns to them: dímelo, ayúdame, escúchalo.

And here is the good news: the rule is identical to the one you learned for usted. Pronouns attach to the end of an affirmative command, indirect before direct, and the tilde appears to hold the stress in place. Nothing about that changes when the addressee becomes a friend.

What is worth ten minutes is the one place tú behaves differently from usted: the reflexive pronoun is te, and with a couple of very common verbs that produces a form you should meet deliberately.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to attach one or two pronouns to an affirmative tú command, with the tilde where it belongs.
2
I will be able to use reflexive tú commands like levántate and tranquilízate.
3
I will be able to explain why ponte and vete look shorter than expected.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Two registers, one rule

To a stranger: Dígame  ·  Sígame  ·  Siéntese
To a friend:   Dime    ·  Sígueme   ·  Siéntate

The verb changed register. The mechanism did not: pronoun on the end, written as one word, tilde wherever the stress needs holding.

Note the exception hiding in plain sight — dime has no tilde while dígame does. That is not an inconsistency, and Part 3 explains it in one line.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Say dime and dígame aloud. Why might one need a tilde and the other not?

✏️ PART 3 · LA TILDE, OTRA VEZ 10 min

Count the syllables. That is the whole test.

The Módulo 3 rule, unchanged: Spanish stresses a vowel-final word on its second-to-last syllable by default. A tilde is needed only when the real stress sits somewhere else.

The working rule: attach one pronoun to a one-syllable command and you usually need no tilde (dime, ponte, hazlo, dilo). Attach one to a longer command, or attach two to anything, and you almost always do (dímelo, ayúdame, levántate).

💡 DOS FORMAS QUE PIERDEN UNA LETRA
The eight one-syllable irregulars from yesterday take pronouns without fuss, and because they are so short the result usually needs no tilde:

pon + te → ponte  ·  ve + te → vete  ·  haz + lo → hazlo  ·  di + lo → dilo

So «ponte el abrigo» (put your coat on), «vete a casa» (go home), «hazlo ahora» (do it now). All two syllables, all stressed on the first, all following the default — so no tilde.

Add a second pronoun and every one of them crosses into tilde territory: dímelo, házmelo. Three syllables, and the stress has to be held.
🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · attach and accent
Your friend is upset and you want to say “tell me about it”. (contar + me)
✅ TRY IT · one syllable, one pronoun
“Tell me” — decir, tú, with me.
✅ TRY IT · add a second pronoun
Now “tell it to me” — the same verb with me and lo.
✅ TRY IT · reflexive
A friend is panicking before a test. «___ , vas a estar bien.» (tranquilizarse)

Ahora tú: a friend has had a bad day. Give four tú commands with attached pronouns.

Cuéntame qué pasó. Escúchame un segundo: no fue tu culpa. Tranquilízate y come algo. Y llámame esta noche si quieres hablar más.

Check yours: tildes on cuéntame, escúchame, tranquilízate, llámame — all three or more syllables, all against the default. And come with nothing attached, so no tilde.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Mensajes de voz, transcritos

—Oye, llámame esta noche. No es urgente pero cuéntame cómo te fue.

—Ya salí del examen. Escúchame: creo que me fue bien. Dímelo tú, porque tú lo hiciste ayer — ¿la última pregunta era imposible o soy yo?

—Era imposible. Tranquilízate. La profesora dijo que la iba a revisar.

—Menos mal. Bueno, ponte algo abrigado, que vamos a caminar hasta el parque. Y hazme un favor: tráeme el cuaderno de historia, lo dejé en tu casa.

—Listo. Vete saliendo y nos vemos en la esquina.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Ten commands with attached pronouns, and the rule from Part 3 holds throughout:

Three or more syllables → tilde: llá-ma-me, cuén-ta-me, es-cú-cha-me, dí-me-lo, tran-qui-lí-za-te, trá-e-me
Two syllables → no tilde: pon-te, ve-te, haz-me

Compare hazme and tráeme directly. Both are a one-syllable irregular command plus me — but haz ends in a consonant and stays two syllables, while trae already carries two vowels and becomes three. Same construction, different length, different answer. That is why the test is count the syllables rather than memorise a list.
🌎 CULTURA · REPÚBLICA DOMINICANA
Las notas de voz

The reading above is a transcript of voice notes, and that is not an arbitrary choice. Across the Dominican Republic and much of Latin America, WhatsApp voice notes have largely replaced typed messages among friends and family — sent in strings, listened to at double speed, answered with another voice note.

Two consequences for a learner. First, the Spanish you will actually receive from Dominican friends is spoken, fast, with dropped final s — closer to 4.01's warning about lo' do' than to anything on a page. Second, the register is full of exactly today's grammar: dime, cuéntame, llámame, mándame. Attached-pronoun commands are the connective tissue of informal Spanish.

Which is a good argument for learning to say them properly rather than only to write them.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO

Look at what the commands in that exchange are actually doing: cuéntame, escúchame, tranquilízate, ponte algo abrigado. Every one is in the other person's interest, and two of them are requests for the friend's attention rather than instructions. El servicio between friends is mostly this — small imperatives that mean I am paying attention to you. Pedir que te cuenten también es una forma de servir.
