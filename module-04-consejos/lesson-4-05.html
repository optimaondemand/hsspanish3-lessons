Lesson 4.05
★ No hables — la forma que vuelve en el Módulo 7
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The one place tú is genuinely different — and the single most important form in the semester.
With usted, the negative was free: add no to the form you already had. With tú it is not. Habla does not become «no habla». It becomes no hables — a different form entirely.

Where does that form come from? From the usted command you built in Módulo 3, plus an -s. No hable → no hables. One letter.

And this is the form that returns in Módulo 7 as the subjunctive. If you learn one thing properly this semester, learn this.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to form the negative tú command of any verb from its Ud. command plus -s.
2
I will be able to explain why the affirmative and negative tú commands look unrelated.
3
I will be able to move pronouns in front of a negative tú command, without a tilde.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Two commands that do not look related

Habla con ella.  →  No hables con ella.
Come ahora.     →  No comas ahora.
Ve a casa.       →  No vayas a casa.

Look at the third pair especially: ve and no vayas share almost nothing. That is not an irregularity to memorise — it is because the two forms come from completely different places. The affirmative borrows the él/ella present; the negative comes from the usted command.

⭐ LOS DOS CAMINOS · why the forms differ
Afirmativo tú ← the él/ella present tense  (él habla → ¡habla!)
Negativo tú ← the usted command + s  (no hable → no hables)

Two different sources, so no wonder they look unrelated. Once you know where each comes from, neither needs memorising.

Nothing new. The order is the fixed sequence you have now met three times: no + pronoun(s) + verb. Same as the Ud. negatives in Módulo 3, and same as the perfect in Módulo 1 (no me he sentido bien).

💡 NO TE PREOCUPES
Learn this one as a whole phrase, because you will use it constantly and it contains everything from today: negative tú command, built from no se preocupe + s, with the reflexive pronoun moved in front and no tilde.

Its usted partner, «no se preocupe», appeared in Módulo 3's Bogotá receptionist. Same reassurance, two registers.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Consejos de un hermano mayor

Mañana empiezas en la universidad, así que te dejo cuatro cosas.

No te sientes siempre con la misma gente en la cafetería. Ya sé que es cómodo, pero el primer mes es cuando se hacen los amigos. Habla con alguien nuevo cada día, aunque cuesta.

No dejes las lecturas para el domingo. No me digas que tú funcionas mejor bajo presión, porque eso me lo dijiste el año pasado y no funcionó.

No seas tímido con los profesores. Ve a las horas de oficina desde la primera semana, incluso sin preguntas. Y no te compares con la gente que parece tenerlo todo resuelto; casi ninguno lo tiene.

Cualquier cosa, llámame. A cualquier hora.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Seven negative commands and three affirmative. Check every negative against the rule: no te sientes (from no se siente), no dejes (no deje), no me digas (no diga), no seas (no sea), no te compares (no se compare). Every one is a Módulo 3 form with an -s.

Then note the three affirmatives — habla, ve, llámame — and that ve and no vayas would be the same verb in opposite forms. Only llámame carries a tilde, because only it has three syllables.
🌎 CULTURA · REPÚBLICA DOMINICANA
El hermano mayor

The letter above is written from a position that carries real weight in Dominican families: the hermano mayor. Across much of Latin America older siblings hold a semi-parental advisory role, especially where families are large or a parent works abroad — and in the Dominican Republic, remittances from relatives in the United States are a substantial part of the economy, so an older sibling abroad advising a younger one at home is an entirely ordinary situation.

Two things follow for the language. Advice from an hermano mayor is expected to be direct — the string of blunt negative commands in that letter would read as caring, not controlling. And the closing offer, «llámame, a cualquier hora», is the part that makes the directness land: authority plus availability.

It is worth noticing how unlike an American self-help register that is. No hedging, no “you might want to consider.” Just no dejes las lecturas — and then a phone number.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO

The hardest sentence in that letter is «no me digas que tú funcionas mejor bajo presión» — because it refuses an excuse the younger sibling has used before, and does it by name. That is a service that costs something: it is far easier to be encouraging than to be accurate. El servicio sometimes means saying the thing your friend would rather you did not. Servir también es negarse a creer una excusa que ya falló.
