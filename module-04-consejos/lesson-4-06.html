★ Aconsejar de verdad — el párrafo de un buen consejo
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Advice that only tells someone what to do is the easy half. The other half is why they should listen.
Last full practice day. You now have affirmative tú commands, negative tú commands, and pronouns in both positions. Today you hold them together across a connected paragraph — the Honors standard, and exactly what tomorrow's Lengua en acción asks for.

Advice has a shape, and it is not a list of instructions. The shape is what makes the difference between advice a friend takes and advice a friend nods at.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to write 6–8 connected sentences of advice using both tú command forms.
2
I will be able to place every pronoun and every tilde correctly in either form.
3
I will be able to name the five moves of a piece of advice and say which one people skip.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Why good advice usually starts by listening

Look again at Módulo 4's very first reading, back on Day 1: the friend awake at midnight asked one question before saying anything else — «¿Cuántas veces has practicado?». Then they gave advice built on the answer.

That is the move almost everyone skips. Advice given before you know the situation is a guess, and it usually sounds like one.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Think of advice you were given that you actually took. What made it land?
🧠 PART 2 · LOS CINCO MOVIMIENTOS 10 min

A shape for advice, and the form each move wants

SentenceMoveFormOpener
1Ask, or acknowledgeafirmativo + pronounCuéntame… / Escúchame…
2–3What to doafirmativoHabla con… / Duerme… / Pide…
4What not to donegativoNo le escribas… / No te compares…
5–6The reasonpresent or pastporque… / a mí me pasó…
7Reassurenegativo or afirmativoNo te preocupes… / Ten paciencia…
8Stay availableafirmativo + pronounLlámame… / Escríbeme…

Move 5–6 is the one people skip, and it is where your other modules earn their keep: the reason is usually a past experience, so it wants the tenses from Módulos 0 to 2. «A mí me pasó lo mismo y no dormí en dos semanas» does more work than any command.

⭐ LA REGLA DE LOS CONECTORES
No connector twice. For advice you have: primero · además · pero · porque · sin embargo · en serio · al final · y otra cosa. Eight, and eight sentences need at most five.
🧠 PART 3 · MODELO ANOTADO 10 min

A una amiga que quiere dejar el equipo

1 Cuéntame otra vez qué pasó en el entrenamiento, porque ayer estabas muy alterada. 2 Habla con la entrenadora antes de decidir nada. 3 Pídele una reunión de diez minutos, no un correo largo. 4 No dejes el equipo esta semana, y no le escribas nada al grupo todavía. 5 A mí me pasó algo parecido en segundo año: quise irme en octubre y me arrepentí en enero. 6 Lo que había cambiado no era el equipo, era yo, y eso se arregló solo. 7 No te compares con las que llevan cinco años; ten paciencia contigo. 8 Llámame el jueves y me cuentas cómo fue.

Count the forms: five affirmative commands (cuéntame, habla, pídele, ten, llámame), four negative (no dejes, no le escribas, no te compares), pronouns attached in the affirmatives with tildes where needed, and in front in the negatives without. Sentences 5 and 6 carry the whole persuasive weight and contain no commands at all — preterite and past perfect from Módulos 0 and 2. Five connectors, none repeated.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · what is missing?
«Habla con la entrenadora. No dejes el equipo. No te compares con nadie. Ten paciencia.» What has been left out?
✅ TRY IT · pronoun position, affirmative
«___ una reunión corta.» (pedir + le, affirmative tú)
✅ TRY IT · pronoun position, negative
«___ nada al grupo todavía.» (escribir + le, negative tú)

Ahora tú — tomorrow's graded task, rehearsed. Write 6–8 sentences of advice to a friend facing a real decision, following Part 2's shape. Keep it; you will revise rather than restart.

Before you call it finished:
• Every command a tú form — this is a friend, not a stranger.
• At least two negative commands, built from the Ud. form + -s.
• At least one pronoun attached to an affirmative, with its tilde: cuéntame, pídele, llámame.
• At least one pronoun in front of a negative, without a tilde: no le escribas, no te compares.
• Sentences 5–6 give a reason, ideally from your own experience — and therefore in a past tense.
• No affirmative irregular used as a negative: no hagas, never «no haz».
• Four or more different connectors, none repeated.
• At least three words from this module's twenty.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

El colmado de la esquina

En muchos barrios dominicanos, el colmado no es solo una tienda pequeña. Es donde se compra el arroz, se paga la luz, se pide un fiado a fin de mes y se pregunta por la gente. El dueño conoce a las familias de tres calles a la redonda.

Mi tía dice que en el colmado se dan más consejos que en cualquier oficina. Alguien llega alterado por un problema, y siempre hay dos o tres personas apoyadas en el mostrador dispuestas a opinar. «No hagas eso ahora». «Habla con él primero». «A mí me pasó igual».

No siempre es buen consejo, pero rara vez le falta lo que sí importa: alguien escuchó, y alguien contó algo propio. Mi tía se ríe y dice que la mitad del país se ha arreglado la vida apoyado en el mostrador de un colmado.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find the three quoted pieces of advice and match each to a move from Part 2: one negative command, one affirmative, and one reason drawn from experience. Between them they cover the shape in three short lines.

Then notice apoyadas and dispuestas — participles working as adjectives, agreeing with the nouns they describe. Módulo 2's haber test, still working. And se ha arreglado in the last line is Módulo 1's present perfect.
🌎 CULTURA · REPÚBLICA DOMINICANA
El colmado

The colmado is a genuinely central Dominican institution and has no clean English equivalent. It is a small neighbourhood shop that also functions as a message board, a credit line, a bar and a place to stand around. The practice of buying al fiado — on informal credit, written in a notebook, settled at the end of the month — still operates on the shopkeeper's judgement of who is good for it.

Why that matters for this module: advice in the Dominican Republic is frequently public and unsolicited, and that is not considered intrusive. Explaining your problem in front of three strangers who then all weigh in is normal. US students often find this the sharpest cultural difference in the module — more than any grammar point.

And notice what the reading says makes it work. Not that the advice is good, but that «alguien escuchó, y alguien contó algo propio» — someone listened, and someone told you something of their own. That is moves 1 and 5 of Part 2, arrived at independently by people leaning on a counter.

★ LA VIRTUD · EL SERVICIO

The model in Part 3 does something that costs the writer more than any of its commands: it admits a mistake. «Quise irme en octubre y me arrepentí en enero» — the advice works because the person giving it was willing to look worse in order to be useful. El servicio quite often means spending your own dignity rather than your time. Servir a veces cuesta contar en qué te equivocaste.
