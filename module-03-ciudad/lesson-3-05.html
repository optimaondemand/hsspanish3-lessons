Lesson 3.05
★ No lo tome — cuando el pronombre se mueve
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
No new verb form today. Only one thing changes — and it changes completely.
To tell somebody not to do something, you put no in front of the command you already built on Day 3. That is the whole form. Tome → no tome. Nothing else to learn.

But the moment a pronoun is involved, everything moves. Yesterday's tómelo becomes no lo tome — the pronoun comes off the end, moves in front, becomes its own word, and the tilde disappears.

This pattern is worth real attention: it is exactly what Módulo 4 does with tú, and the forms themselves return in Módulo 7 as the subjunctive.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to form the negative Ud. and Uds. command of any verb.
2
I will be able to move pronouns in front of a negative command, in the right order, without a tilde.
3
I will be able to give a warning politely, and explain what softens a negative command.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Watch the pronoun jump

Tómelo  →  No lo tome
Sígame  →  No me siga
Siéntese  →  No se siente

Three things happened each time. The pronoun detached. It moved in front. And the tilde vanished, because the verb went back to being a short word that follows the default stress.

The verb form itself never changed. Tome is tome in both.

⚠️ TRES COSAS QUE CAMBIAN, UNA QUE NO
Change: the pronoun detaches · it moves in front of the verb · the attachment tilde disappears
Does not change: the order of two pronouns — indirect still comes before direct

démelo → no me lo dé  ·  never «no lo me dé»

And the whole sequence is fixed: no + pronoun(s) + verb. Four slots, one order.
✏️ CUIDADO: HAY DOS CLASES DE TILDE
Look carefully at that line above: démelo lost its tilde — but no me lo dé still has one. Those are two different tildes doing two different jobs, and confusing them is the commonest error in this lesson.

1. La tilde de posición — appears only because a pronoun was attached and the word got longer. It disappears when the pronoun leaves:
tómelo → no lo tome  ·  sígame → no me siga

2. La tilde diacrítica — belongs to the word itself, to keep it apart from a look-alike. It never disappears:
dé (give) vs de (of)  ·  esté (be) vs este (this)
So: no dé papaya  ·  no esté nervioso — tildes stay.

The test: ask whether the tilde was there before any pronoun arrived. Tome never had one, so it has none in the negative. Dé always had one, so it keeps it. Same family as tú/tu, mí/mi, sé/se from Módulo 4.

You have met this shape before. In Módulo 1 the order for the perfect was no + pronoun + haber + participio — no me he sentido bien. Spanish is consistent about this: negation first, then pronouns, then the verb. One habit covers both.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Consejos del recepcionista, segunda parte

—Una última cosa antes de salir. No lleve el pasaporte encima; déjelo en la caja fuerte del cuarto. No saque el teléfono en la calle para mirar el mapa; métase en una tienda y mírelo ahí.
—¿Es peligroso el centro?
—No, para nada. Pero no dé papaya, como decimos aquí.
—¿Papaya?
—Es una expresión nuestra. Significa: no le facilite las cosas a alguien con malas intenciones. No deje el bolso en el respaldo de la silla. No cuente el dinero en la puerta del banco. Cosas de sentido común.
—Entendido.
—Y no se preocupe demasiado. Bogotá es una ciudad tranquila si uno pone atención. Disfrútela.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Count the negative commands — there are eight — and notice that none of them carries a tilde, while the two affirmatives (déjelo, disfrútela) both do. That contrast is the lesson, in one conversation.

Then find the two with pronouns in front: no le facilite and no se preocupe. Compare them with métase, where the same kind of pronoun is attached because the command is affirmative.
🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
«No dé papaya»

This is the single most Colombian expression you will learn in this course, and it is — conveniently — a negative command. No dar papaya means roughly: do not leave yourself an easy target. Do not put temptation in front of people.

Colombians use it constantly and about far more than crime: leaving your laptop visible in a car is dar papaya, and so is handing your opponent an easy argument. The image is a ripe papaya left out where anyone can take it.

Note what it is not. It is not a warning that Colombia is dangerous, and Colombians can be sharp about that assumption. It is closer to a shared piece of practical common sense — and it puts the responsibility on your own attention rather than on other people's character. Which is why the receptionist says it in the same breath as «no, para nada» and «disfrútela».

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA

A negative command is the least courteous thing in a grammar book — and notice how the receptionist makes eight of them land warmly. He explains the reason, admits the place is safe, teaches an expression rather than lecturing, and finishes with disfrútela. La cortesía is rarely about avoiding the direct thing; it is about what you put around it. No es el mandato lo que ofende, sino el mandato solo.
