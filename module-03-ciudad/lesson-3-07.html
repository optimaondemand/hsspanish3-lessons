🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
«A la orden»

One more Colombian phrase before the graded work, and you will hear it constantly: «a la orden». Shopkeepers say it as you walk in, meaning roughly “at your service — what can I do for you?” They also say it as you leave, meaning “you're welcome.” And a street vendor will call it out to nobody in particular.

It has no single English equivalent, which is why it is worth knowing rather than translating. Together with con mucho gusto for de nada, it is part of a register of everyday courtesy that visitors to Colombia notice immediately and often comment on.

If somebody says «a la orden» to you, the safe answers are «gracias» or simply telling them what you need.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA

Revising yesterday's paragraph rather than writing a new one is the courteous choice toward your reader, oddly enough: a revised set of instructions is one somebody can actually follow, and a fresh draft written at speed usually is not. La cortesía and care are frequently the same act. Corregir es pensar otra vez en quien va a leerte.
