Lesson 3.04
★ Tómelo, sígame — cuando el mandato lleva pronombre
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The pronoun sticks to the end — and that is where the written accent comes from.
Yesterday you built the command. Today you attach things to it: tómelo, sígame, démelo.

This is where a lot of students start writing Spanish that looks almost right. The rule itself is simple — pronouns attach to the end of an affirmative command — but attaching them moves the word's shape, and Spanish then requires a tilde to keep the stress where it was. That tilde is not decoration; it is the only thing holding the pronunciation in place.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to attach one or two pronouns to an affirmative Ud. command in the right order.
2
I will be able to place the written accent on tómelo, sígame, démelo and say why it is required.
3
I will be able to give a full set of directions in Ud. commands, with pronouns where they belong.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

One word you already know by heart

¿Dígame?

That is how a shop assistant in Spain answers the phone or turns to serve you. It is diga (the Ud. command of decir) with me stuck on the end — literally “tell me” — and it carries a written accent that diga alone does not.

The accent appeared the moment the pronoun did. Today's lesson is essentially that one observation, made systematic.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Say diga and then dígame aloud. Where does the stress fall in each?
🧠 PART 2 · SE PEGAN AL FINAL 11 min

Attached, and written as one word

Click the 🔊, then read the table aloud. With an affirmative command the pronoun is not a separate word — it becomes part of the verb.

Order when there are two: indirect before direct — me then lo. You already know this from the double-object pronouns in Spanish 2; nothing has changed except that they are now glued to the end of a command.

⭐ UNA NOTA SOBRE SE
Reflexive verbs work the same way: siéntese (sit down), tranquilícese (calm down), póngase el cinturón (put on your seatbelt). The reflexive pronoun for usted is se, so it lands on the end just like the others — and brings a tilde with it.

You met siéntese in Módulo 4's register practice; now you can build it yourself.
✏️ PART 3 · DE DÓNDE SALE LA TILDE 10 min

The accent is doing arithmetic, not decoration

Spanish has a default: a word ending in a vowel is stressed on the second-to-last syllable. To-me follows the default, so it needs no tilde.

Now attach a pronoun. Tomelo would have three syllables — and the default would put the stress on me, giving to-ME-lo. That is not the word. So Spanish writes the tilde to hold the stress where it belongs:

WordSyllablesStress falls onTilde?
tometo-meTO (the default)No
tómelotó-me-loTO (against the default)Yes
digadi-gaDI (the default)No
dígamedí-ga-meDI (against the default)Yes
démelodé-me-loDE (against the default)Yes

The working rule: attach a pronoun to a command and you almost always need a tilde. If the result is three syllables or more with the stress still on the original one, write it.

This is the same principle you met with leído in Módulo 1 and había in Módulo 2: the tilde is written pronunciation. Here it is doing the most visible version of that job in the whole language.

💡 UNA EXCEPCIÓN ÚTIL
One-syllable commands that gain only one pronoun often need no tilde, because the result already follows the default: dé + me = deme (two syllables, stress on the first — the default, so no tilde needed; you will also see déme, and both are accepted).

But add a second pronoun and the tilde becomes obligatory: dé + me + lo = démelo. Three syllables, stress on the first, against the default.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

En la ventanilla de TransMilenio

—Buenos días. Necesito ir al Museo del Oro.
—Dígame una cosa: ¿tiene tarjeta?
—No, es mi primer día en Bogotá.
—Entonces cómprela aquí; son dos mil pesos. Cárguela con lo que necesita para hoy. Guárdela bien, porque sin tarjeta no se puede entrar.
—¿Y qué ruta tomo?
—Tome la ruta que va al centro y bájese en la estación Museo del Oro. Son ocho paradas. Siéntese si encuentra sitio, que a esta hora va llenísimo.
—Perfecto. Muchas gracias.
—Con mucho gusto. Cuídese.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Eight commands. Find the ones with a pronoun attached (dígame, cómprela, cárguela, guárdela, bájese, siéntese, cuídese) and the one without (tome) — and notice that every single attached one carries a tilde while the bare one does not. That is the rule from Part 3, visible in eight consecutive lines.

Then notice cárguela: the verb is cargar, so the Ud. command needed g→gu before the e, and then the pronoun and the tilde. Two rules stacked in one word.

And the closing «cuídese» — literally “take care of yourself” — is a standard Colombian farewell. It is a command, and it is affectionate.
🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
El TransMilenio

The TransMilenio is Bogotá's answer to not having a metro: articulated buses running in dedicated lanes with raised stations you enter through turnstiles — a subway system that happens to be on wheels. It opened in 2000, carries millions of passengers a week, and has been copied by cities across Latin America and beyond.

It is also, by common Bogotano agreement, extremely crowded. The word you will hear is trancón for a traffic jam — Colombian rather than pan-Hispanic, where other countries say atasco or embotellamiento.

Note what the ticket seller does with language: seven commands in a row, all with usted, and she closes with cuídese. Being addressed in a stream of imperatives by a stranger is not brusque here — it is service.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA

Look at what the ticket seller told the visitor that nobody asked: that the card must be kept safe, that the bus is full at this hour, that he should sit if he can. She had a queue behind her and she spent three extra sentences anyway. La cortesía is usually measured in sentences somebody did not owe you. La cortesía se cuenta en frases que nadie te pidió.
