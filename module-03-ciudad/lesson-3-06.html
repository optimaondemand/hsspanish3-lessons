📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A set of instructions is not a list of commands. It has an order, and it tells you what to do when it goes wrong.
Last full practice day. You now have affirmative commands, negative commands, and pronouns in both positions. Today you hold them together across a connected paragraph — the Honors standard, and exactly what tomorrow's Lengua en acción asks for.

The discipline is the same as in every module: choose the shape first, then fill it. Instructions have a shape, and most people's fail because they skip part of it rather than because a verb was wrong.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to write 6–8 connected sentences of instructions using both affirmative and negative commands.
2
I will be able to place every pronoun correctly and every tilde where it belongs.
3
I will be able to explain why Cartagena's old town needs different instructions from Bogotá.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Where instructions go wrong

Not in the verbs. In two things: all affirmative and no warnings, or all warnings and no route. The first leaves someone confidently walking into a problem; the second leaves them anxious and still lost.

Good instructions alternate. Do this, then this, not that, and here is what you will see.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which do you think you would over-use if you wrote instructions in Spanish right now?
🧠 PART 2 · LA ARQUITECTURA 10 min

A shape you can reuse for any set of instructions

SentenceJobFormOpener
1Where to startafirmativoSalga de… / Tome…
2–3The routeafirmativoSiga… / Doble… / Cruce…
4The warningnegativoNo cruce… / No lo tome…
5The landmarkpresentQueda al lado de… / Se ve desde…
6–7What to do on arrivalafirmativo + pronounPídalo… / Guárdela…
8The fallbackafirmativo or negativoSi se pierde… / No se preocupe…

Sentence 4 is the one to put in deliberately. A single negative command in the middle of a route is what distinguishes instructions written by someone who has actually walked it. It is also the sentence the rubric looks for.

⭐ LA REGLA DE LOS CONECTORES · carried from Módulo 0
No connector twice. For instructions you have: primero · luego · después · en la esquina · al llegar · más adelante · al final · si. Eight, and eight sentences need at most five.
🧠 PART 3 · MODELO ANOTADO 10 min

Del hotel al Castillo de San Felipe

1 Salga del hotel y doble a la izquierda. 2 Siga derecho por la muralla unas seis cuadras. 3 Al llegar a la plaza grande, cruce hacia el puente. 4 No cruce por el otro lado: no hay acera y los carros pasan rápido. 5 El castillo se ve desde el puente; queda enfrente, en la colina. 6 Compre la entrada abajo y guárdela, porque la piden otra vez arriba. 7 Póngase sombrero y lleve agua; a mediodía el sol pega muy fuerte. 8 Si se pierde, no se preocupe: pregunte por «el castillo» y cualquiera le indica.

Count what the model does: nine affirmative commands, two negative, two pronouns attached (guárdela, póngase — both with tildes), one pronoun in front (no se preocupe — no tilde). Five different connectors, none repeated. And two of the eight sentences exist purely to help: the warning and the fallback.

🧠 PART 4 · PRACTÍCALO 10 min
✅ TRY IT · what is missing?
«Salga del hotel. Doble a la derecha. Siga cuatro cuadras. Cruce la avenida. El museo queda ahí.» What has been left out?
✅ TRY IT · the tilde in context
«Compre la entrada y ___ .» (guardar + la)
✅ TRY IT · the warning slot
«___ por el otro lado; no hay acera.» (cruzar — negative, usted)

Ahora tú — tomorrow's graded task, rehearsed. Write 6–8 sentences of instructions taking a visitor from your school to somewhere worth going, following Part 2's shape. Keep it; you will revise rather than restart.

Before you call it finished:
• Every command an Ud. form — this is a visitor, not a friend.
• At least one negative command, in the warning slot.
• At least one pronoun attached to an affirmative, with its tilde: guárdela, tómelo, póngase.
• At least one pronoun in front of a negative, without a tilde: no lo tome, no se preocupe.
• Spelling shifts checked: cruce, busque, llegue.
• Any inherently accented form keeps its tilde: no dé, no esté.
• A landmark somebody could actually see.
• Four or more different connectors, none repeated.
📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Por qué Cartagena se explica distinto

El centro histórico de Cartagena está rodeado por una muralla de piedra construida hace más de cuatrocientos años. Dentro, las calles son estrechas y no siguen una cuadrícula: se curvan, cambian de nombre en una esquina y muchas terminan en una plaza pequeña.

Por eso allí nadie da instrucciones con números. Un cartagenero le dice: «Camine hasta la torre del reloj y doble a la derecha en la casa amarilla». Las referencias son visuales — una iglesia, un balcón, un color, el mar — porque son lo único estable.

En Bogotá pasa lo contrario: la ciudad es una cuadrícula numerada y las distancias se calculan. Dos ciudades del mismo país, dos maneras de explicar el mismo camino. La lección práctica es sencilla: antes de dar instrucciones, mire qué clase de ciudad tiene delante.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Find the two commands inside the quoted Cartagena directions (camine, doble) and the one addressed to you at the end (mire). Then compare the paragraph with 3.01's Bogotá address system: the same country, and the useful answer is different in each.

Notice also rodeado and construida — participles working as adjectives, agreeing with what they describe. That is Módulo 2's haber test, still doing its job three modules later.
🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
La ciudad amurallada

Cartagena de Indias was founded in 1533 and became the most heavily fortified port in the Spanish Americas — the walls and the Castillo de San Felipe de Barajas exist because the city was repeatedly attacked by rival European powers and privateers. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site, and the old town is still lived in rather than preserved as a museum.

Two practical things follow. The narrow streets were built for shade and defence, not for traffic, which is why nobody navigates them by block count. And the heat is genuinely serious — Cartagena sits at sea level on the Caribbean, about 30 °C most of the year, while Bogotá is cool and grey at 2,600 metres. Colombians talk about tierra caliente and tierra fría as different worlds, and they are right.

Which is why the model instructions in Part 3 tell the visitor to take a hat. Local instructions carry local knowledge.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA

Look at which sentences in the Part 3 model were not strictly necessary: the warning about the missing pavement, the hat, the water, and the reassurance at the end. Four out of eight. A person who only answered the question asked would have written four sentences and been perfectly correct. La cortesía lives in the other half. Lo indispensable cabe en cuatro frases; la cortesía ocupa las otras cuatro.
