★ Tarea oral — consejos para alguien que acaba de llegar
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Commands are short. That makes them easy to say and easy to swallow.
Today you record the Tarea oral (20 pts) — 20% of your semester grade. The content is chosen: yesterday's reading asked you to pick what you would tell somebody arriving in your city or school for the first time.

The specific challenge is that Ud. commands are one or two syllables. Siga. Tome. Doble. Said quickly they blur into each other, and a listener loses the route. Today's pronunciation work is about landing them.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to speak for 60–90 seconds giving practical advice in Ud. commands.
2
I will be able to pronounce an attached pronoun so the stressed syllable is audible.
3
I will be able to include at least one negative command without sounding harsh.
⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 5 min

Six words, not a script

Same method as every module: a six-word outline, not a written-out text. A read script always sounds read, and with commands it is worse — a list of instructions recited from paper loses exactly the quality that makes advice useful, which is sounding like somebody who has walked the route.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Say «guárdela» aloud. Which syllable did you land on?
🎤 PART 2 · LA TAREA 9 min

El tema: ¿Qué le diría usted a alguien que llega por primera vez a su ciudad o a su escuela?

Practical advice, not a tour. Where to go, how to get around, what to avoid, what to do instead. The best version is what you would genuinely say to a new student on their first morning.

⭐ ENSAYA TRES VECES
Say the whole thing aloud three times from your six words. On run three, listen specifically for two things: the stressed syllable in any attached pronoun, and whether your negative command sounded like advice rather than a rule.
🔊 PART 4 · PRONUNCIACIÓN DE LOS MANDATOS 11 min

Click each and repeat aloud.

  • tome, coma, escriba…short — land the vowel
  • dé, esté, vaya, sea, sepathe five irregulars
  • tómelo, sígame, démelostress on the first syllable
  • no lo tome, no me digathree separate words

1. Attached pronouns keep the stress at the front. TÓ-me-lo, SÍ-ga-me, GUÁR-de-la. English speakers drift the stress to the middle, which makes the word unrecognisable. Exaggerate the first syllable more than feels natural.

2. Negative commands are three words, not one. No lo tome has small gaps in it. Running them together as “nolotome” is the audible equivalent of writing them joined up.

3. Short commands need a landing. Siga and tome are two syllables and disappear if rushed. Give each one a small pause after it — that is what makes a spoken route followable.

✅ TRY IT · what the rubric weighs

⏺ PART 5 · GRABA Y ENTREGA 6 min

Record in Canvas on the assignment. Listen once before submitting and check three things:

  1. Is there a negative command in there?
  2. Did your attached pronoun land on the first syllable?
  3. Did you reach 60 seconds? Short takes are usually missing beats 5 and 6.

If all three pass, submit.

🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
Los mandatos suenan amables

A register point that matters for your recording. English speakers instinctively soften instructions into questions — could you maybe…? — and then feel that a bare Spanish command sounds bossy. It does not.

You have seen this three times in this module: the hotel receptionist, the TransMilenio ticket seller, and the guide sentence in 3.08. All of them used strings of plain imperatives, and all of them were being helpful. In Colombia a stream of commands from a stranger reads as service, not authority.

What softens a command in Spanish is not a question form. It is por favor, a reason attached («vaya antes de las cinco, que cierran a las seis»), or a closing courtesy — con mucho gusto, a la orden, cuídese. Use those in your recording and the commands will land exactly right.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA

Rehearsing three times so that a stranger can follow you the first time is a courtesy nobody will ever see. The whole point of this recording is somebody else's convenience — and that is what separates instructions from a performance. Se ensaya por quien va a escuchar, no por quien habla.
