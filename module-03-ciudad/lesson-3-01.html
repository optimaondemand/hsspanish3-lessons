📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Before you can tell someone where to go, you need the words for what they will pass.
Módulo 3 turns the course outward. Modules 0 to 2 were about the past — narrating it, ordering it. From here the theme is opiniones y consejos, and it starts with the most practical version of telling someone what to do: directions.

Today: ten words for the parts of a city, and the verb Latin Americans actually use to ask where something is. No new grammar — that arrives on Day 3.

⏰ PART 1 · PARA EMPEZAR 6 min

The most useful question you do not yet know

In Spanish 1 you learned ¿Dónde está…? It is correct, and you will be understood everywhere. But across most of Latin America the question people actually ask about a building or a street is:

¿Dónde queda el museo?

Quedar is used for things that do not move — buildings, streets, neighbourhoods. Estar works for anything, including people and temporary positions. Using quedar for a museum is one of those small choices that makes you sound like someone who has been there.

✏️ ESCRIBIR CON TILDES Y LA Ñ · you will be graded on this
This module needs both marks: el semáforo takes a tilde, and la señal takes the ñ. In this course accents are graded, including in typed short answers — and senal is not a near miss, it is a different word.

The ñ is a separate letter of the Spanish alphabet, not an n with decoration. If you cannot type it quickly you will lose marks on a module full of directions: Cómo escribir las tildes y la ñ — Windows, Mac, phone and Chromebook.

One more from this module you will meet on Day 5: «dónde» asking a question versus «donde» simply pointing. ¿Dónde está la parada? — but la esquina donde está la parada.
⭐ PARA IR MÁS LEJOS · optional · +5 min
Two regional notes worth having. La cuadra is the Latin-American word for a city block; in Spain you would hear la manzana — which also means apple. And el barrio is neutral in Spanish: it simply means the part of town you live in, with none of the connotations the borrowed English word has picked up. A wealthy Bogotá neighbourhood is un barrio too.
🧠 PART 3 · DECIR DÓNDE ESTÁ ALGO 8 min

Locating without instructing

Today you are describing, not directing. These four frames do it, and none needs new grammar:

FrameUseExample
quedar + placeFixed locationsEl museo queda en la plaza.
estar a + distanceHow farEstá a tres cuadras de aquí.
al lado de / enfrente deRelative positionQueda al lado de la parada.
entre… y…Between two thingsQueda entre la avenida y el puente.

«Está a tres cuadras» is worth memorising whole — that little a is doing the work of “away.” Without it, «está tres cuadras» is not Spanish. And note that quedar also means to remain and to arrange to meet; context sorts it out without difficulty.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Cómo funciona una dirección en Bogotá

En Bogotá las direcciones no llevan nombres de calles, sino números. Las calles van de oriente a occidente y las carreras de sur a norte. Una dirección típica es así:

Calle 72 # 10–34

Eso significa: la Calle 72, cerca del cruce con la Carrera 10, en el número 34. Con esos tres datos, un taxista sabe exactamente adónde va, y tú también.

El sistema parece frío al principio, pero es enormemente práctico. Si tu hotel queda en la Calle 80 y el museo en la Calle 72, están a ocho cuadras. Con nombres de calles, esa resta no existe.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Work out roughly how far Calle 72 # 10–34 is from Calle 76 # 11–20. Then notice how the paragraph uses today's vocabulary: el cruce, la cuadra, and queda twice for buildings.
🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
Una ciudad que se puede calcular

This module's country is Colombia, and the numbered-grid address system is a genuinely useful thing to understand before you ever go. Bogotá sits at 2,600 metres in the Andes — high enough that visitors get short of breath walking uphill — and the mountains are always to the east, so you can orient yourself by looking for them.

The grid is not merely tidy. Bogotá has around eight million people, and the system means an address is also a distance calculation: Calle 80 to Calle 72 is eight blocks, no map required. Cities built on street names cannot do that.

You will use this on Day 8, when you read a real travel guide to Bogotá written in Spanish.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA

This module's virtue is la cortesía — courtesy. It belongs with directions for a reason that becomes obvious the moment you are lost in a city where you do not speak the language well: asking for help requires you to admit you need it, and giving help well requires patience with someone who does not understand the first time. Both are courtesy, and both are harder than they sound. Preguntar bien y contestar con paciencia son la misma virtud.
