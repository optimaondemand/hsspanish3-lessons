Lesson 3.03
★ Mandatos formales — la forma que volverá en el Módulo 7
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Learn this form properly today and the hardest thing in the course becomes easy in November.
Today you build the formal command — tome, siga, cruce — the form used by a doctor, a police officer, a travel guide, a sign in an airport. Anyone addressing you as usted.

It is worth knowing now, before you start, that this is not just a lesson about street directions. The forms you learn today are the exact same forms you will use for the subjunctive in Módulo 7 — the biggest single topic of the year. Nothing new will be added to them. Only the reason for using them changes.

So the students who own this cold in October find November straightforward, and the ones who half-learn it do not.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to build the Ud. and Uds. command of any regular verb using the yo-form method.
2
I will be able to spell cruce, siga and busque correctly and explain why the spelling shifts.
3
I will be able to give a stranger three-step directions entirely in Ud. commands.

Three steps, and it works for almost every verb

💡 LOS TRES PASOS
1. Take the yo form of the present tense — tomo, sigo, cruzo
2. Drop the -o — tom-, sig-, cruz-
3. Add the opposite vowel: -ar verbs take -e, -er/-ir verbs take -a

For ustedes, add an -n: tome → tomen.
Infinitiveyo formUd.Uds.

doblardoblodobledoblen
comercomocomacoman

tener (irregular yo)tengotengatengan
salir (irregular yo)salgosalgasalgan

Why the yo form and not the infinitive? Because it carries the irregularities with it for free. Seguir becomes sigo, so the command is siga — not «segua». Tener becomes tengo, so the command is tenga. Any verb whose yo form is odd hands that oddness straight to its command, and you do not have to learn a second list.

✏️ PART 3 · ORTOGRAFÍA 9 min

Three spelling shifts that protect the sound

You met these in Módulo 0 with busqué, llegué and empecé. Exactly the same logic applies here, because the vowel flipping to -e would otherwise change how the consonant sounds.

VerbShiftUd. commandWhy
cruzarz → ccruceSpanish does not write ze; it writes ce
buscarc → qubusqueKeeps the hard /k/ before e
llegarg → gullegueKeeps the hard /g/ before e

The verb has not become irregular. The sound stayed the same and the spelling moved to keep up — the same sentence you read in Módulo 0. Cruce la calle is pronounced with the same first syllable as cruzar.

And a set of genuine irregulars — five, worth memorising as a block. Click to hear them:

  • dé, esté, vaya, sea, sepadar, estar, ir, ser, saber

These five have no usable yo form to build from (doy, estoy, voy, soy, sé), which is exactly why they are irregular. Note dé carries an accent to separate it from the preposition de — the same tilde diacrítica job as tú/tu and él/el. And esté is accented too: este without it is the demonstrative “this”.

📖 PART 5 · A LEER 5 min

Instrucciones en la recepción del hotel

—Buenas tardes. ¿Cómo llego al Museo del Oro?
—Es muy fácil. Salga por la puerta principal y doble a la derecha. Siga derecho cuatro cuadras hasta la Avenida Jiménez. Cruce la avenida en el semáforo — no en la esquina, ahí no hay señal. Después camine media cuadra más y el museo queda al lado de la estación.
—¿Y si me pierdo?
—Pregunte en cualquier tienda; la gente aquí ayuda mucho. O tome un taxi, son baratos. Pero vaya antes de las cinco, que cierran a las seis.
—Muchas gracias.
—Con mucho gusto.

⭐ COMPRUEBA · optional
Eight Ud. commands. Sort them by how they were built: which come straight from a regular yo form (doble, camine, pregunte, tome), which carry an irregular yo (salga from salgo, siga from sigo), which needed a spelling shift (cruce), and which is one of the five irregulars (vaya)?

Notice also that the receptionist uses usted throughout with a guest — and closes with con mucho gusto rather than de nada. Keep that phrase; you will hear it constantly in Colombia.
🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
El usted que no marca distancia

Colombia does something with usted that will surprise you. In much of the country — especially Bogotá and the Andean interior — people use usted with family and close friends, not only with strangers. Parents say it to their children. Couples say it to each other.

So the neat rule you learned in Módulo 4's register lesson — usted = distance, tú = closeness — is a good default across the Spanish-speaking world and not a universal law. In Colombia usted can carry warmth, and there is a third form, vos, used in Medellín and the Andes region.

The practical takeaway: keep using usted with people you do not know, everywhere. But when a Colombian friend addresses you as usted, do not read it as coldness. It may be the opposite.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA

This module's virtue is la cortesía — courtesy. Notice what the receptionist actually does: gives clear directions, warns about the crossing, offers a fallback if the guest gets lost, and mentions the closing time unprompted. None of that was asked for. Courtesy is mostly anticipating the next question, and it costs one extra sentence. La cortesía es contestar también lo que no te preguntaron.
