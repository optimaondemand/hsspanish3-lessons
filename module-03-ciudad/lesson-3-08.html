📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
One real sentence, written for travellers to Bogotá, containing everything you learned this week.
Módulo 3's reading day. The text is a genuine Spanish-language travel guide — not adapted, not simplified — and it happens to be the tidiest example in the whole semester of why this course teaches what it teaches.

In a single sentence it uses an affirmative command with an irregular stem, a second affirmative command, and a negative command. That is Days 3, 4 and 5 of this module, written by someone who was not thinking about your grammar at all.

The graded Lectura interpretativa (15 pts) opens today.

⏰ PART 1 · ANTES DE LEER 6 min

Contexto y tres cosas que buscar

El contexto. This is from a collaboratively written travel guide, in the section on taking taxis in Bogotá. It is the kind of practical safety paragraph every guide to every large city contains — and, as you saw in 3.05, Colombians would recognise the underlying advice as no dar papaya.

Busca estas tres cosas. No dictionary on the first two passes:

  1. ¿Qué dos cosas recomienda hacer, y qué cosa recomienda no hacer?
  2. ¿Cuántos mandatos hay en la oración? ¿Cuáles son afirmativos y cuál es negativo?
  3. ¿Por qué ofrece una alternativa en vez de solo prohibir?
⭐ UNA ORACIÓN LARGA · a technique
This is one long sentence with a hinge in the middle: por tanto (“therefore”). Read the part before it as the reason, and the part after it as the advice. Splitting a long sentence at its connector is faster than translating it left to right.
📚 PART 2 · EL TEXTO 10 min
📖 LECTURA AUTÉNTICA · TEXTO CON LICENCIA ABIERTA
Tenga siempre en cuenta que los taxis pueden ser usados para realizar atracos a los pasajeros y otras variedades de robos, por tanto, intente siempre solicitarlos por teléfono y no tome taxis que se ofrezcan insistentemente a llevarlo.
Wikivoyage, «Bogotá», sección En taxi. Texto disponible bajo licencia Creative Commons Atribución-CompartirIgual 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Ayuda de vocabulario — only after your second reading: tener en cuenta = to bear in mind · el atraco = a mugging · el robo = a robbery · por tanto = therefore · solicitar = to request, to order · insistentemente = insistently · llevarlo = to take you (somewhere).

One form is not yours yet: «taxis que se ofrezcan». Read it as “taxis that offer” and move past it. It is a subjunctive, and you meet that mood properly in Módulo 7, with its remaining uses in Semester 2 of this course. Nothing on this page asks you to use it. Authentic texts contain grammar from all over the language, and reading around an unfamiliar form is a real skill — the same habit Módulo 0 asked you to build.

Notice what this proves. Nobody wrote that sentence to teach you the yo-form method — a volunteer wrote it to keep travellers safe in Bogotá. The method is simply how the language works, and you can now read it operating in the wild.

One more detail worth seeing: solicitarlos and llevarlo. Those are pronouns attached to infinitives, not commands — and infinitives take no tilde when a pronoun attaches, because the stress was already on the last syllable. Compare:

Attached to…ExampleTilde?
a commandtómelo, guárdelaYes
an infinitivesolicitarlos, llevarloNo

Same rule underneath, opposite result — because the two words start with their stress in different places. The tilde is always answering the same question: did the word's stress end up somewhere the default would not put it?

🎤 PART 5 · PUENTE A LA PRODUCCIÓN 6 min

The guide gives a reason, then advice, then an alternative. Tomorrow's Tarea oral asks you to do the same thing for somewhere you actually know, so choose now:

¿Qué le diría usted a alguien que llega por primera vez a su ciudad o a su escuela?

Not a tour — practical advice. Where to go, what to avoid, what to do instead. The most useful version of this task is the one you would genuinely say to a new student on their first morning.

🌎 CULTURA · COLOMBIA
Cómo leer una advertencia

Read that sentence's register carefully, because it is easy to over-read. It says taxis pueden ser usados for robberies — can be, not are. It gives a specific practical alternative. It does not tell you to avoid taxis, and it does not describe Bogotá as dangerous.

That precision matters to Colombians. The country spent decades in international coverage defined almost entirely by violence, and a great many Colombians are tired of visitors arriving frightened. Bogotá is a capital of eight million people where the ordinary advice is the ordinary advice of any large city — the same things a guide would tell you about Madrid, Chicago or Naples.

So the useful reading of this passage is the Colombian one from 3.05: no dé papaya. Pay attention, do not make yourself an easy target, and then enjoy the city. The guide's own next line, which you did not read, points out that traffic jams are the more likely problem.

★ LA VIRTUD · LA CORTESÍA

A volunteer wrote that sentence for strangers they will never meet, and took the trouble to include the alternative rather than only the prohibition. Warning someone without leaving them stranded takes one extra clause and a little thought about how the warning will land. La cortesía in writing is mostly that: imagining the reader. Advertir sin dejar a nadie sin salida — eso cuesta una frase más.
